# Data protection, cybersecurity, outsourcing, e-execution and record retention for Udofin (LSP/DLA serving NBFCs), as of 10 October 2026

**Executive summary.** On 10 October 2026 Udofin's data and security obligations come from five sources that are already in force, plus one large regime that is notified but mostly not yet in force:

- **RBI digital lending data rules.** The rules issued on 8 May 2025, which RBI reportedly moved on 28 November 2025 into Chapter III of the NBFC Credit Facilities Directions, 2025, require:
  - only need-based data, collected with explicit consent;
  - storage only on servers in India;
  - no more than "basic minimal data" stored by an LSP.
- **RBI outsourcing rules.** The NBFC "Managing Risks in Outsourcing" Directions, 2025 (RBI/DOR/2025-26/363, 28 November 2025) merge the 2017 financial-services outsourcing rules and the 2023 IT-outsourcing rules. They require the NBFC to obtain from Udofin: audit rights, RBI inspection access (including of Udofin's subcontractors), BCP/DR, confidentiality and isolation of each lender's data, and controls on sub-outsourcing.
- **CERT-In Directions of 28 April 2022.** These bind Udofin directly as a body corporate: report incidents within 6 hours, keep 180 days of logs within India, and synchronise clocks to NIC/NPL NTP.
- **IT Act 2000 and SPDI Rules 2011.** These remain the operative privacy baseline until the DPDP substantive provisions commence.
- **Contract, stamp and security-registration law.** This covers eSign, e-stamp and CERSAI.

The **Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025** were notified on 13/14 November 2025, but only the Data Protection Board machinery is live. The consent-manager registration rule follows at about 12 months (around 13/14 November 2026). Notice, consent, security, breach-notification, erasure and rights obligations commence at about 18 months (around 13/14 May 2027). MeitY consulted in January 2026 on shortening that window to 12 months; I could not confirm that this was notified. Udofin should build to DPDP standards now, because the product will launch into that regime and every partner NBFC will contractually require it. The owner's plan conflicts with these rules at several points:

- backend hosting on Railway, which to my knowledge has no India region;
- Udofin storing full KYC and bureau data as its own "marketplace" data;
- Udofin running Aadhaar OTP eKYC and CIBIL pulls in its own name;
- executing promissory notes and mortgages by eSign;
- SMS/expense scraping;
- an AI agent sending borrower data to offshore LLM APIs;
- sharing data with group company Equator without separate consent.

### 2. DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025

**2.1 Timeline.**

- Notified 13/14 November 2025. Sources differ on whether the operative date is the 13th or the 14th [confirmed].
- Immediately in force: Rules 1, 2 and 17–21, which set up the Data Protection Board.
- **Rule 4** (consent-manager registration) takes effect one year after publication, around **13/14 November 2026**, per most sources. SCC Online and some others describe it as "on a later date to be notified", so the date is not settled [confirmed].
- **Rules 3, 5–16, 22 and 23** take effect at 18 months, around **13/14 May 2027** [confirmed]. These cover notice, processing by the State, security safeguards, breach intimation, erasure, DPO contact, children, SDF duties, rights, cross-border transfer, research exemptions and calls for information.
- In January 2026 MeitY consulted on **cutting the 18-month window to 12 months**, which would bring the final date to around 13 November 2026, and on enforcing SDF cross-border restrictions immediately. Comments were due 4 February 2026 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/technology/tech-news/meity-may-cut-compliance-timeline-for-key-dpdp-rules-to-12-months-126012201293_1.html), 22 Jan 2026; [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1773554/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs)). Sources disagree on whether the cut covers everyone or only SDFs.
- **I found no gazette notification adopting the cut** [unverified]. If it were adopted, substantive obligations would apply about **one month from today**. Treat November 2026 as the planning date and May 2027 as the legal backstop.

**2.2 Duties when live** [likely; based on the Act and Rules text, not re-fetched]:

- **Grounds (s.4, s.6, s.7).** Processing needs consent that is free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, given by clear affirmative action and limited to the specified purpose, or a "legitimate use". Withdrawal must be as easy as giving consent. Under s.6(10) the fiduciary bears the burden of proving notice and consent.
  - _Design consequence:_ loan consent cannot be bundled with marketing, "books of accounts" analytics, or referral to Equator.
- **Notice (Rule 3).** It must be understandable on its own and separate from other information. It must give an itemised list of the personal data and the specific purpose. It must link to withdrawing consent, exercising rights, and complaining to the Board.
- **Security (s.8(5), Rule 6).** "Reasonable security safeguards" must at minimum include:
  - encryption, obfuscation, masking or virtual tokens;
  - access controls;
  - visibility of access through logs, monitoring and review;
  - backups and measures for continued processing;
  - **logs and personal data kept for one year** for detection and investigation, unless law requires otherwise;
  - safeguards written into contracts with processors.
- **Breach (s.8(6), Rule 7).**
  - Each affected data principal must be told "without delay": nature, extent, timing and location of the breach, likely consequences, mitigation, protective steps they can take, and a contact.
  - The Board must be told without delay, followed by a detailed report **within 72 hours** (or longer if the Board allows). The report covers facts, mitigation, findings about who caused the breach, remedial measures, and the notices sent to data principals.
- **Erasure (s.8(7)–(8), Rule 8).** Erase when consent is withdrawn or the purpose is served, **unless retention is required by law**, and make processors erase too. The Third Schedule's fixed inactivity periods cover only named classes (large e-commerce, gaming and social-media platforms), not lenders. Rule 8 also sets a **one-year minimum** retention of personal data, traffic data and logs for specified state purposes.
- **Grievance (s.8(10), s.13, Rule 14).** Publish how to exercise rights and respond within **at most 90 days**. RBI's grievance timelines are shorter (the RE has 30 days before the borrower can go to the RBI Ombudsman), so they govern in practice.
- **Children (s.9).** Lending to minors is not viable, since a minor's contract is void under the Contract Act. Udofin should verify age 18+ at onboarding so that the s.9 verifiable parental-consent regime is never triggered [recommendation].
- **Penalties (Schedule).** Up to ₹250 crore for failing to take reasonable security safeguards, and up to ₹200 crore for failing to notify a breach [likely].
- **Collections exemption, s.17(1)(f).** Most of Chapters II–III and s.16 do not apply to processing needed to ascertain the financial information, assets and liabilities of a person who has defaulted on a loan from a financial institution, provided it follows disclosure rules in other laws [likely].
  - The exemption is narrow. It does **not** lift the duty in s.8(5) to keep data secure.
  - It does not authorise using contact lists or harassment, which RBI rules separately prohibit [interpretation].

**2.3 Fiduciary or processor: Udofin's role.** The Act defines a fiduciary as whoever "alone or in conjunction with other persons determines the purpose and means" (s.2(i)) and a processor as one who processes "on behalf of" a fiduciary (s.2(k)). The DL Directions likewise define an LSP as acting _on behalf of_ the RE. My reading **[interpretation]** is that Udofin is a hybrid:

| Activity                                                                   | Who sets purpose and means          | Udofin's role                                                                                             |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| App account, login, device data, offer comparison, comparison analytics    | Udofin                              | **Data fiduciary**                                                                                        |
| Collecting the application, KYC and underwriting inputs **for a named RE** | That RE (credit policy, KYC rules)  | **Processor** for that RE. This must sit under a s.8(2) contract (DPA)                                    |
| Sending one applicant's data to several REs for offers                     | The borrower's choice, then each RE | Udofin as fiduciary **discloses** to each RE (a separate fiduciary) only on explicit, RE-specific consent |
| Collections on the RE's behalf (FLDG-backed)                               | RE (recovery policy)                | Processor/recovery agent. Udofin's own FLDG interest raises a risk of being treated as a joint fiduciary  |
| "Books of accounts"/expense view, marketing, cross-sell                    | Udofin                              | **Fiduciary**. Must not flow to REs without separate consent                                              |
| Referral of a grievance to Equator                                         | Udofin/Equator                      | A disclosure to a third party, so it needs specific consent (§10, Challenge 10)                           |

As a result, Udofin needs (a) its own DPDP programme (notice, consent ledger, rights handling, security, breach response) and (b) a DPA with every RE in which the RE is the fiduciary for loan data. That DPA must include RBI-mandated audit and inspection terms, purge-on-instruction, and a ban on reusing one RE's loan data for any other RE.

**2.4 Significant Data Fiduciary (s.10, Rule 13).** No class of SDF had been notified as of my searches [unverified]. If notified, SDF duties are:

- a DPO based in India;
- an independent data auditor;
- an annual DPIA and audit;
- due diligence on algorithmic software;
- localisation of any data categories the government specifies. The January 2026 proposal would bring the SDF cross-border restrictions in sooner.

A multi-lender platform holding financial data at scale is a plausible future SDF, so Udofin should keep a DPIA ready [recommendation].

**2.5 Cross-border transfer (s.16, Rule 15).** The Act uses a negative list: transfers are allowed except to countries the government notifies. **However, s.16(2) keeps stricter sectoral rules in force.** For Udofin that stricter rule is RBI's requirement that digital-lending data be stored on servers in India (§3). DPDP therefore does not relax anything for Udofin [likely].

**2.6 Consent managers (s.6(7)–(9), Rule 4, First Schedule).** Consent managers must be registered with the Board, incorporated in India, and have net worth of at least ₹2 crore. They must run an interoperable platform, avoid conflicts of interest, and keep consent records for **7 years** [confirmed, secondary: Mondaq/Anantam]. Using a consent manager is optional for Udofin. For financial-data sharing, RBI's Account Aggregator (NBFC-AA) system is the relevant consent layer. Under the AA framework a Financial Information User must be regulated by a financial-sector regulator, so an unregulated LSP must use AA through an RE that is the FIU [likely].

**2.7 Before DPDP commences.** IT Act s.43A and the SPDI Rules 2011 still apply [likely]:

- a privacy policy (Rule 4);
- **written consent** for "sensitive personal data", which includes financial information such as bank, card and payment details (Rule 5);
- conditions on disclosure and transfer (Rules 6–7);
- "reasonable security practices", for which ISO/IEC 27001 is deemed compliant, with **an independent audit at least once a year** or after a significant upgrade (Rule 8).

### 3. RBI digital lending data and technology rules (now NBFC Credit Facilities Directions 2025, Chapter III)

The following data and technology obligations come from the DL Directions 2025, carrying forward the September 2022 guidelines. Each is something the RE must ensure the LSP/DLA does [confirmed, secondary: [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/fin-tech/1634560/harmonising-rbis-digital-lending-directions-with-indian-data-protection-and-it-laws); [Argus](https://www.argus-p.com/updates/updates/rbi-digital-lending-directions-2025-an-overview/); [Legal500/Juris Corp](https://www.legal500.com/developments/thought-leadership/the-rbis-digital-lending-directions-2025-a-unified-code-for-a-fragmented-sector/); [Vinod Kothari](https://vinodkothari.com/2025/05/digital-lending-directions-largely-a-consolidation-new-rules-on-multi-lender-platforms-and-lending-apps/)]. I could not verify paragraph numbers in the consolidated Chapter III.

- **Collect only what is needed, with prior explicit consent and an audit trail.** The borrower must be able to accept or refuse specific uses, restrict disclosure to third parties, revoke consent, and have collected data deleted [confirmed].
- **Device access.** Apps must not access files and media, contact lists, call logs or telephony functions. Camera, microphone and location may be accessed **one time**, with explicit consent, only where needed for onboarding/KYC [likely; carried forward from the 2022 guidelines, not re-verified].
- **The LSP must not store personal data other than "basic minimal data"** (for example name, address and contact details) needed for its operations [confirmed]. **No biometric storage** unless the law allows it [confirmed].
- **Servers in India.** All data must be stored only on servers located in India. Data processed abroad must be deleted there and brought back to India **within 24 hours** [confirmed].
- **Public policies.** A comprehensive privacy policy, naming the third parties that collect data through the DLA, must be published by both RE and LSP [confirmed]. A data-storage policy must also be published, covering type of data, retention period, restrictions on use, destruction protocol and breach-handling standards [likely].
- **Security standards.** RE and LSP must meet RBI and other agencies' technology and cybersecurity standards, and the RE remains accountable for the LSP [confirmed].
- **Grievance and multi-lender display.** Nodal grievance officers must be named on the DLA. The multi-lender rule (from 1 Nov 2025) requires an unbiased display of all matching offers. Another research track covers both; they matter here because the comparison engine must keep each RE's data separate.

### 4. Outsourcing: what the RE must require of Udofin

**4.1 NBFC Managing Risks in Outsourcing Directions, 2025** (RBI/DOR/2025-26/363, 28 Nov 2025) [confirmed, secondary]:

- **No prior RBI approval** is needed to outsource, but RBI monitoring and inspection extend to the arrangement.
- **Core functions cannot be outsourced:** internal audit, strategic and compliance functions, KYC compliance decisions, **loan sanctioning**, and investment portfolio management ([search summary of TaxGuru/Complinity](https://taxguru.in/rbi/rbi-non-banking-financial-companies-managing-risks-outsourcing-directions-2025.html)).
  - _Consequence:_ Udofin's decision engine may only run the RE's Board-approved credit policy. The RE must own and sign off the decision, the rule set and any model.
- **Board-approved outsourcing policy** covering financial and IT outsourcing: risk assessment, monitoring, BCP and **exit strategy** [confirmed].
- **Contract terms** [confirmed, secondary: [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/financial-services/1727478/rbis-outsourcing-directions-2025-re-defining-control-accountability-and-contracting-for-nbfcs); [KSK](https://ksandk.com/corporate/rbi-outsourcing-directions-2025/)]:
  - audit by the RE's internal and external auditors, with access to books, records, systems and **logs**;
  - **inspection by RBI or its authorised persons of the service provider and its subcontractors**;
  - strict controls on subcontracting, with the primary provider remaining accountable;
  - BCP/DR and an orderly exit.
  - Commentators warn that access clauses which depend on vendor consent or "commercial feasibility" create non-compliance risk.
- **Carried forward from the 2017 NBFC outsourcing directions** [likely; not re-verified in the 2025 text]:
  - the service provider must be able to **isolate and identify each NBFC's customer data, documents and assets**, especially where it serves several NBFCs, and hand them back on termination;
  - customer confidentiality must survive the end of the contract;
  - **subcontractors only with the RE's prior consent**;
  - the RE must **notify RBI immediately of any security breach or leak of customer data**;
  - the provider must document, maintain and test business-continuity and recovery procedures;
  - for recovery agents: a code of conduct, borrower disclosure, and calls only between **8 am and 7 pm** (Aug 2022 circular).
- **Chapter IV (IT outsourcing)** applies to NBFC-ML and above. Base-layer NBFCs follow only the general chapters [confirmed, secondary]. Udofin should still offer every partner the Chapter IV standard, so it has one contract template and is ready for upgrades.

**4.2 IT outsourcing provisions** (from the 2023 MD, now Chapter IV) [likely]:

- due diligence on the provider;
- the agreement covers data ownership and confidentiality, RBI and RE access to the provider's IT infrastructure, data and logs **including sub-contractors'**, the right to audit (pooled audits and certifications such as ISO 27001 or SOC 2 can support but not replace RE oversight), incident reporting, BCP/DR testing, termination and exit assistance;
- concentration-risk management;
- an annex on cloud and an annex on outsourced SOCs;
- a requirement that the provider's grievance handling does not dilute the RE's.

For Udofin this applies twice: once as LSP, and again if **Finbyx** (LOS/LMS) is sold to the same NBFCs.

### 5. RBI MD on IT Governance, Risk, Controls and Assurance Practices (2023)

This binds REs (NBFC-ML and above). Through the outsourcing contract and RBI's "RE remains responsible" principle, it reaches Udofin as flow-down [likely; not re-verified]:

- IT Strategy and Steering Committees, a CISO, an information security policy and cyber-risk management;
- IT service and change management, patching and secure SDLC;
- **VA at least every 6 months and PT at least every 12 months** for critical and internet-facing (DMZ, customer-facing) systems, plus VA/PT before go-live and after major changes [likely];
- BCP/DR with defined RTO/RPO and **periodic DR drills** (a half-yearly cadence is my recollection; **[unverified]**);
- audit-log capture and review, and an IS audit function.

**RBI cyber-incident reporting:** RBI has moved to **6-hour** reporting windows in several 2023–24 directions. The exact NBFC requirement in the consolidated text is **[unverified]**. Udofin should contractually report to the RE **within 2 hours** of detection so the RE can meet any 6-hour clock [recommendation].

### 6. CERT-In Directions, 28 April 2022 (s.70B(6) IT Act)

These bind Udofin directly as a body corporate [likely; high confidence, not re-fetched]:

1. **Report** any of the listed incident types, which include data breaches and leaks, unauthorised access, ransomware, attacks on applications and fake apps, **within 6 hours** of noticing them or being told of them.
2. **Logs** of all ICT systems must be enabled, kept securely for a **rolling 180 days within Indian jurisdiction**, and produced to CERT-In on request.
3. **Clocks** must be synchronised to NIC or NPL NTP servers, or to servers traceable to them.
4. A **point of contact** must be designated and registered with CERT-In.

The 2022 FAQs were reported to allow logs to sit outside India if they can be produced promptly **[unverified]**. Because of the RBI rule that data must be stored on servers in India, Udofin should **keep logs in India regardless**. CERT-In's later audit-policy guidelines (reportedly July 2025, calling for at least annual audits by empanelled auditors) are **[unverified]**.

### 7. Data localisation: consolidated position for Udofin

- **Digital-lending data** (everything collected through the DLA) stays on servers in India. Offshore processing must be deleted and returned within 24 hours [confirmed].
- **Payment data** (RBI, 6 Apr 2018): the PSO stores the entire payment data in India. The 2019 FAQs allow processing abroad if the data is deleted and returned within one business day or 24 hours, whichever is earlier [likely]. This is the PA's obligation, but Udofin should not hold card data (tokenisation and PA rules).
- **CERT-In logs:** 180 days in India [likely].
- **Udofin's own e-books of account** (Companies (Accounts) Rules 2014, r.3, as amended in August 2022): accessible in India at all times, with a **daily backup on servers physically located in India** [likely].
- **DPDP s.16:** no restriction notified yet, and stricter RBI rules prevail [likely].
- **Hosting consequence** [interpretation]:
  - Supabase in Mumbai is acceptable only if **all** of these stay in India: database, storage, auth, backups, logs and edge/serverless functions.
  - A Node backend on **Railway**, whose regions to my knowledge are US, EU and Singapore with no India region **[unverified]**, would process and log borrower data offshore by default.
  - Move compute to an Indian region now: AWS ap-south-1 Mumbai or ap-south-2 Hyderabad, GCP asia-south1/2, or an Azure India region.

### 8. Electronic records, eSign, stamping, NeSL DDE, CERSAI

- **IT Act 2000** [likely]:
  - s.4: legal recognition of electronic records.
  - s.3A and the **Second Schedule**: "e-authentication technique using Aadhaar or other e-KYC services" is a recognised electronic signature, delivered through CCA-licensed eSign Service Providers. Udofin can act as an **Application Service Provider (ASP)** on boarding with an ESP. The ESP does the Aadhaar authentication; Udofin should never receive or store the Aadhaar number.
  - s.10A: contracts formed electronically are valid.
  - s.7: retained electronic records must stay accessible, in the original format, with origin, destination and time metadata.
  - **First Schedule exclusions:** the IT Act does **not** apply to a **negotiable instrument other than a cheque** (so a **demand promissory note cannot be e-signed**), a power of attorney, a trust, a will, or **any contract for sale or conveyance of immovable property**.
  - Evidence: Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam 2023 s.63 (which replaced Evidence Act s.65B from 1 July 2024) requires a certificate for electronic records. Keep eSign audit trails and hash chains.
- **Stamp duty** [likely]:
  - Loan agreements, hypothecation, pledge and mortgage documents are chargeable under the Indian Stamp Act 1899 and **state** stamp laws. Since the 2019 amendment the definition of "instrument" (s.2(14)) **includes electronic records**.
  - Duty depends on the state of execution and the type of instrument. It must be paid on or before execution through state e-stamping (SHCIL as Central Record-keeping Agency in many states) or franking.
  - Since _In re Interplay between Arbitration Agreements and the Stamp Act_ (7-judge bench, 13 Dec 2023), an unstamped instrument is **inadmissible in evidence but not void**, and the defect can be cured. It still disrupts enforcement, including Equator-run arbitration.
  - **NeSL Digital Document Execution (DDE)** combines e-stamping, eSign and storage with an Information Utility record, which helps prove debt under the IBC and for enforcement. State coverage varies.
- **Mortgage loans (LAP/home):** an equitable mortgage (deposit of title deeds) or registered mortgage needs **physical or state e-registration processes**, not Aadhaar eSign. Vehicle loans need hypothecation endorsed on the RC (VAHAN).
- **CERSAI (SARFAESI Act 2002)** [likely]: the RE (the secured creditor, **not Udofin**) must register the security interest, whether mortgage, vehicle hypothecation or other movables, **within 30 days** of creation (s.23). Under s.26D, an unregistered interest cannot be enforced under SARFAESI. For loan against securities the pledge is created in the depository system (NSDL/CDSL), not through CERSAI. Udofin's role is to supply the data and evidence workflow.

### 9. Record-retention schedule

The **RE is the legal record-keeper** for KYC, loan and transaction records. Udofin either (a) holds them **in an RE-owned, RE-keyed vault as processor**, following the RE's schedule, or (b) passes them to the RE and keeps only basic minimal data, as the DL Directions require.

Statutory anchors [likely]:

- **PMLA s.12 / PML Rules:** transaction records 5 years from the transaction; identity records **5 years after the business relationship ends or the account closes, whichever is later**.
- **RBI KYC MD** "Record Management": the same 5-year anchors.
- **Companies Act s.128(5):** books and vouchers for **8 financial years**.
- **CGST s.36:** 72 months from the annual-return due date.
- **CERT-In:** logs for 180 days.
- **DPDP Rules 6 and 8:** logs and personal data for at least 1 year (from 2027).
- **Limitation Act:** 3 years for contract claims and 12 years for mortgage-money suits, which drive the dispute-defence periods.

### 10. FREE-AI and AI chatbots

RBI released the **FREE-AI** (Framework for Responsible and Ethical Enablement of AI) committee report in August 2025 [likely; not re-verified this session]. It sets out 7 "sutras" (principles) and about 26 recommendations covering:

- a Board-approved AI policy and an AI inventory;
- model-risk management, data-lifecycle governance and red-teaming;
- AI incident reporting;
- **telling customers when they are dealing with AI**, with a path to a human and grievance redress;
- audit and assurance.

It is a **committee report, not a binding direction**. Whether RBI has since issued binding AI directions is **[unverified]**. Partner REs will still apply it to vendors. My interpretation for Udofin's AI agent:

1. Disclose that it is AI, and offer a human hand-off.
2. It must not make credit decisions or give personalised advice outside the RE-approved script.
3. It must not act as the RE's grievance redressal officer, and every complaint must be logged into the RE's grievance system.
4. Borrower PII sent to an offshore LLM API is "processing outside India". It is permissible only with deletion and repatriation within 24 hours and contractual zero retention. India-region model hosting is preferable; confirm current regional availability before choosing.
5. Keep an AI register, an evaluation and red-team record, and incident logs.

## Challenges to the owner's plan

1. **Railway backend (no India region, to my knowledge).** _Problem:_ borrower data would be processed, and logs stored, outside India. _Authority:_ DL Directions (servers in India, 24-hour repatriation); CERT-In logs in India; data-residency review by the RE's IT outsourcing team. _Alternative:_ host compute in an Indian region (AWS Mumbai/Hyderabad, GCP Mumbai/Delhi or Azure India). Keep Supabase in ap-south-1 and confirm that auth, storage, edge functions, logs and backups also stay in India.
2. **Udofin as data owner of the "marketplace" KYC, bureau and loan data.** _Problem:_ the LSP may store only basic minimal data. Keeping full KYC and bureau files in Udofin's own database for cross-lender reuse breaches the DL Directions and outsourcing confidentiality and isolation duties. _Alternative:_ a per-RE vault owned and keyed by the RE, with Udofin as processor. Data reaches a second RE only through a fresh, explicit, RE-specific consent.
3. **"CIBIL soft pull by Udofin" via Surepass.** _Problem:_ under the CIC regime credit information goes to credit institutions and specified users. An LSP pulling reports in its own name for lending is legally doubtful, and the data then sits with the LSP [interpretation; covered in depth by the KYC/bureau track]. _Alternative:_ pull through the RE's CIC membership as its processor, or use a borrower-consented pre-qualification product a CIC expressly licenses to LSPs. Delete pre-qualification data within ≤30 days.
4. **"Aadhaar fetched via OTP" by Udofin.** _Problem:_ Aadhaar OTP eKYC needs a UIDAI-licensed AUA/KUA, and storing Aadhaar numbers needs an Aadhaar Data Vault [likely]. _Alternative:_ run Aadhaar eKYC through the RE as KUA, or use DigiLocker or offline Aadhaar XML. Never store the Aadhaar number; keep masked copies only.
5. **eSign + eStamp "in Udofin" for every product.** _Problem:_ DPNs (negotiable instruments) and contracts for sale or conveyance of immovable property fall outside the IT Act (First Schedule). Mortgages need registration or deposit of title deeds. Stamp duty varies by state. _Alternative:_ use NeSL DDE or an ESP with state e-stamping. Drop the DPN or execute it physically. Run LAP and home loans through the RE's physical or e-registration workflow, with the RE filing CERSAI within 30 days.
6. **Phone permissions (location, OTP reading, camera).** _Problem:_ the DL Directions allow only one-time camera, mic and location for KYC with consent. Reading SMS is outside that and against Play policy. _Alternative:_ use the SMS Retriever/User Consent API for OTP, one-time camera for document capture, and coarse one-time location only if the RE's KYC needs it.
7. **Personal "books of accounts"/expense view.** _Problem:_ building it needs SMS or bank data. SMS/media access is prohibited, and the use is a new Udofin purpose, separate from lending. _Alternative:_ fetch bank data through Account Aggregator via an RE acting as FIU, with separate optional consent, kept in a separate store and never shared with REs without fresh consent.
8. **Marketing push notifications.** _Problem:_ bundling marketing consent with the loan breaches "unconditional"/specific consent (DPDP) and the DL Directions' explicit-consent rule. _Alternative:_ a separate opt-in, one-tap opt-out, and no use of loan or bureau data for targeting without consent.
9. **AI agent answering borrower queries.** _Problem:_ offshore LLM processing of PII, unclear accountability, and no human path. _Alternative:_ apply FREE-AI-style controls (§10), India-hosted or zero-retention inference with 24-hour deletion, PII redaction, an AI register, and RE-approved knowledge bases.
10. **Escalating grievances to Equator (group ADR) "without a separate app".** _Problem:_ it is a disclosure to a third party (group affiliate), which needs specific explicit consent. It is a conflict of interest. It cannot replace or delay the RE's grievance process or the borrower's right to approach the RBI Integrated Ombudsman. _Alternative:_ make Equator a voluntary, post-dispute option with a separate consent screen, minimal data transfer, a DPA, and clear disclosure of the group relationship.
11. **Stripe and PayPal "for all products".** _Problem (data angle):_ PSO payment-data localisation. PayPal discontinued domestic INR payments in India in 2021 and Stripe restricted onboarding of Indian businesses [likely; the payments track should verify]. _Alternative:_ RBI-authorised Indian PAs (for example Razorpay) for collections, and NPCI e-mandate rails through the RE's sponsor bank.
12. **Finbyx LOS/LMS sold to the same NBFCs that use Udofin.** _Problem:_ a conflict of interest and cross-lender data-leakage risk. Two IT-outsourcing relationships both need Chapter IV-compliant contracts. _Alternative:_ logically and cryptographically separate tenants, separate DPAs, a Chinese-wall access model, and disclosure to REs.
13. **FLDG-linked collections by Udofin.** _Problem:_ DPDP s.17(1)(f) is narrow, and the DL Directions bar contact-list harvesting. _Alternative:_ collections as the RE's disclosed recovery agent under the RE's recovery policy, a dialer enforcing 8 am–7 pm, recordings in India, and no third-party contact.

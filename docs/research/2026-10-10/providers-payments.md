# Integration providers and money-movement rails for Udofin (Indian LSP), as of 2026-10-10

**Executive summary.** All of Udofin's regulated rails, where money moves or regulated data is accessed, have to run in the name of each partnering regulated entity (RE), not in Udofin's own name. These rails are repayment collection, eNACH/UPI AutoPay mandates, disbursement, bureau pulls, CKYC, Account Aggregator consent, depository pledges and MF lien marking. Udofin's legitimate role is technology service provider (TSP) and LSP. It builds and runs the integrations using each RE's merchant IDs, mandate utility codes, bureau memberships and FIU registrations. It never holds or routes borrower funds. Secondary commentary on the RBI (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025 (8 May 2025), and on their reported consolidation into entity-wise directions on 28 Nov 2025, is consistent on two points. Disbursals and repayments must flow directly between the borrower's bank account and the RE's bank account. No LSP or third-party pass-through or pool account is allowed, except for co-lending and same-day-reflected cash recovery on delinquent loans. Under the 2023 RBI FAQ, an RBI-authorised payment aggregator that only does payment aggregation may still facilitate loan repayment. The owner's plan to use **Stripe and PayPal "for all products" is not viable for domestic loan repayments**. Stripe has been invite-only in India since May 2024 and its own help pages still describe it that way. PayPal shut its domestic India payments business on 1 April 2021 and handles only cross-border payments. Use Razorpay, Cashfree, PayU or Juspay with each RE as the merchant, plus NPCI e-mandate rails. Surepass covers most KYC checks (CKYC, multi-bureau, PAN, penny-drop, penny-less and reverse penny-drop, UAN/EPFO, face match/liveness, DigiLocker SDK, NSDL/Aadhaar eSign). It authenticates with a Bearer token, has a sandbox (`sandbox.surepass.app`) and offers webhook callbacks. **Surepass does not make Udofin a CIC member or a CERSAI reporting entity.** Bureau and CKYC pulls must be made against each RE's membership. **Research limitation:** the session hit its shared web-search cap of 200 calls, and egress to most primary domains was blocked (rbi.org.in, npci.org.in, razorpay.com, cashfree.com, supabase.com, docs.expo.dev, surepass.io, sahamati.org.in, help.tallysolutions.com). Primary verification was possible only for Supabase and Expo docs (their GitHub sources) and Surepass's GitHub SDK README. Everything else is marked **[unverified]** or rests on secondary sources and needs re-checking before any build or legal decision.

**Regulatory overlay [interpretation].** Surepass is a technology aggregator. It is not a credit institution under CICRA, a CERSAI reporting entity or a UIDAI AUA/KUA licensed for Udofin's purposes. So:

- **Bureau pulls** for credit underwriting should be made under each RE's bureau membership, with the RE's member credentials. A consumer-consented "soft pull" in Udofin's name is legally weak support for a lending decision, and other research streams cover this.
- **CKYC search/download** is permitted to reporting entities, so it must use the RE's CKYC/FI credentials, with Udofin acting as the RE's agent.
- **Aadhaar OTP e-KYC** for a loan must be the RE's e-KYC under the RE's (or its KUA's) licence. The DigiLocker or Aadhaar offline-XML route is cleaner for an LSP-run UI.

Ask Surepass in writing whose CIC membership, CERSAI code and AUA/KUA licence each endpoint runs under.

### 2. eSign: CCA-licensed ESPs vs aggregators (ASPs)

- **Legal basis [likely].** Under the Information Technology Act, 2000 (s.3A and the Second Schedule), "eSign" (online Aadhaar e-KYC-based electronic signature) is a recognised electronic signature. It can be provided only by **eSign Service Providers (ESPs)**, which are Certifying Authorities licensed and empanelled by the Controller of Certifying Authorities ([CCA](https://cca.gov.in), not fetched). ESPs generally said to be empanelled include eMudhra, Protean (formerly NSDL e-Gov), (n)Code Solutions, C-DAC and Capricorn. **[unverified – check the current CCA ESP list]**.
- **Aggregators are ASPs, not ESPs [likely].** Leegality, Digio, SignDesk, Signzy and Surepass ("NSDL e-Sign") act as Application Service Providers. They integrate with one or more ESPs and add workflow, multi-party signing, stamping and audit trails. Protean and eMudhra are ESPs and also sell direct ASP integrations.
- **Evidence an ESP/ASP flow returns [likely, from the CCA eSign API pattern]:**
  1. The ESP's signed response XML, containing the ESP **transaction ID**, timestamp, response/error codes and the signer's short-lived **X.509 signing certificate**. The certificate carries the name as per Aadhaar e-KYC, state/pincode and a hashed or masked identifier, and chains to the CCA root.
  2. A **PKCS#7/CMS signature** over the document hash, which the ASP embeds into the **signed PDF**.
  3. The ASP's **audit trail**: IP, device, geo (if consented), timestamps for viewing, consent and OTP, document hash before and after, and signer sequence. It is usually appended as a certificate page and stored separately.

  Retain all three and verify the PDF signature server-side on receipt.

- **Exclusions [likely – IT Act First Schedule].** Electronic signatures do not apply to negotiable instruments other than cheques, powers of attorney, trusts, wills, or contracts for sale or conveyance of immovable property. **Home loan and LAP mortgage documents** (registered mortgages, memoranda of deposit of title deeds where state law requires them, POAs) therefore need wet-ink or registration workflows, not eSign. Pledge of demat securities is executed through the depository, not by eSign.

### 3. E-stamping

- **SHCIL** acts as the Central Record Keeping Agency for e-stamping in the states that have adopted the SHCIL e-stamp system. Each certificate has a unique certificate number that can be verified on [shcilestamp.com](https://www.shcilestamp.com) **[likely; not fetched]**.
- **NeSL Digital Document Execution (DDE)** combines e-stamping, eSign and storage on NeSL (an Information Utility under the IBC). It is available only in states that have authorised NeSL for e-stamping. The state count changes, so obtain NeSL's current state list **[unverified]**. Aggregators such as Leegality, Digio and SignDesk procure e-stamps through SHCIL or state vendors or NeSL, depending on the state.
- **Design rules [interpretation].** Stamp duty depends on the **state of execution** and the instrument (loan agreement, hypothecation, pledge, guarantee). With borrowers spread across states, stamp state is chosen per loan. The e-stamp must name the actual parties (RE and borrower), carry the correct article and amount, and be procured **before or at** execution. Pre-bought "stamp paper banks" in an aggregator's name, scanned physical stamp paper, and stamps bought in the wrong state are recurring enforceability risks. The **RE** is the stakeholder in enforceability, so the RE should approve the stamping matrix (state × product × article × duty).

### 4. Recurring collection: eNACH / API e-Mandate / UPI AutoPay

- **Rails [likely].** NPCI's NACH supports physical NACH, **API e-Mandate** (net-banking or debit-card authentication; an Aadhaar/eSign-based e-mandate was also offered) and **UPI AutoPay**.
  - API e-Mandate amount caps are commonly cited as up to ₹1 crore per mandate for net-banking/debit-card modes. **[unverified – confirm on NPCI and sponsor-bank docs]**.
  - UPI AutoPay mandates follow the RBI e-mandate framework for cards, PPIs and UPI. Per-debit additional factor of authentication (AFA) is exempt up to ₹15,000, with a higher ₹1 lakh cap for specified categories (MF subscriptions, insurance premiums, credit-card bills). Loan EMIs are understood **not** to be in that ₹1 lakh list. A pre-debit notification at least 24 hours before each debit is required. **[unverified in-session; verify against RBI's e-mandate circulars]**.
  - NPCI has also issued AutoPay operating changes on execution windows and retry caps. **[unverified – obtain the current NPCI circular from the provider]**.
- **Sponsor bank and utility [likely].** The **RE** (as the "corporate"/utility) obtains a NACH utility code through a **sponsor bank**. Each mandate gets a UMRN registered against the borrower's **destination bank**. Providers (Razorpay, Cashfree, Digio, PayU, Worldline/Paynimo, Decentro and others) are NPCI-certified technology/aggregator layers over their sponsor-bank tie-ups. For DL compliance, the mandate creditor must be the **RE**, and debited funds must settle into the RE's account, not Udofin's **[interpretation from the DL flow-of-funds rule]**.
- **Lifecycle (generic) [likely].**
  1. Create mandate (amount cap, frequency or "as presented", start/end dates, purpose).
  2. Borrower authenticates (net-banking, debit card or UPI PIN).
  3. Destination bank accepts or rejects, which produces the UMRN.
  4. Presentation: the debit file or API call goes via the sponsor bank on or before the due date.
  5. Settlement, or a return with an **NPCI return reason code** (e.g., insufficient balance, account closed, mandate not registered or cancelled, payment stopped).
  6. Re-presentation within the rail's retry rules.
  7. Amendment, suspension or cancellation, including borrower-initiated cancellation through their bank.
  8. Mandate expiry or revocation at loan closure.

  Get the current return-code table from the chosen provider or sponsor bank. Do not hard-code it from memory.

- **Bounce handling [likely].** Any bounce or "dishonour" charge is a **penal charge** under RBI's Aug 2023 penal-charges circular, effective 1 Jan 2024. It must be reasonable, disclosed in the KFS/sanction letter, and not capitalised or compounded. Udofin's collections UX must show the RE's charge schedule, not Udofin's.
- **Provider notes.** **Razorpay**: recurring payments with eMandate, UPI AutoPay, cards and paper NACH; tokens per customer; subsequent debits via API ([docs root](https://razorpay.com/docs/), not fetched). **Cashfree**: Subscriptions covering eNACH, pNACH, UPI AutoPay and cards ([docs](https://www.cashfree.com/docs/), not fetched). **Digio**: eNACH, UPI AutoPay, eSign and e-stamp in one stack, popular with NBFCs. **Worldline/Paynimo**: long-standing NBFC eNACH. **PayU**: recurring payments. **Decentro**: API layer over partner banks for NACH/UPI. All of these are **[likely; not fetched]**.

### 5. Payment gateways for one-time repayment, part-prepayment and foreclosure

- **Razorpay, Cashfree, PayU and Juspay [likely].** These are RBI-regulated online payment aggregators or (for Juspay) primarily a payment-orchestration layer over multiple PAs. Check each one's PA authorisation status on RBI's list. RBI's PA regime was reportedly consolidated in 2025. Per Stripe's India help pages, RBI's revised PA rules require liveness/video-KYC for new merchant onboarding from 1 Jan 2026 ([Stripe – 2026 document collection](https://support.stripe.com/questions/2026-document-collection-for-india-accounts); [Stripe – video KYC](https://support.stripe.com/questions/video-kyc-for-india-onboarding)) **[likely]**. Each RE onboarding as a merchant will go through this.
- **Stripe [confirmed – vendor help page via search snippet].** From May 2024, "Stripe services will be invite only in India". Businesses must request an invite, and the focus is on international expansion ([Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/moving-to-invite-only-in-india); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/us-payments-giant-stripe-goes-invite-only-in-india/), May 2024). Secondary reporting says Stripe received RBI PA authorisation on 15 Jan 2024 and that general availability had **not** resumed by 2026 **[likely]**. **Verdict:** not suitable as a primary rail for domestic INR loan repayments, and no known NACH/UPI AutoPay support for lenders **[interpretation]**.
- **PayPal [confirmed – secondary, multiple outlets].** PayPal wound down domestic payments in India from **1 April 2021** and kept only cross-border services ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/paypal-says-to-shut-domestic-payments-business-in-india/story/430375.html), 5 Feb 2021; [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/paypal-to-shut-down-domestic-payment-operations-in-india)). **Verdict:** cannot collect domestic INR EMIs from Indian borrowers. Not suitable.
- **Settlement must land in the RE's account.**
  - The DL flow-of-funds rule bars any LSP or third-party pass-through or pool account ([Nishith Desai, 2025](https://www.nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/regulatory-hotline/indias-new-digital-lending-law-building-trust-transparency-and-accountability-15354); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=b5bc9efb-1199-41ee-bc2d-4a149573793b)) **[likely – secondary; RBI text not reachable]**.
  - RBI's Feb 2023 DL FAQ allowed entities providing **only payment aggregation** to facilitate disbursals and repayments. A PA that also acts as an LSP must comply with the DL rules ([Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2023/02/223-rbi-faqs-digital-lending-guidelines/); [Argus](https://www.argus-p.com/updates/updates/faqs-on-digital-lending-guidelines/)) **[likely; check that it survives the 2025 Directions]**.
  - In practice:
    - **The RE is the merchant of record** on the PA, with settlement to the RE's current account.
    - Udofin either holds a **platform/partner account** with each RE as a **sub-merchant** (e.g., Razorpay Partner/OAuth) or uses API keys delegated by the RE.
    - **Do not** use split-settlement (e.g., "Route") to divert any part of a borrower's payment to Udofin. LSP fees must be paid by the RE and cannot be collected from borrowers ([Nishith Desai](https://www.nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/regulatory-hotline/indias-new-digital-lending-law-building-trust-transparency-and-accountability-15354)) **[interpretation]**.
- **Better repayment rails for reconciliation [likely].**
  - **Borrower-specific virtual accounts or static UPI VPAs** in the RE's name (bank-issued, or Razorpay Smart Collect / Cashfree Auto Collect in RE's name) for NEFT/IMPS/UPI repayments.
  - **BBPS "Loan Repayment" biller** onboarding of the RE, so borrowers can pay from any UPI or bank app.
  - **Payment links / UPI intent** generated from the RE's merchant account for overdue collections.

### 6. Disbursement rails

- **Rule [likely – secondary].** Disbursal goes into the **borrower's bank account**. Exceptions are a statutory or regulatory mandate, co-lending flows between REs, and specific end-use disbursal direct to the end-beneficiary such as a vendor, dealer, hospital or builder ([Nishith Desai](https://www.nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/regulatory-hotline/indias-new-digital-lending-law-building-trust-transparency-and-accountability-15354)). This matters for vehicle loans (dealer), medical finance (hospital) and "resolution finance" (counter-party/award-holder). Each needs the agreement to specify the end-beneficiary.
- **Mechanics [likely].** The RE's own corporate banking APIs (e.g., ICICI, HDFC, Axis, Yes, IDFC FIRST connected-banking APIs) using IMPS, NEFT or RTGS. NEFT and RTGS run 24×7. RTGS has a ₹2 lakh minimum. IMPS is capped at ₹5 lakh per transaction **[unverified in-session]**.
- **Payout platforms.** RazorpayX and Cashfree Payouts can be used **only** in a mode where payouts debit the **RE's own current account** (connected/direct-banking mode). Do not pre-fund a payout provider's pooled account controlled by Udofin, which risks being a "pass-through" **[interpretation]**. RBI's beneficiary-name look-up for RTGS/NEFT (banks were directed to provide it by 1 Apr 2025) adds a name-match control before credit **[unverified in-session]**.
- **Idempotency.** Use one disbursal instruction per loan, keyed on the loan ID. Rely on bank or payout-provider idempotency headers (e.g., RazorpayX payout idempotency key) **[unverified]** and on UTR-based reconciliation. Never auto-retry on timeout without a status enquiry.

### 7. Bank-account verification

- **Penny drop** credits ₹1 by IMPS and returns the beneficiary name. **Penny-less** validates the account and name without moving money. **Reverse penny drop** has the borrower pay ₹1 via UPI and returns the remitter account, IFSC and name, which proves the borrower controls the account; it is refunded later. **UPI VPA validation** is also available. Vendors include Surepass, [Cashfree Verification Suite](https://docs.cashfree.com/v3/reference/verification-api-overview) and [Signzy](https://docs.signzy.com/api-marketplace/introduction) **[confirmed – vendor docs via search]**. Cashfree says refunds of reverse-penny-drop amounts happen within 48 hours ([Cashfree](https://www.cashfree.com/docs/secure-id/kyc-stack/reverse-penny-drop)).
- **Control.** Name-match the bank account against the KYC name (fuzzy score plus manual review band). Bind the verified account to the loan for both disbursal and mandate. Re-verify on any change.

### 8. Account Aggregator (AA)

- **[likely – prior knowledge; sahamati.org.in not reachable].** Under RBI's NBFC-AA Master Direction (2016, as amended), **FIUs must be entities regulated by a financial-sector regulator** (RBI, SEBI, IRDAI or PFRDA). An unregulated LSP **cannot be an FIU**. It can be the **TSP of an RE-FIU**. Consent is requested in the RE's name, with the RE's purpose code, data life and frequency. Data is decrypted only in the RE's environment, or in Udofin's environment acting strictly as the RE's processor under contract. In a multi-lender marketplace, consent has to name each RE that will see the data (or be obtained separately per RE) **[interpretation]**. See [Sahamati](https://sahamati.org.in) (not fetched).

### 9. RBIH Unified Lending Interface (ULI)

- **[unverified – rbihub.in not reachable].** RBIH's ULI (formerly the Public Tech Platform for Frictionless Credit, piloted from Aug 2023 and renamed ULI in 2024) exposes consent-based APIs to **lenders**. These include land records, dairy/agri data, GST, AA and eSign. Onboarding is for REs. Udofin's role would be as the RE's technology partner, if RBIH permits. Treat ULI as a later, RE-driven integration.

### 10. Loan against securities (LAS)

- **Shares (NSDL/CDSL) [likely].** Under SEBI (Depositories and Participants) Regulations, 2018, the pledge is created by the borrower (pledgor/BO) in favour of the **pledgee's demat account**, and the depository records it. The pledgee invokes the pledge on default. Both depositories offer online, OTP-authenticated pledge initiation that lenders and their DPs integrate with. **Only the lender (RE) can be pledgee.** An LSP can orchestrate the UX as the RE's TSP. SEBI's 2020 "margin pledge" regime is for stock-broker margins, not lender pledges, so do not conflate them.
- **Mutual funds [likely].** Non-demat (SoA) units are lien-marked via the RTAs (**CAMS** and **KFintech**) or via **MF Central** (their joint platform), in favour of the lender. Demat MF units use the depository pledge route. LSPs such as Volt Money run digital LAMF journeys as LSPs for partner lenders, with the lien marked in the lender's favour **[likely]**. Udofin needs the **RE** to be onboarded as lien holder or pledgee with each RTA or depository, with Udofin as TSP.
- **Lending rules** (LTV caps, e.g., 50% for NBFC lending against listed shares, and bank LAS limits) belong to the RE's credit policy. Other research streams cover them. **[unverified here]**.

### 11. Accounting exports

- **TallyPrime [likely – help.tallysolutions.com not reachable].**
  - Integration is XML (and, in recent releases, reportedly JSON) **over HTTP to a running Tally instance**, default port **9000**, using `<ENVELOPE>` requests to import vouchers or masters and export reports or collections. TDL customises it, and ODBC is available.
  - There is **no hosted multi-tenant cloud API**. Tally must be running on a desktop or a hosted VM.
  - Practical pattern:
    1. Udofin generates **daily summarised journal vouchers** per RE, product and GL mapping.
    2. A small **on-premise connector** (or the customer's operator) pulls them from Udofin's API and posts them to `localhost:9000`, with a voucher `GUID`/`REMOTEID` built from Udofin's journal ID for idempotency.
    3. The connector pushes the results back.
  - A file-based XML/Excel import is the fallback. The exact JSON support in TallyPrime 6.x/7.x is **[unverified]**.
- **SAP S/4HANA [likely – api.sap.com not reachable].**
  - Post journal entries through SAP's Journal Entry posting APIs. In S/4HANA Cloud these are SOAP "Journal Entry – Post", synchronous or asynchronous. On-premise uses BAPI_ACC_DOCUMENT_POST or the ACC_DOCUMENT IDoc.
  - Read back through the OData journal-entry item APIs.
  - Use the external reference or document-header text as the idempotency key. Reconcile by posting date and company code.
- **Whose books.** Loan principal, interest and charges sit in the **RE's** books, and Finbyx or the RE's LMS is the system of record. Udofin's own books carry LSP fee income, DLG (FLDG) deposits or guarantees, and collection-agency fees. Never mix the two.

### 12. Messaging: SMS (DLT), WhatsApp, push

- **SMS DLT (TRAI TCCCPR, 2018 and amendments) [likely].**
  - The sender (Principal Entity) registers itself, its **headers** (sender IDs), **content templates** (with `{#var#}` placeholders) and consent templates on an operator DLT platform.
  - Messages that do not match a registered template are blocked.
  - TRAI directed that SMS containing URLs, APKs, OTT links or call-back numbers not whitelisted in the template be blocked from late 2024. It also made PE→TM chain traceability mandatory from Dec 2024. A Feb 2025 TCCCPR amendment added header suffixes by category and tightened consent rules.
  - All of these are **[unverified in-session; verify on trai.gov.in]**.
  - OTPs go as **service-implicit/transactional** templates.
  - Providers include MSG91, Gupshup, Kaleyra (Tata Communications), Route Mobile, Karix and Twilio (India traffic still needs a DLT-registered entity, header and template).
- **Supabase phone OTP with a DLT provider [confirmed – Supabase docs source].**
  - Supabase's **Send SMS hook** replaces built-in SMS sending. The payload contains `user` (with `phone`) and `sms.otp`.
  - Implement it as an HTTP hook (e.g., an Edge Function). Verify with the `standardwebhooks` library using the `v1,whsec_…` secret, and return HTTP 200 with an empty body on success. Supabase's docs name **MSG91** for India and allow customising the body, e.g., to append an Android **AppHash** for the SMS Retriever API ([Send SMS hook](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/auth-hooks/send-sms-hook)).
  - Hooks have a **5-second total budget including retries**. HTTP hooks retry at most three times, only on 429/503 with a `retry-after` header ([Auth hooks](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/auth-hooks)).
  - The hook therefore has to call the DLT provider's OTP API (e.g., MSG91 with the DLT template ID) quickly. Queue a fallback provider asynchronously instead of retrying inside the budget.
  - Built-in providers are MessageBird, Twilio, Vonage and TextLocal (community) ([Phone login](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/phone-login)). None handles DLT natively, so use the hook.
- **WhatsApp Business Platform [likely].** Business-initiated messages need prior **opt-in** that names the business, plus Meta-approved templates (marketing, utility or authentication categories). Authentication templates support OTP copy-code or one-tap autofill. Marketing on WhatsApp also needs DPDP-grade consent, separate from loan servicing. Recovery or reminder messages must follow the RE's fair-practices and recovery-conduct rules.
- **Push (Expo → FCM/APNs) [confirmed – Expo docs source].**
  - Endpoint: `https://exp.host/--/api/v2/push/send`. Up to 100 messages per request and 600 notifications per second per project.
  - Tickets are returned immediately. **Receipts** should be fetched about 15 minutes later and are deleted after 24 hours. Handle `DeviceNotRegistered` by disabling the token.
  - Enable "enhanced push security" (an access token) ([Sending notifications](https://docs.expo.dev/push-notifications/sending-notifications/)).
  - From SDK 53, Expo Go does not support push, so use a development build. FCM v1 and APNs credentials are required. Expo's service is free. Native device tokens can bypass Expo and go direct to FCM/APNs ([FAQ](https://docs.expo.dev/push-notifications/faq/)).
  - **Data-localisation caution [interpretation].** Expo, FCM and APNs process payloads outside India. Keep loan or PII data out of push payloads and send only "You have a new message", with deep-link IDs.

## Challenges to the owner's plan

1. **"Razorpay, Stripe and PayPal for all products."**
   - _Problem:_ Stripe is invite-only in India and its focus is international expansion ([Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/moving-to-invite-only-in-india)). PayPal has had no domestic India payments since 1 Apr 2021 ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/paypal-says-to-shut-domestic-payments-business-in-india/story/430375.html)). Neither supports NACH or UPI AutoPay for Indian lenders.
   - _Recommendation:_ Use Razorpay or Cashfree (plus PayU, or Juspay orchestration, as backup), NPCI e-mandates, VANs and BBPS. Drop Stripe and PayPal for loan flows. Revisit only if the business adds a cross-border product.
2. **Udofin as payment merchant or collector.**
   - _Problem:_ Under the DL flow-of-funds rule, repayment must go directly to the RE's account. LSP pass-through or pool accounts are barred (secondary sources; RBI text unverified in-session).
   - _Recommendation:_ Each RE is merchant of record, mandate utility and VAN owner. Udofin integrates through platform or partner access, with no split-settlement to Udofin.
3. **"5% FLDG, therefore Udofin handles collections."**
   - _Problem:_ Providing DLG does not change the flow of funds. Udofin as recovery agent still cannot receive borrower money other than RE-receipted cash on delinquent loans, posted the same day. DLG must sit with the RE in the permitted forms. _Authority:_ DL Directions 2025 (DLG chapter; secondary).
   - _Recommendation:_ Appoint Udofin as the RE's recovery agent under the RE's outsourcing policy. Settle DLG invocations separately from collections.
4. **Surepass contract in Udofin's name for CIBIL "soft pull" and CKYC.**
   - _Problem:_ Bureau access is for CIC members (CICRA), and CKYC is for reporting entities.
   - _Recommendation:_ Use Surepass in Udofin's name only for non-member checks (PAN, bank verification, OCR, UAN). Run bureau, CKYC and Aadhaar e-KYC under each RE's credentials, through a tri-party (RE–Udofin–Surepass) agreement or the RE's own vendor contract.
5. **"Aadhaar fetched via OTP" by Udofin.**
   - _Problem:_ Aadhaar OTP e-KYC needs a UIDAI licence (AUA/KUA) and is the RE's KYC.
   - _Recommendation:_ Prefer DigiLocker or offline XML in the RE's name. If OTP e-KYC is used, run it as the RE's e-KYC and never store the Aadhaar number.
6. **"eSign + eStamp in Udofin" for all products.**
   - _Problem:_ eSign is excluded for immovable-property conveyance and POAs (IT Act First Schedule). Stamping is state-specific, and NeSL/SHCIL coverage varies.
   - _Recommendation:_ Approve a per-product document matrix with each RE. Home loans and LAP get a hybrid physical/registration flow. Evidence is stored as the RE's record.
7. **OTP auto-reading permission.**
   - _Problem:_ Google Play restricts READ_SMS/RECEIVE_SMS. Other research streams cover the details.
   - _Recommendation:_ Use the SMS Retriever or SMS User Consent API, with the AppHash appended via the Supabase Send SMS hook.
8. **Marketing push notifications.**
   - _Problem:_ Consent under DPDP is needed, and Expo/FCM/APNs process payloads abroad.
   - _Recommendation:_ Separate marketing consent, opaque payloads, and in-app retrieval of content.
9. **LAS via NSDL/CDSL.**
   - _Problem:_ Only the lender can be pledgee or lien holder.
   - _Recommendation:_ Partner with REs already onboarded with depositories and RTAs, or have the RE onboard. Udofin builds the TSP orchestration.
10. **Disbursement or payouts from Udofin's RazorpayX/Cashfree account.**
    - _Problem:_ This is a pass-through and the RE loses control.
    - _Recommendation:_ Payout from the RE's own current account (bank API or payout-provider connected-account mode).
11. **Tally "export".**
    - _Problem:_ There is no hosted cloud API.
    - _Recommendation:_ Daily voucher files or an on-prem connector to port 9000, with idempotent voucher IDs. SAP gets Journal Entry API/IDoc adapters.

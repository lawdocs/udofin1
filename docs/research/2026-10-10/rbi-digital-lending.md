# RBI digital lending framework as of 10 October 2026: what applies to Udofin as an LSP/DLA

## 1. Bottom line

1. **The 8 May 2025 Digital Lending Directions moved into the consolidated "Credit Facilities" Master Directions on 28 November 2025.** There is one per type of lender, and the digital lending chapter (Chapter III) carries over the May 2025 substance. That substance covers:
   - LSP arrangements, multi-lender display, KFS and cooling-off;
   - data, device permissions and grievance;
   - CIMS reporting of DLAs, credit reporting, and the default loss guarantee (DLG).
2. **The 31 July 2026 instruments are supervisory, not digital-lending rules.** They are the Department of Supervision consolidation: 64 Master Directions replacing 628 circulars, including cybersecurity/technology risk, fraud risk and internal audit. I found **no digital-lending-specific direction or amendment dated 31 July 2026.** They still matter to Udofin, because lenders must pass IT outsourcing, audit and incident-reporting duties down to their LSPs and tech vendors.
3. **The 2026 changes that do touch digital lending:**
   - 13 Feb 2026: NBFCs may count DLG in Expected Credit Loss (ECL) provisioning, subject to conditions.
   - Credit-bureau reporting moved to weekly from 1 July 2026.
   - New Integrated Ombudsman Scheme 2026 in force from 1 July 2026.
   - 15 Jun 2026: anti-mis-selling and dark-pattern rules, effective 1 Jan 2027.
   - 6 Aug 2026: harmonised recovery-agent rules, effective 1 Jan 2027.
   - Two pending drafts, both from Aug 2026: a ban on revolving credit by most NBFCs, and harmonised interest-rate directions that include an APR cap on small-value loans (≤ ₹50,000).
4. **Owner's plan.** The multi-lender aggregator model is allowed. However, several parts of the plan conflict with the rules:
   - The "FLDG, therefore we collect" logic.
   - Udofin running payment rails for repayments.
   - Udofin doing its own bureau pull.
   - Device permissions.
   - The AI agent and the Equator escalation path.
   - Payday loans.
   - Hosting borrower data outside India (Railway, LLM APIs).

## 2. Instrument map

### 2.1 Reserve Bank of India (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025, dated 8 May 2025

- **Identity.** Reference RBI/2025-26/36, DOR.STR.REC.19/21.07.001/2025-26 (secondary). It is RBI notification Id 12848: [RBI notification](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12848) (8 May 2025), with a copy on the [new RBI website](https://website.rbi.org.in/documents/d/rbi/directionsondigitallending).
- **What it merged.** The 2022 Digital Lending Guidelines, the 2023 DLG Guidelines and the related FAQs ([Vinod Kothari](https://vinodkothari.com/2025/05/digital-lending-directions-largely-a-consolidation-new-rules-on-multi-lender-platforms-and-lending-apps/), May 2025; [AZB update](https://www.azbpartners.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/AZB-Update-Digital-Lending-Directions-2025.pdf), May 2025).
- **Who it covered.** Commercial banks, co-operative banks, NBFCs (including HFCs) and All-India Financial Institutions (AIFIs).
- **Phased start dates:**
  - 8 May 2025: most provisions.
  - 15 June 2025: mandatory reporting of DLAs on CIMS. The portal opened by 13 May 2025.
  - 1 Nov 2025: **paragraph 6**, arrangements between an LSP and multiple lenders ([IndiaCorpLaw](https://indiacorplaw.in/2025/07/19/rbis-revised-digital-lending-guidelines-strengthening-guardrails-in-an-evolving-ecosystem/), Jul 2025; [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/news/rbi-asks-regulated-entities-to-report-digital-lending-apps-on-cims-125050901500_1.html), May 2025).

Two practical consequences:

- **Contracts and policies.** Udofin's lender contracts and policies should cite the Credit Facilities Directions for each lender type, and treat the May 2025 paragraph numbers only as cross-references.
- **One rulebook in code.** The substance is identical across lender types, but numbering and terms differ ("NBFC-LSP", "bank-LSP"). Udofin should keep one rulebook and map it to each instrument.

## 3. Obligations relevant to an LSP

Who bears each duty: **the lender (RE) is legally responsible for all of these.** The RBI binds LSPs through the lender's contract and outsourcing obligations, not through a licence. Paragraph numbers are from the May 2025 Directions where a source gave them; numbering in the consolidated instruments is unverified.

### 3.1 Definitions

- **Digital Lending App (DLA).** Mobile and/or web applications with a user interface that facilitate digital lending. This includes apps run by the lender and apps run by an LSP.
- **Lending Service Provider (LSP).** An agent of a lender, which may itself be another lender, that carries out one or more of the lender's digital lending functions under the outsourcing rules. Those functions include customer acquisition, services incidental to underwriting and pricing, servicing, monitoring and recovery.
- **Multi-lender arrangement.** An LSP with arrangements with more than one lender, which shows matching offers to borrowers.
- **What this means for Udofin.** It would be an LSP for every lender it works with, and the Udofin app would be a DLA that each lender must report on CIMS. Sources: [TaxGuru on NBFC CF Directions](https://taxguru.in/rbi/rbi-non-banking-financial-companies-credit-facilities-directions-2025.html) and [Lawrbit](https://www.lawrbit.com/article/reserve-bank-of-india-digital-lending-directions-2025/). The wording changed between the October 2025 draft and the final text.

### 3.2 Lender due diligence and outsourcing

- **Due diligence.** Before signing, the lender must check the LSP's technical ability, data-privacy policies and storage systems, fairness of conduct, and regulatory compliance, and must review this periodically.
- **Contract.** There must be a written contract defining roles and rights, with audit and inspection access.
- **Outsourcing rules apply** under the 2025 Outsourcing Directions.
- **Responsibility stays with the lender.** The lender remains fully responsible for what the LSP does.

### 3.3 How money moves

- **Disbursement goes straight into the borrower's bank account.** The only exceptions are:
  1. disbursals required by statute or regulation;
  2. money moving between lenders in co-lending;
  3. loans for a specific end-use, paid directly to the end-beneficiary.
- **Repayment goes straight into the lender's bank account.** No LSP pass-through account or pool account is allowed, and no third party may control the flow of funds ([Argus](https://www.argus-p.com/updates/updates/rbi-digital-lending-directions-2025-an-overview/); [Synergia](https://synergialegal.com/an-overview-of-the-rbis-digital-lending-direction-2025/)).
- Status: confirmed (secondary).

### 3.4 LSP fees

- **The lender pays the LSP; the borrower is never charged directly.** Any LSP fee or charge must come from the lender. Status: confirmed (secondary).

### 3.5 KFS and APR

- **When.** A KFS must be given **before the contract is executed**, in the standard format from the [KFS circular of 15 Apr 2024](https://website.rbi.org.in/web/rbi/-/notifications/key-facts-statement-kfs-for-loans-advances). That circular applied to new retail and MSME term loans from 1 Oct 2024 and now sits in the RBC Directions.
- **APR.** The APR is the all-inclusive cost, including third-party charges passed through.
- **No hidden charges.** A charge that is not in the KFS cannot be levied later without the borrower's explicit consent.
- **One KFS per offer** in a multi-lender setup.
- **Validity window.** The KFS has a validity window: three working days where the tenor is 7 days or more, one working day where it is shorter. Status: likely; check against the KFS text.

### 3.6 Multi-lender offer display (May 2025 para 6, in force from 1 Nov 2025)

- **Show every matching offer.** The LSP must show a digital view of **all** offers that match the borrower's request, from all lenders it works with. It must also **name the lenders that did not match.**
- **Neutral.** The view must be unbiased and objective, follow a **consistent, documented matching method**, and contain **no dark patterns** that steer the borrower to a particular lender.
- **What each offer shows** (para 6(iii)):
  - name of the lender;
  - loan amount and tenor;
  - APR;
  - monthly repayment;
  - penal charges, if any;
  - a **link to the KFS**.
- Sources: [Lawrbit](https://www.lawrbit.com/article/reserve-bank-of-india-digital-lending-directions-2025/), [Leegality](https://www.leegality.com/blog/digital-lending-directions-2025).
- **From 1 Jan 2027** the RBC Second Amendment's ban on dark patterns and its requirement for user testing are added on top.

### 3.7 Cooling-off (look-up) period

- **Length.** At least **one day**; the lender's board may set a longer period.
- **Exit right.** During this period the borrower can exit by repaying principal plus proportionate APR, with no penalty.
- Status: confirmed (secondary); the 2022 rules left the period entirely to the board.

### 3.8 Credit limits and creditworthiness

- **No automatic increase in a credit limit without the borrower's explicit consent.** Status: likely, carried over from 2022; no 2025 snippet confirmed it.
- **Underwriting.** The lender must record the borrower's economic profile (age, occupation, income) and assess creditworthiness. The LSP may support underwriting but cannot make the credit decision for the lender.

### 3.9 Data

- **Collection.** Only data that is needed, with **prior explicit consent** and an audit trail. Borrowers can refuse particular data, revoke consent, and ask for deletion.
- **Purpose limitation** applies.
- **What the LSP or DLA may keep.** It **must not store personal data beyond the basic minimum: name, address and contact details.**
- **Biometrics.** No collection or storage of biometric data unless the law allows it.
- **Location.** Data must be **stored only on servers in India.** If it is processed abroad, it must be deleted there and brought back to India **within 24 hours**.
- **Privacy policy.** A full privacy policy must be published, naming the third parties allowed to collect data.
- **Responsibility.** The lender remains responsible for data held by the LSP.
- Sources: [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=b5bc9efb-1199-41ee-bc2d-4a149573793b), [NLIU CBCL](https://cbcl.nliu.ac.in/contemporary-issues/analysing-rbis-digital-lending-directions-2025-a-positive-step-towards-responsible-lending/).
- **Separately**, the DPDP Act 2023 and the 2025 DPDP Rules apply. They were not researched here.

### 3.10 Device permissions

- **Not allowed.** DLAs must not access **files and media, contacts, call logs or telephony functions.**
- **Allowed once, for onboarding/KYC only.** One-time access to the **camera, microphone or location** is allowed, with explicit consent.
- **Google Play goes further for Indian personal-loan apps.** It also bars READ_MEDIA_IMAGES, READ_MEDIA_VIDEOS, READ_CONTACTS, ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION, READ_PHONE_NUMBERS, READ/WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE and QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES ([Play Developer Program Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14906471?hl=en)).

### 3.11 Grievances

- **Officers.** The lender and the LSP must each name a nodal grievance redressal officer. Their details go on the lender's website, the LSP's website, the DLA and the KFS.
- **Escalation.** If the lender has not resolved a complaint within **30 days**, the borrower may go to the RBI Ombudsman.
  - From 1 Jul 2026 this is **RB-IOS 2026**. The complaint must first be made to the lender. After 30 days without resolution the borrower may file, within a 90-day window ([RB-IOS 2026 FAQ](https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/Upload/English/FAQs/PDFs/RBIOS01072026.pdf); salient-features notices published by lenders).
- **AI and ADR are add-ons only.** Neither an AI chatbot nor an ADR platform can replace or delay this path.

### 3.12 CIMS reporting and the public DLA directory

- **Who reports.** Lenders report every DLA they use, their own or an LSP's, exclusive or shared, on CIMS. The deadline was 15 Jun 2025, and additions and removals continue to be reported. The Chief Compliance Officer certifies accuracy and compliance ([Vinod Kothari](https://vinodkothari.com/2025/09/understanding-the-ccos-certification-for-dlas-digital-lending-directions-2025/), Sep 2025).
- **The public directory.** RBI's directory ("DLAs deployed by Regulated Entities", under Citizen's Corner) has been **live since 1 Jul 2025** ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2241255&reg=3&lang=2), 2026). It is filled from CIMS data **as reported, with no RBI validation.** A listing must not be presented as RBI approval.
- **App-store consequence.** Google Play requires Indian personal-loan apps to appear on this list: new apps from 30 Oct 2025, existing apps by 28 Jan 2026 ([Play Console – Personal Loans in India](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16604194?hl=en)). In practice, **Udofin cannot launch on Play until at least one partner lender has reported the Udofin DLA on CIMS.**

### 3.13 Credit reporting

- **The lender reports**, not the LSP. Every loan made through a DLA must be reported to the credit bureaus **regardless of its nature or tenor**, including BNPL and short-term credit.
- **Weekly from 1 Jul 2026.**

### 3.14 Recovery

- **Udofin can collect only as the lender's recovery agent**, under the outsourcing contract.
- **What the lender must tell the borrower:**
  - at sanction, which LSP acts as recovery agent;
  - if recovery is reassigned, the agent's details by SMS or email **before** the agent makes contact.
- **What the lender must publish:** its recovery agencies.
- **Conduct rules.** The current RBC Directions apply until 31 Dec 2026, followed by the stricter Third Amendment framework from 1 Jan 2027.
- **Industry code.** The SRO FACE published fintech debt-recovery guidelines on 29 Aug 2025 ([FACE](https://faceofindia.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/FACE-Guidelines-on-debt-recovery_29-Aug-2025_website.pdf)).

### 3.15 Default Loss Guarantee (DLG/FLDG)

- **Who can provide it.** An LSP, or a lender acting as an LSP, that is **incorporated under the Companies Act, 2013.** The lender needs a **board-approved DLG policy** covering provider eligibility, monitoring and fees, and a **legally enforceable contract** setting out the extent of cover, its form, the invocation timeline and disclosures.
- **Cap.** Total cover on any outstanding portfolio must not exceed **5% of the amount disbursed out of that portfolio** (the "DLG set"). The portfolio is fixed once earmarked. Invoked amounts are not reinstated, which RBI shows with an illustration.
- **Forms of cover:**
  - cash deposited with the lender;
  - a fixed deposit with a scheduled commercial bank, with lien in the lender's favour;
  - a bank guarantee in the lender's favour.
- **Invocation.** Within a **maximum of 120 days overdue**, unless the borrower pays first.
- **Provisioning.**
  - The lender must still recognise NPAs and provision for them **whatever DLG cover exists.**
  - From 13 Feb 2026, NBFCs using ECL may take DLG into account (paras 36A–36C) **if it is integral to the loan contract.**
- **Disclosure.** LSPs that give DLG must publish on their website **the number of portfolios covered and the amount of each.**
- **Excluded products.** Revolving credit offered digitally (including credit-card-type products), loans under credit-guarantee trust schemes, and P2P loans.
- **Co-lending.** Lenders may also use DLG in co-lending, an extension made in Aug 2025.
- Sources: [Corporate Professionals](https://www.corporateprofessionals.com/articles/default-loss-guarantee-dlg-in-digital-lending/), [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/financial-services/1332146/rbi-guidelines-on-default-loss-guarantee-dlg-in-digital-lending), [TaxCorp](https://thetaxcorp.in/article/rbi-issues-unified-nbfc-credit-rules-digital-lending-dlg-goldsilver-loans).
- **DLG creates no right or duty to collect.** Nothing in the DLG rules does this. Collection is a separate outsourced function. The lender keeps responsibility for underwriting, servicing and NPA recognition, and all repayments still go to the lender's account.
- Two points are likely but not confirmed in this pass: recoveries after invocation may be shared with the DLG provider under the contract, and the DLG must run for at least the longest loan tenor in the portfolio.

### 3.16 Penal charges, Fair Practices Code and interest-rate policy

- **Penal charges** (circular of 18 Aug 2023, DoR.MCS.REC.28/01.01.001/2023-24):
  - No penal _interest_, only reasonable penal _charges_, which are not capitalised and are disclosed in the KFS.
  - Start date was moved from 1 Jan 2024 to **1 Apr 2024** by the circular of 29 Dec 2023 ([RBI extension circular](https://website.rbi.org.in/documents/87730/39710850/Final+Circular+-+Penal+Charges+in+Loan+Accounts+-+Extension+-+29122023.pdf); [RBI FAQ](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/FAQView.aspx?Id=162)).
  - Now consolidated in the RBC Directions.
- **Fair Practices Code and interest-rate model.** Both must be board-approved, with rates annualised and published. They are now in the RBC Directions and may be replaced by the draft Interest Rates Directions, 2026.

## 4. Payday and very short-tenor unsecured loans

- **RBI has no separate "payday" category and no minimum tenor.** The digital lending rules apply to any tenor, and the KFS validity rule even contemplates loans under 7 days.
- **Draft APR cap.** The **August 2026 draft Interest Rate Directions propose capping APR on small-value loans (≤ ₹50,000)**. If finalised, likely from 1 Apr 2027, this undermines high-APR payday pricing. The cap level is unverified.
- **Supervisory concern.** The June 2026 Financial Stability Report flagged fintech-originated small-ticket personal loans:
  - 90–179-day delinquency of about 6.4% in March 2026;
  - growth of about 41.6%;
  - about 70.5% unsecured ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/news/financial-system-remains-resilient-on-strong-bank-nbfc-balance-sheets-rbi-126063000822_1.html), 30 Jun 2026; [PolicyCircle](https://www.policycircle.org/economy/rbi-financial-stability-report-loans/)). Figures are secondary.
- **Recovery rules.** The 2027 recovery framework tightens collections on exactly this kind of book.
- **Google Play bars it outright.** Its policy prohibits apps that promote personal loans **requiring full repayment in 60 days or less** ("short-term personal loans") ([Play Financial Services policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9876821?hl=en)). As written, the payday product cannot be listed on Play.

## 5. What the 31 Jul 2026 supervisory directions mean for Udofin

- **These bind lenders, not LSPs directly.** Partner NBFCs and banks will add clauses to their contracts with Udofin covering:
  - board-approved IT security standards;
  - RBI and lender audit and access rights;
  - retention of administrative audit logs;
  - vulnerability assessment and penetration testing evidence;
  - business continuity and disaster recovery;
  - **notifying the lender of incidents fast enough for it to report on DAKSH within 6 hours.**
- **Fraud risk directions.** These will also require lenders to monitor early-warning signals at their LSP partners.
- **Commercial opportunity.** Finbyx and LawDocsComply should map these instruments to controls, since partner lenders will ask for evidence of them.

## Challenges to the owner's plan

1. DLG does not create a collections right. RBI's DLG rules give the DLG provider no right or duty to collect. Collecting is a separate function the lender outsources. Udofin may collect only as the lender's appointed recovery agent under an outsourcing contract. From 1 Jan 2027 the recovery Amendment Directions of 6 Aug 2026 apply: IIBF-certified agents, contact only 8am-7pm, recorded calls kept 6 months, advance notice to the borrower, and the lender publishing its agencies. Being both the loss absorber and the collector also creates a conflict of interest that pushes toward aggressive collection. Recommendation: put collections in a separate, policy-governed module run under each lender's recovery policy, with complaint monitoring, and treat it as an optional service, not a consequence of giving DLG.
2. '5% FLDG to NBFCs' is under-specified. The 5% cap is per lender and per earmarked portfolio (the 'DLG set'), measured on the amount disbursed. Cover can only be cash with the lender, a fixed deposit with lien in the lender's favour, or a bank guarantee. It must be invoked within 120 days overdue, is not reinstated, and cannot cover revolving credit. Udofin must be incorporated under the Companies Act 2013, sign a contract that meets the lender's board-approved DLG policy, and publish on its website the number of portfolios covered and the amount of each. For NBFCs using ECL, the DLG must be integral to the loan contract to count in provisioning (13 Feb 2026 amendment). Recommendation: build a DLG ledger per lender and portfolio with automatic cap checks and an invocation tracker, and model Udofin's capital at risk across several lenders. Get legal review on whether large guarantee exposure affects Udofin's own regulatory characterisation.
3. Razorpay, Stripe and PayPal for all products. Repayments must go directly into the lender's account and disbursements directly into the borrower's account, with no LSP pass-through or pool account. Udofin cannot be the merchant of record for loan repayments, and cannot hold or route loan money through its own gateway accounts. Recommendation: eNACH/UPI AutoPay mandates and payment links issued under each lender's own merchant or utility credentials, settling to the lender's account, with Udofin having read-only reconciliation. Udofin's own gateway account should be used only for Udofin's B2B fees. Stripe and PayPal are poor fits for domestic Indian loan collections; this is unverified here, so check in the payments research.
4. Udofin's own CIBIL 'soft pull' through Surepass. The rules limit an LSP to storing minimal data, so bureau reports belong with the lender. Bureau access is governed by CICRA 2005: only credit institutions and specified users who are members may draw reports, so a reseller route is a red flag (interpretation; not researched in depth here). Recommendation: each lender pulls the bureau report with the borrower's explicit consent through its own membership. Udofin receives only the eligibility or offer result, or uses a consented Account Aggregator flow where a regulated lender is the FIU.
5. Aadhaar OTP, CKYC and DigiLocker run 'by Udofin'. KYC is the lender's obligation. Udofin is not an AUA/KUA and must not store Aadhaar numbers, biometrics or full KYC documents beyond minimal data. Recommendation: run KYC through the lender's KUA and CKYC credentials, or as the lender's agent with documents stored in the lender's tenant or vault, not in Udofin's database.
6. Device permissions as planned (location, OTP reading, camera scanning). RBI bars DLAs from files and media, contacts, call logs and telephony, and allows camera, microphone and location only once, for KYC, with explicit consent. Google Play additionally bars ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION, READ_MEDIA_IMAGES/VIDEOS, READ_CONTACTS, READ_PHONE_NUMBERS, storage permissions and QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES for personal-loan apps. Recommendation: OTP autofill through the Android SMS Retriever or SMS User Consent API (no SMS permission); document capture through a one-time camera session plus the system photo picker; coarse location, if any, used once at KYC; a permission-lint step in CI for the Expo config and AndroidManifest.
7. Personal 'books of accounts' / expense view. This needs transaction data. Reading SMS or files is barred, and the LSP may store only minimal data. Using loan data for a different purpose breaks purpose limitation and DPDP consent. Recommendation: if the feature is kept, make it a separate opt-in product with its own consent, sourced from Account Aggregator through a regulated FIU, stored separately from lending data, never used for lending without separate consent, and possible to delete.
8. Marketing push notifications. From 1 Jan 2027 the Responsible Business Conduct (Second Amendment) Directions of 15 Jun 2026 require explicit marketing consent separately for each product, ban dark patterns and bundling, and require interfaces to be user-tested and audited. Loan servicing cannot depend on agreeing to marketing. Recommendation: keep transactional and marketing notification channels separate, record consent per purpose, make opting out one tap, and keep personal data out of push payloads, since Expo/FCM/APNs route data outside India.
9. AI agent answering borrower queries. The grievance route must reach a named human nodal grievance officer at both Udofin and the lender. Ombudsman rights after 30 days must be shown, and nothing the bot says may be misleading. An LLM API hosted outside India would process borrower personal data abroad, which conflicts with India-only storage unless the data is deleted and brought back within 24 hours. Recommendation: limit the bot to FAQ and servicing information, offer a 'raise a complaint' handoff to a human at every turn, log transcripts as records, strip personal data before LLM calls or use an India-hosted model, and have each lender approve the content.
10. Escalating unresolved grievances to Equator (a group-owned ADR platform). The statutory path is complaint to the lender, then the lender's or LSP's grievance officer, then RB-IOS 2026 if unresolved after 30 days. ADR cannot replace, precede or delay that path. Steering borrowers to a related-party platform is a conflict of interest, and pairing it with 'resolution finance' for Equator awards compounds the conflict. Recommendation: present Equator only as an optional, clearly disclosed alternative after or alongside the RB-IOS information; keep ADR clauses out of standard loan terms unless the lender adopts them; disclose the group relationship; and wall off Equator data.
11. Multi-lender comparison with Udofin's commercial incentives. Every matching offer must be shown and unmatched lenders named, using a documented and consistent matching method. Default ordering cannot be driven by Udofin's fee, there can be no dark patterns, and each lender pays Udofin's fees (the borrower does not). Recommendation: publish the matching and sorting rules, sort by APR or let the borrower choose the order, add no 'recommended' badges driven by fees, log every display for audit, and pass borrower data to a lender only after the borrower consents to that lender.
12. eSign + eStamp 'in Udofin'. The loan agreement and KFS are the lender's documents. Udofin may run the signing workflow as the lender's agent, but the KFS must come from the lender's own pricing, be shown before execution, and be acknowledged. Signed documents must go to the borrower and be stored by the lender. Recommendation: generate documents from each lender's templates, keep versioned and hashed copies, and block eSign until the KFS is acknowledged and the cooling-off terms are shown.
13. PAYDAY loans. Google Play bars apps promoting personal loans repayable in full in 60 days or less. RBI's August 2026 draft Interest Rate Directions would cap APR on loans of ₹50,000 or less. The June 2026 FSR flags rising delinquency in fintech small-ticket loans, and the 2027 recovery rules tighten collections. Recommendation: drop payday loans; if a small-ticket product is needed, use tenors over 60 days with APR well within the expected cap.
14. Credit lines or flexi products through NBFC partners. The August 2026 draft would limit most NBFCs to term loans, and DLG cannot cover revolving credit. Recommendation: build every NBFC product as a term loan, and offer revolving or line products only through bank or credit-card-authorised partners.
15. Hosting: Supabase in Mumbai is fine, but the Node.js backend on Railway and any foreign SaaS tools (LLM, analytics, push, error tracking) will process borrower data outside India. RBI requires India-only storage, with deletion and return within 24 hours if data is processed abroad. My understanding is that Railway has no India region (unverified). Recommendation: run every service that touches borrower data in AWS ap-south-1 (or another India region) from launch, keep a vendor and data-flow register, and scrub personal data from telemetry.
16. Reading '31 July 2026' as a digital lending update. The 31 Jul 2026 instruments are the Department of Supervision consolidation (64 directions, including cybersecurity, fraud risk and internal audit); they do not amend digital lending. The live digital lending rules are in the 28 Nov 2025 Credit Facilities Directions for each lender type, as amended in 2026. Recommendation: LawDocsComply and the Udofin rulebook should track those instruments, plus the RBC Second and Third Amendments (effective 1 Jan 2027), RB-IOS 2026, the weekly bureau reporting, and the pending drafts.
17. Launch sequencing. Google Play requires the app to appear in RBI's DLA directory, which is filled from lenders' CIMS reports. The app cannot go live on Play until a partner lender has reported it on CIMS and its Chief Compliance Officer has certified compliance. Recommendation: put CIMS reporting and CCO sign-off on the go-live checklist with each lender, and build a 'partner lenders' page so the app's description matches the lenders' reports.

# Phase 08a independent review: what was found and what became of it

Written 19 October 2026 for the product owner. **Phase 08a has not been approved by the owner.** This record says what two independent reviews found, what the fix wave did about each finding and what is still open. It does not say that Phase 08a is correct, safe or compliant, and it is not evidence for any regulatory control.

The decisions that came out of the review are in [`docs/product/open-decisions.md`](../../product/open-decisions.md) (D-111 to D-150, and corrections to D-84 to D-91, D-100, D-107 and D-110). The fix notes this record is built from are the `fixes-*.md` files in this folder.

## 1. The short version

- Two independent reviews of the Phase 08a code produced **165 findings**: 15 high, 67 medium and 83 low. The first review looked at the application layer (59 findings, A0 to A58). The second looked at the database migrations (47 findings, M0 to M46, and 59 more, U0 to U58, that the skeptic checks did not reach because of usage limits).
- A fix wave followed. Reading the fix notes, **151 findings are recorded as fixed** (9 of those by adding missing tests only), **4 partly fixed**, **8 are documentation corrections** assigned to the documentation builders, and **2 are not recorded in any fix note** (A12 and U43; both listed in section 5).
- Many fixes are not final answers to a business question. They are the safest behaviour the builders could choose. Each such choice is a numbered row in the decision register (D-111 to D-150) so that you can confirm, change or reject it.
- A review that finds defects does not prove that no defects remain. See section 5.

## 2. How the reviews were done

**Application-layer review** (run identifier wf_7f01577f-a93). Ten parts of the code were examined: the borrower API, the lender servicing API, the lender payments and reminders API, the accounts API, the background workers, the provider callback receiver, the two portals, the mobile app, the honesty of the documents, and the seams between modules. Every candidate finding was handed to two independent skeptics whose job was to try to refute it. The 59 findings that survived both skeptics are A0 to A58.

**Migration review** (run identifier wf_d2b9177e-711). Nine parts of the database layer were examined: accounts and activation, servicing, payments, reminders, mandates, reconciliation, the borrower reads and hand-off links, privacy and security. The reviewers' candidate findings were again tried by skeptics.

- **M0 to M46 (47 findings)** survived at least one skeptic, most survived two.
- **U0 to U58 (59 findings)** were reported by a reviewer but the skeptic checks were cut off by usage limits, so they reached the fix builders marked **unverified**. The builders were told to verify each one themselves before fixing it, and to decline it with evidence if they could not reproduce or trace it. The fix notes record no U finding as not real. Three were reproduced on the unfixed database before being fixed (U53 and U55 in the accounts note, U39 in the reminders note), and the fix notes name a test for each of the others.

In total that is 10 application parts plus 9 database parts: **19 review dimensions** (mandates appears in both migration lists and is counted once).

**What the fix builders did.** Each fix builder took one area, wrote a forward-only change (database migrations 20261018000920 to 20261018000926, TypeScript, portals, the mobile app), added tests that fail when the fix is removed, and wrote a note of what changed, what the owner must decide and what risks remain. Those notes are the `fixes-*.md` files and they are the source of every outcome in section 6.

**What this record's author did.** I read every fix note and the two findings files. For the fifteen high findings I also checked that the identifiers the notes name exist in the migrations at the base commit (for example the hold columns in migration 924, the unique settlement reference in 922, the in-query notice filters in 921, the link rule in 920 and the file-import connection branch in 924). I did not run the test suites. Final test totals are not stated here: [[COUNTS]].

## 4. The most serious findings, in plain words

Fifteen findings were rated high. They fall into eight stories.

**1. Pre-debit notices could stop for every lender (A22).** The database picked the oldest 100 unsent notices and only then skipped the ones that could never go out. Nothing closed those dead notices, so 100 of them (for cancelled debits, or a lender whose mandates switch was off) would block every lender's notices for good, with no error. Fixed by SM and IX: every condition is now tested inside the query, notices that can never go out are closed as "not needed", and the worker keeps asking until it gets an empty answer. The test uses 120 dead notices and one live notice of another lender.

**2. The first activation switch could not be turned on (A42, U48).** `servicing_sync` required an active lender-system connection, and the only function that creates a connection required `servicing_sync` to be on. No lender could ever be activated, and the reminders, statements and mandates switches depend on it. Fixed by SA: a file-import or manual connection can be recorded while the switch is off (by a lender user with the sync-management permission and a second-factor session, at an active lender whose onboarding gate is activated); every other kind of connection and every other servicing write still waits for the switch. The whole order is now proved through the HTTP routes with two platform people (IX2, `lender-activation.integration.test.ts`).

**3. Three ways a payment could be mishandled (M22, M23, M24).** A second successful payment on the same order was not flagged when it arrived as "authorized" and then "captured" (M22). A payment with one failed attempt stayed "pending" for ever after its link expired and locked the borrower out of paying (M23). A genuine capture that arrived after an "expired" or "cancelled" update, which out-of-order delivery makes ordinary, was dropped with no exception (M24). Fixed by SP: a duplicate is flagged whenever a payment first reaches success, an intent whose link ended expires unless money is reported on it or an attempt is still in flight (the 24-hour hold is a chosen constant: D-117), and a capture is always recorded, with later contradictory updates becoming exceptions for a person.

**4. A "due today" reminder to a borrower already overdue (M33).** A reminder worded "your installment is due today" could be sent for an installment that was already a day late, with no penal-charge sentence, because only overdue wording carried that check. Fixed by SRM: such a message is suppressed once its day has passed, messages for later installments wait while any overdue amount exists (D-129), and overdue wording remains the only way to talk about an overdue installment and still needs the lender's approved sentence.

**5. A debit taken after the borrower had cancelled (M44).** A debit settled after the mandate was revoked, cancelled, expired or paused (or the loan was closed) was attached to its old planned debit and recorded with no exception. Fixed by SM: such a debit raises a critical exception to the lender, and planned debits and unsent notices are closed when a mandate ends (A52, M45).

**6. The platform's emergency stop could be undone by the lender (M0, U11, U49, and A13).** After the platform or the system suspended a payment configuration, the lender's own two people (the one who prepares and the one who approves) could resubmit and approve it again, with no platform person involved. Fixed by SA: a suspension now leaves a hold that the lender cannot lift; only a different platform person, with a reason and a second-factor session, can release it. IX2 added the routes and P2 the screens. Holds are lender-wide; who may lift them and what lenders see: D-133 and D-135.

**7. The evidence that Udofin holds no borrower money was weak (U12, U13, U14).** The reconciliation line meant to show money settled to a non-lender account added up only item amounts, so a batch with no or few items reported zero; a bank credit covering only part of a batch confirmed all of its payments; and one bank credit could confirm any number of batches that shared a reference. Fixed by SR: the line is computed from the batches, a batch whose items do not add up is never confirmed, and a bank credit confirms a batch only when exactly one batch carries its reference. SR also made "zero" count as evidence only when a settlement report exists and no batch lacks a verified destination (A44). What remains: the bank credits are the lender's own statements, not a bank feed, and the bank reference shape is unverified (D-122, D-124).

**8. A suspended configuration still handed out payment links (U27, and A0).** After a payment configuration was suspended, the borrower could still fetch the stored payment link and pay into a destination the platform had just declared invalid, and the callback for that payment would be discarded. Fixed by SP: one rule decides whether a link may be handed out (configuration active and valid, loan active, no money reported on the order, no cancellation request) and links are withdrawn at once. What remains: a payment already made through a link that was open at the provider is not recorded, and reconciliation is the only backstop (D-120).

## 5. What this record cannot tell you

- **Independent review is not proof of absence.** The reviewers read the code as it stood at commit 8463bb1. They worked from the code and a local database. No real payment provider, bank, lender system or device exists in this repository to test against. Every statement about provider behaviour, bank references and regulation remains unverified until the provider, the bank, the lender or counsel supplies the text.
- **The fixes are new code.** The fix wave added seven migrations (20261018000920 to 20261018000926) and changed TypeScript, portal and mobile code. No review of the fix wave itself is recorded in the fix notes. New defects are possible.
- **"Fixed" is what the fix notes say.** I read the notes and spot-checked the high findings against the code. I did not re-run the tests. [[COUNTS]]
- **Findings the fix notes do not settle, found while writing this record** (all listed in `docs-D3.md` for the lead):
  - **A12** (two people resolving the same exception at once write duplicate history, and two bank-credit uploads of the same bank reference race) is mentioned in no fix note and the code is unchanged.
  - **U43** (the stored penal-charge figure is not tied to the sentence the borrower sees) is mentioned in no fix note and the code is unchanged. It is raised as D-149.
  - **U30, mandate half** (a rail whose maximum is 0 is still offered to the borrower and then refused) is in no fix note; `payments.my_mandate_options` is unchanged and `private.rail_limits_ok` (redefined in 924 only for text screening) still accepts a maximum of 0.
  - **A25 and A55, database side**: only the worker stops a provider from lengthening an ordinary payment link. `payments.record_order_created` still accepts any expiry up to 31 days (migration 920 line 600); it clamps only a foreclosure link to its quotation.
  - **A5**: the portal can say whether a row number is a file row or a record position, but cannot give the exact file row for a stored finding. D-140.
  - **A37**: the app now says up front that auto-pay is not available, but the cause (no device-integrity verifier, no attestation flow in the app) remains. D-111.
- **Risks the fix builders named and did not fix** are carried into the decision register rather than repeated here: D-114, D-115, D-120, D-126, D-127, D-130, D-133, D-135, D-145 and D-147.

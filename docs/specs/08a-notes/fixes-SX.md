## 3. The flows (what they cross, what they found)

Everything is done through public functions as the roles `authenticated` (people) and `service_role` (workers, the receiver). Fixtures that are not Phase 08a are owner inserts and labelled: the organisations, memberships and roles, the borrowers' accounts, the loan products and applications of earlier phases, the identity module's step-up and device-integrity rows, and the clock seams below. Two lenders (X and Y) go through the same activation, so every "nobody else sees it" check compares two lenders that both have data.

- **A.1 activation** (SA notes, section 5): connection with receipts capability, `servicing_sync`, `statements_noc`, reminders (policy and three wordings by one person, approved by another), the own-account list attested by a verifier, a registry entry, settlement account, merchant claim, configuration (maker, submitter, approver), `payments`, mandate settings, `mandates`; all five switches decided by a second platform person, never by the requester.
- **A.2 payment**: request, order, sealed link stored and fetched (not by another borrower), captured event, applied, stage words (`started`, `provider_received`, `lender_posted`, and the lender's `provider_captured`, `provider_settled`, `bank_confirmed`, `lender_posted`), settlement report, the lender's bank credit, two reconciliation runs, the receipt the lender posts; lender Y and the other borrower see none of it (payment tables, receipts, settlement batches, credits, runs, matches, summary, the upload).
- **A.3 suspension**: a platform person suspends the configuration in the middle of a payment: the link is withdrawn at once, a new request is refused (`collection_target_invalid`), a delivery for the suspended configuration is rejected at the door and only a receipt remains, the lender's maker cannot re-submit, cannot submit a new draft and the approver cannot retire (`platform_hold`), the person who suspended cannot release, a different person does, the lender's pair activates again, the event now reaches the database and the payment completes. Lender Y keeps working.
- **B mandate**: step-up required, request, hand-off, link, registered event, planner, notice claimed once and recorded, debit presented and settled (a settled debit keeps the quiet period), a second mandate whose notice is late from the start, the borrower's cancellation (no next debit, no notice, one cancellation queued), the sweep queues it again an hour later (the fixture states the passing of time by moving `cancel_enqueued_at` and `notice_due_at`), the worker marks it sent, the provider's confirmation ends the mandate and cancels the planned debit and closes the notice, a debit presented after the end is recorded as unplanned and raised critical to the lender; lender Y's mandate is untouched.
- **C reminders**: policy and wording from A.1, plan, claim only inside the window (the window is judged through the test clock seam that only the window check reads), report with the claim token (a wrong token is refused `not_claimed`), the borrower reads the lender's wording with the lender's figures, a hold on another lender's loan is refused, a hold placed and released resumes the messages still to come, an overdue message without the lender's approved penal-charge sentence is stopped (and the lender is told), the sentence drafted by one person and approved by another, a second loan reported overdue afterwards gets its message with the sentence verbatim, the stopped one stays stopped; the quiet period decided by the lender's receipts: a payment reversed by 0.01 keeps a loan quiet, one reversed in full does not.
- **D partial reversal** (section 2).

What the flows found: no defect in a function other than the five M9 readers and the items above. Observations, none changed:

1. After a platform suspension a pending payment can neither be paid (link withdrawn) nor cancelled by the borrower (`cancellable` is true only before the order exists); it lapses with its link period. SA already listed "suspension should also expire the configuration's created and pending intents".
2. A notice is "late" whenever the planner sees the debit inside the lead time; a notice is on time only if the planner ran before the notice was due. So tests must move time to claim a punctual notice.
3. `payments.reconciliation_summary` is security invoker: another lender's call returns no rows rather than an error.

Time of day: the flows give the same answers at any hour of the Indian day: a loan due today has its pre-due message due since yesterday (the due-day message is scheduled for 09:00 IST, so the check names the kind), the receipts' value dates are the India-time day of their own report, and the loans whose notice must be late or on time are due in two or three days. An earlier draft of the file passed at 04:18 to 06:00 IST and failed at 18:54 IST on exactly this; the corrected file was run at 18:54 IST. Its behaviour before 09:00 IST and in the first hours after India-time midnight is covered by reasoning (the clock cannot be moved here).

## 5. Owner decisions and choices made

1. **Net rule.** A receipt reversed by 0.01 is still posted, matched and counted in the quiet period, although its net is no longer the provider's amount; the reconciliation match stays on the gross amount. The alternative (match on the net) would make every partial reversal a `lender_receipt_missing`.
2. **Exception class for 2b.** `mandate_unresolved` is reused (warning, platform): the class list is closed, the portal word reads "A mandate outcome is unknown". A distinct class needs the list, the contract and the portal changed first. The waiting time is the existing `p_unresolved_after` (24 hours).
3. **"Cannot be refreshed" means** the mandate's own configuration is not active (suspended, retired) and the last event or lookup is older than the waiting time. A lender whose mandates switch is OFF is not covered: the worker's lookup list skips such a lender (SM risk), but its events still arrive.
4. **Evidence screen.** Values with nine or more digits are refused even when spaces or hyphens split them, so a date and time such as `2026-10-18 10:30` is refused in an evidence value (the same rule as every other free-text column of the accounts area). The three table checks that use the function are evaluated again when a row is updated: a row written under the weaker screen with evidence the new screen refuses cannot be updated until its evidence is replaced. Phase 08a is not deployed, so no such row exists; no data is rewritten.
5. **Rule v1.** Only a draft can be edited. A database whose `servicing.reminder_window.default` v1 was already approved needs a version 2 proposed by a platform person with the corrected text (an approved rule is immutable); the migration does not create one.
6. **`invalid_validity` stays out** of the refused-file list: it is a quotation problem, not a file error, as the TypeScript comment says.

## 6. Risks seen, not fixed

- `supabase/tests/029_p08a_fix_servicing.test.sql` (SS) failed once in four full runs (assertions 149, 152 and 154, counts of 2 instead of 1). Probable cause: two successive calls of its row builder `pg_temp.loan(...)` write a timestamp with one-second resolution, and a second boundary between them makes two different quarantine rows; the assertions also count outbox events of the whole database. It passed in the next run and in three standalone runs. Not my file; a SQL builder should compute the report time once.
- See observation 1 of section 3 (a pending payment under a suspended configuration).
- A first reconciliation of a lender's history sets every receipt's received time to the import (reminders: 48 hours quiet), as SRM noted.
- The sweep raises at most 200 new exceptions a run; a lender with more live mandates on a suspended configuration needs several runs.
- A run of the flows in the first hours after India-time midnight was not exercised (section 3).

## Owner decisions of 14 October 2026 (approval to build Phase 08a)

The owner approved this specification with the following decisions (recorded in `docs/product/open-decisions.md`, "Phase 08 owner decisions"). Where they differ from a recommendation in section 23, **these control.**

1. **Decomposition:** 08a core servicing, payments, mandates and reconciliation; 08b collections; 08c DLG. Only 08a is authorised. 08b and 08c are not built as active workflows and stay disabled for every lender.
2. **Events:** verified provider events and unverified webhook receipts are separate records; unverified receipts are never financial events; a forged callback cannot reserve a genuine event id; replay protection is database-backed; an event is verified only after authentication, integrity, correlation and business-validation checks.
3. **Account ownership:** fail-closed account fingerprint and ownership registry (owner, permitted purposes, verification status, effective dates, Udofin and group accounts identifiable); destinations that are unknown, unverified, inconsistent or prohibited are rejected or held; no account details are seeded; real payment activation stays blocked until verified details are supplied.
4. **LMS:** the lenders' LMS is not confirmed. Manual and file import first, a lender adapter interface for later REST, REST is never a dependency of 08a. Source lender, source system, import batch, source record id and reported timestamp are recorded; nothing is silently overwritten.
5. **Reminders:** in-app and push only. No SMS provider is integrated, and no mock SMS in staging or production. Window 08:00 to 19:00 IST unless stricter. Overdue reminders are suppressed when approved penal-charge text is missing.
6. **Primary documents:** supplied later by the owner; their future arrival is not evidence. Only the ten RBI texts named in section 3.1 are confirmed. A register of missing texts is kept (section 24.2). Nothing that depends on an unresolved legal, regulatory, provider or contractual prerequisite is activated.
7. **Collections and DLG:** disabled for every lender until gates are evidenced; a DLG arrangement does not make Udofin responsible for collection; contractual responsibilities, operational permissions, approvals and activation status are separate records.

8. **Account-number format (answer of 14 Oct 2026):** 9 to 18 digits is the initial range, configurable by country and account type (data, not code); a rule is `unverified` until its source is supplied; invalid formats are rejected under the applicable rule; unvalidated details are never fingerprinted.
9. **Unknown provider event kinds (answer of 14 Oct 2026):** an authentic event whose kind (or status) the platform does not support is stored as verified-and-ignored and changes no payment, mandate or settlement state; only failed verification goes to the unverified-receipts store.
10. **Import files with errors (answer of 14 Oct 2026):** all or nothing in one database transaction; the whole file is validated first; row-level errors are shown and the file can be corrected and resubmitted; a failed attempt leaves an audit record (file hash, counts, row positions and error codes) and no row values.

Approval to implement Phase 08a is not approval to launch real payments: live activation additionally needs verified lender arrangements, settlement configuration, provider documentation and the legal and compliance gates in section 25.

## 1. Objective in plain English

After a loan is disbursed (Phase 07), the **lender's loan system** (its LMS) remains the only record of what the borrower owes. Phase 08 lets Udofin:

1. **Copy** what the lender's LMS reports (status, balances, schedule, receipts, charges) into append-only read models with the time the lender said it was true, and show it to the borrower as "as reported by <lender> at <time>". Udofin never calculates a balance, an interest figure, a penal charge or a foreclosure amount.
2. Let the borrower **pay the lender directly**: a payment link or one-time payment made under the **lender's own** payment-provider merchant account that settles into the **lender's own** bank account, and a repayment **mandate** (eNACH or UPI AutoPay) with the lender as the creditor. Udofin creates the instruction, receives signed provider callbacks, and records them. It never holds, routes or "settles" money.
3. **Prove** where every rupee went: provider events, provider settlement reports, the lender's bank credits and the lender's LMS receipts are compared daily; differences become an exception queue worked by named people. Nothing is marked settled because someone asked.
4. Send **service reminders** (before the due date, on it, and after it) in the borrower's language, with the **lender's own penal-charge text** in overdue reminders, inside contact windows, never marketing.
5. Provide the borrower with a **statement of account**, a **foreclosure quote** and a **no-dues / NOC** that the lender issues, and track the lender's release of any security (for example a mutual-fund lien) on closure.
6. Behind separate, per-lender switches that default to **off**, support Udofin acting as the lender's **recovery agent** (D-11) and keeping a **default loss guarantee ledger** (D-10), with the segregation ADR 0023 requires.

Udofin does not lend, decide credit, set or recompute terms, collect or hold money, own the loan record, or tell a borrower anything about a guarantee it gives to a lender.

## 2. Assumptions (to be confirmed in section 23)

1. First lenders are NBFCs (MGM Financiers, Megapode Vyapar; D-03 partly). Only the NBFC texts on file are used. Banks, UCBs and other lender types get **no** Phase 08 rules until their text is read (D-31).
2. No lender LMS API, no payment-provider contract, no mandate sponsor-bank arrangement, no SMS/DLT provider, no telephony provider and no production document store exist today. The build therefore ships **ports, labelled mocks, engines, database guards and gates**, and every production path stays blocked (section 25).
3. The lender is the merchant of record, the NACH "corporate"/UPI AutoPay merchant and the owner of the settlement account (ADR 0008). Udofin acts on the lender's credentials as technology service provider and holds no collection account.
4. `servicing.loan_accounts` is created from the lender's disbursement evidence (existing database rule: an application cannot become `disbursed` without it). Phase 07 explicitly leaves disbursement out, so the **only** path to a loan account in this phase is the LMS sync or a lender's manual entry, both labelled by `source_system`.
5. Collections and DLG go live only per lender, only after the owner and counsel gates in sections 13 and 14, and are built as separate sub-phases (08a core, 08b collections, 08c DLG) with separate pull requests (recommendation, Q1).
6. English and Hindi everywhere (D-15). Hindi legal or lender-authored text is shown only when approved; otherwise the approved English text is shown with a plain notice, as in Phase 07.
7. Money is `bigint` paise end to end. Lenders book whole rupees (RBC para 103); Udofin displays the lender's figures and accepts borrower-entered amounts in whole rupees.

## 4. Scope

**In scope (built and tested with mocks and fixtures):** per-lender activation switches; `LmsPort` and the sync model; loan-account creation from lender evidence; payment configuration with settlement-account and merchant validation; payment links and one-time payments (installment, overdue, part-prepayment, foreclosure) through a `PaymentProviderPort`; mandate lifecycle through a `MandateProviderPort`; the provider webhook receiver; event processing; reconciliation and exception queue; reminders; statement, foreclosure quote and NOC flows (lender-issued); closure tasks including security (lien) release prompts; borrower and lender screens; English and Hindi UI; observability hooks. **Gated sub-phases:** collections (08b) and DLG ledger (08c).

**Out of scope:** any real LMS, provider, sponsor bank, SMS/DLT or telephony integration (labelled mocks only); Udofin's own books and Tally/SAP exports and fee invoicing (Phase 10); disbursement; field-agent app (D-21); cash recovery (stays off); grievance workflow (Phase 09; this phase only deep-links and pauses contact); restructuring or settlement decisions (lender only); credit-bureau reporting (lender); AI features; marketing messages (D-14); any computation of APR, EMI, interest, penal charge, foreclosure amount or balance.

## 6. Servicing sync: `LmsPort` and the read-model (no competing ledger)

### 6.1 Port (`packages/adapters/src/lms.ts`, `ProviderCapability: 'lms'` already exists)

```ts
interface LmsPort {
  readonly descriptor: ProviderDescriptor; // key e.g. 'finbyx' | 'generic_rest' | 'mock_lms', mode 'mock'|'sandbox'|'production'
  readonly capabilities: {
    pull: boolean;
    push: boolean;
    foreclosureQuote: boolean;
    statement: boolean;
    closureDocuments: boolean;
    receipts: boolean;
  };
  listChanges(ctx: ProviderCallContext, cursor: string | null): Promise<LmsResult<LmsChangePage>>;
  fetchLoan(ctx, lmsLoanRef: string): Promise<LmsResult<LmsLoanReport>>;
  fetchSchedule(ctx, lmsLoanRef: string): Promise<LmsResult<LmsScheduleReport>>;
  fetchReceipts(
    ctx,
    lmsLoanRef: string,
    sinceCursor: string | null,
  ): Promise<LmsResult<LmsReceiptPage>>;
  requestForeclosureQuote(ctx, lmsLoanRef: string, asOfDate: string): Promise<LmsResult<LmsQuote>>;
  fetchStatement(
    ctx,
    lmsLoanRef: string,
    period: { from: string; to: string },
  ): Promise<LmsResult<LmsDocumentRef>>;
  fetchClosureDocuments(ctx, lmsLoanRef: string): Promise<LmsResult<LmsDocumentRef[]>>;
}
```

All amounts are `bigint` paise (decimal strings on the wire, parsed by one `parseMoney` helper that rejects floats, more than two decimals and exponent forms); dates are `YYYY-MM-DD`; the lender's `asOf` timestamp is carried on every report. A timeout is `unknown`, never an empty report (same rule as `LenderConnector`). Every call is idempotent or read-only; there is **no write port**: Udofin cannot post a receipt, change a schedule or close a loan in an LMS. Where the lender has no API, a lender-portal **manual import** (validated JSON/CSV) produces the same normalised reports with `source_system = 'manual_import'` and the importing user recorded.

### 6.2 Sync model

- **Modes** per lender (`servicing.lms_connections.kind`): `finbyx`, `generic_rest`, `file_import`, `manual`, `mock` (mock only where the session allows it, same guard pattern as `udofin.allow_mock_connectors`). No connector exists for any real LMS: Finbyx is blocked on D-04 (Mumbai) and the contract (D-26 disclosure); generic REST on that lender's specification (D-43).
- **Pull:** a worker job per lender walks `listChanges` from a stored cursor (every N minutes, D-71), fetches changed loans, and calls one database function `servicing.apply_lms_report(...)` per loan. **Push:** the existing callback-receiver process accepts the lender's signed message (scheme per lender, D-43), de-duplicates on `servicing.lms_inbound (lender_org_id, message_id)`, and enqueues the same function. Both are at-least-once; the function is idempotent on `(loan, content_sha256)` and on the lender's sequence number.
- **Loan account creation:** the first report for an application with disbursement evidence (date, amount, bank reference together) creates `servicing.loan_accounts` through the existing rules and lets the system move the application to `disbursed` (existing trigger). A report that references an unknown lender-side loan, a different lender's application or a mismatching borrower is stored as `quarantined` with a reason class and an exception for the lender; it creates nothing.
- **What is stored (append-only, reported as received):**
  - `servicing.balance_snapshots`: sequence, `reported_as_of`, `received_at`, status as reported, outstanding principal, overdue principal, overdue interest, penal charges, other charges, total overdue, next due date and amount, **days past due as reported by the lender**, `content_sha256`, `source`. The sum columns are what the lender sent; Udofin never adds them up for display except as an arithmetic equality check that flags (not fixes) a report whose parts do not equal its total.
  - `servicing.schedule_snapshots` (existing) gain `reported_as_of`, `content_sha256`, `source`; a new version supersedes the old, the old stays.
  - `servicing.lms_receipts`: each receipt the lender posted (lender receipt ref unique per lender, amount, value date, mode, bank reference such as UTR, allocation as reported, reversal rows referencing the receipt they reverse).
  - `servicing.loan_account_events` (remediation) records status/disbursement changes.
- **Regression and anomaly rules (flag, never correct):** an older sequence or an older `reported_as_of` is ignored and counted; a status moving backwards (for example `closed` to `active`) or an outstanding amount that rises without a reported charge is stored and flagged `sync_anomaly` for the lender; `closed`, `foreclosed`, `written_off` are terminal for Udofin features (payments and mandates stop) but a lender correction is still recorded.
- **Staleness:** every borrower and lender view carries `reported_as_of`; if the newest snapshot is older than the lender's threshold (default 24 hours, D-71) the app says so and payment buttons that depend on the amount (overdue, foreclosure) switch to "ask the lender / refresh" rather than showing an old figure; the sync-lag metric and alert fire (section 19).
- **Borrower wording:** every figure is labelled "as reported by <lender> on <date and time IST>". No balance, interest or "you owe" figure is derived by Udofin.

## 7. Payments: configuration, links and one-time payments

### 7.1 Per-lender payment configuration and settlement-account validation

New `payments.lender_payment_configs`: `lender_org_id`, `provider`, `provider_mode` (`mock` only where allowed, else `sandbox` or `production`), `merchant_ref` (the provider's merchant/account id), `settlement_account_id` (composite FK to `tenancy.lender_settlement_accounts`, **status must be `verified`**), `secret_ref` (the NAME of the secret in the secrets manager, never the secret), `webhook_secret_ref`, `endpoint_token_hash` (opaque token that routes inbound callbacks; the lender id never appears in a URL), `rail_limits` (from provider documentation with a source note), `status` (`draft` → `pending_approval` → `active` / `suspended` / `retired`), `drafted_by`, `approved_by` (a different person), evidence document (provider onboarding confirmation naming the lender as merchant), `contract_section_ref`. Approval is two-person (ADR 0025) and is blocked until validation passes. Validation is one database function, `payments.validate_collection_target(config_id)`, run on approval, on every intent creation (cheap re-check), and nightly (drift):

1. the organisation is `kind = 'lender'`, onboarding gates for payments met, and the settlement account belongs to **the same lender** and is `verified` (not `unverified`/`revoked`);
2. the settlement account carries an **account fingerprint** (keyed HMAC of the account number computed at registration under a platform key; the number itself is never stored) that is **unique across all organisations** and **not on `payments.udofin_own_account_fingerprints`** (accounts owned by Udofin or any group company, registered by platform administrators, including its fee-collection account): an account shared with, or owned by, anyone but the lender is refused;
3. `merchant_ref` is unique per provider across all organisations and its registered purpose is `repayment` for exactly one lender; Udofin's own merchant account (for LSP fee invoices) is a different record type that can never be referenced by a payment intent;
4. the provider mode is allowed for the environment (section 20) and the provider is on the platform's provider register with its RBI payment-aggregator authorisation evidence recorded (PAY-05), or is a pure mandate/orchestration provider with its own evidence;
5. after the first order/link is created, the provider's response is **bound back**: if it reports a different merchant or settlement profile than the configuration, the intent fails with `merchant_mismatch`, the configuration is suspended and a critical exception opens. Where a provider exposes the merchant settlement profile, a nightly read compares it with the configuration (capability is provider-specific and unverified).

This is the database-level answer to roadmap criterion "validation rejects a payment link or mandate whose settlement account or merchant ID is not the lender's". The API repeats the check (tenant isolation is enforced twice); pgTAP proves the database refuses even if the API is bypassed.

### 7.2 Intents, links and one-time payments

Existing `payments.payment_intents` stays the instruction record; additions: `config_id` (composite FK with the lender), `provider_mode`, `quote_id` (foreclosure and cooling-off exit), `target_installment_seq` (optional), `link_expires_at`, `settlement_account_id` becomes **not null** for new rows, free-text `settlement_account_ref` is no longer accepted for new rows. Creation:

- **Who:** only a database function `payments.request_payment(...)` called by the API for the authenticated borrower (idempotent on `Idempotency-Key`, one intent per key per lender). It checks: the borrower owns an `active` loan; the configuration is active and valid (7.1); the amount rule below; no other `created`/`pending` intent for the same installment and purpose unless the first is cancelled or expired; the lender's communication hold does not forbid it (a hold never blocks the borrower paying).
- **Amount rules (Udofin never decides the amount, it only accepts the lender's):** `installment`/`overdue`: exactly the latest reported next-due/total-overdue amount, or, if the lender enabled partial payment, an amount between the lender's minimum and that figure; `part_prepayment`: between the lender's minimum and the reported outstanding principal, and only if the lender enabled it; `foreclosure`/cooling-off exit: exactly the amount of an unexpired lender quote (section 12.2); `charges`: only for a charge the lender reported and that is on the KFS or has recorded consent (RBC 29(5)). Staleness blocks amount-dependent purposes (6.2).
- **Provider call:** after commit the outbox event `payment.create_order` makes the worker (system login, in `src/workers`) call `PaymentProviderPort.createOrder` with the lender's credentials and `operationKey = intent id`; the result stores `provider_order_ref` and moves the intent `created → pending` (existing transitions). A timeout is `outcome_unknown`: the worker looks the order up by receipt reference before any retry (never blind re-creation). The API waits briefly (about 3 seconds) for the order and otherwise returns the intent in `created` for the app to poll.
- **The borrower pays at the provider** (hosted checkout, UPI intent or a link opened in an in-app browser); Udofin never sees card, bank or UPI credentials (no PCI scope). A payment link is a bearer secret: it is not stored in clear, is not logged, and travels by SMS only if D-72 whitelists the sender domain.
- **Nothing the app says moves state.** The intent changes only through section 9. The app shows "payment started", then "payment received by the payment provider for <lender>" after a verified event, and "<lender> has posted it to your loan" only when the lender's receipt appears (D-70). It never shows "paid" from a redirect return.
- **Expiry and cancellation:** links expire per configuration; the system expires `created`/`pending` intents without a verified success after the provider's validity plus a grace period (status lookup first); a borrower may cancel only a `created` intent (existing rule).

### 7.3 Partial payments, over-payments, duplicates and reversals

- A partial payment is simply an intent for a smaller, lender-permitted amount; **allocation to principal, interest or charges is the lender's** (shown after the lender posts it).
- A provider capture whose amount differs from the intent never makes the intent `succeeded`; the event is stored, the intent stays `pending`, and an `amount_mismatch` exception opens for the lender (the borrower may have paid money that now sits with the lender).
- A second capture on the same order/link (customer paid twice) is a separate `provider_payments` row and a `duplicate_payment` exception; Udofin cannot refund (it holds no money): the lender or provider refunds, and the refund event is recorded.
- Refunds, chargebacks and reversals are **compensating rows** in `payments.payment_adjustments` (append-only, positive amounts, kind `refund | chargeback | reversal | correction`, referencing the provider payment and the source event). An intent's amount, parties and `succeeded` status are never edited; "net received" is a view for reconciliation only and is not a loan balance. A lender-posted receipt reversal in the LMS is likewise a reversal row in `servicing.lms_receipts`.

## 8. Mandates (eNACH / API mandate and UPI AutoPay), lender as creditor

### 8.1 Rails and policy

Rails supported by the port: `enach_api` (NPCI API e-mandate through net banking or debit card, with the lender as the NACH corporate via its sponsor bank) and `upi_autopay` (lender as merchant). Physical NACH is out of scope. Whether a mandate is required before disbursal, required before the first due date, or optional is a **lender setting** (D-67); if required before disbursal, the mandate step is pulled into the Phase 07 journey as the roadmap says. Prior owner decisions apply: creating a mandate needs the tiered device-integrity result and a **step-up code bound to the mandate** (D-24, step-up action `mandate_creation` already exists), and the bank account used must be the account verified in Phase 07 (the mandate stores a reference and the masked account, not the number).

### 8.2 Lifecycle (state machine in the database and `packages/domain`, with a drift test)

`draft → registration_pending → active` (success), or `rejected` / `failed` / `expired` / `requires_reconciliation` (provider outcome unknown); `active ↔ paused` (provider/lender action); `active → cancelled` (borrower or loan closure) or `revoked` (borrower revoked at the bank, learned by verified event or status lookup); `active → expired` at the end date. Rules:

- Only the system moves a mandate after `draft`, and only on a **verified** provider event or a provider status lookup; a redirect back to the app proves nothing (the same discipline as payments).
- The creditor is the lender: `payments.mandates` references the active payment configuration and stores the lender's utility/merchant reference from it; creation is refused if the rail's creditor identifier is not the lender's (7.1, PAY-07).
- Maximum debit amount is set by lender policy and the rail limit stored in configuration (copied from provider documentation; not hard-coded). **Interpretation:** if a lender's instalment exceeds the rail's no-AFA debit limit, a UPI AutoPay mandate would need authorisation on each debit; the configuration therefore marks such rails `needs_afa_each_debit` and the app steers to `enach_api` or tells the borrower a bank prompt will appear. Udofin never tries to avoid an AFA.
- **Debits:** `payments.mandate_debits` has one row per instalment per mandate (unique on mandate, schedule snapshot and sequence): scheduled amount (equal to the **lender-reported** instalment/overdue figure, never above the mandate maximum, never invented), presentment date, provider debit ref, status `scheduled → notified → presented → settled | returned | cancelled | unknown`, and the provider's **return/reject code stored verbatim** with a class mapped through a configuration table maintained from the sponsor bank/provider (no hard-coded code list). Who initiates a debit (lender/sponsor-bank schedule or Udofin presenting on the lender's reported schedule) is lender configuration (D-66). Retries and re-presentation follow the rail's rules as configured and are visible to the lender; Udofin adds no retry of its own beyond what the configuration allows, and a returned debit never creates a Udofin charge. Any bounce or late charge is the lender's penal charge (RBC 30; the text on file does not mention bounce charges) and appears only as lender-reported.
- **Pre-debit notice:** the issuer's own 24-hour notice is reported in secondary sources for UPI (3.2). Udofin additionally sends a service message (section 11) before each debit and logs it in `payments.pre_debit_notices` (planned time, sent time, delivery evidence); a debit scheduled sooner than the configured lead time is flagged to the lender, not silently presented. The lead time is a configuration value (default 48 hours, D-66).
- **Cancellation:** the borrower can cancel in the app (request to the provider, state `cancelled` only on confirmation); closure of the loan cancels the mandate by a job that verifies the cancellation and raises an exception if the provider does not confirm. A changed verified bank account (Phase 07) cancels and requires a new mandate with step-up.
- **History:** `payments.mandate_events` append-only with the verified source event; the status column is only a cache of the last event.

## 9. Provider webhook endpoint

Runs in the **separate callback-receiver process** (own database login, signature check before any database work, as `workers/callbacks-main.ts` does for lenders), not in the public API. One route family: `POST /v1/hooks/provider/{endpointToken}`. The worker (system login) applies events; the receiver never marks anything successful.

1. **Routing without enumeration:** the token resolves to one `lender_payment_configs` row through its hash. Unknown, suspended or wrong-provider tokens, bad content-type, oversize bodies and unsigned requests all receive the same fixed response shape and status family (lesson of audit F-03: no distinguishable answer per lender).
2. **Verification before storage:** the adapter's `WebhookVerifier` checks the signature over the **raw body** with the lender's webhook secret (constant-time compare), a timestamp tolerance (default 5 minutes) and, where the provider documents it, the source network. A scheme is implemented only from the provider's written specification; until then the route answers `503 provider_scheme_not_configured` for that provider (same rule as `api` lender connectors, D-43). The labelled mock scheme (`mock-standard-webhooks-v1`) exists for tests only.
3. **Replay protection stored in the database:** `private.mark_webhook_seen(source = provider key, message id)` (from the remediation migration) records the delivery id; a repeat is acknowledged `200` without reprocessing. This is separate from, and in addition to, `unique (provider, provider_event_id)` on `payments.provider_events`.
4. **Every delivery leaves a receipt, verified or not:** new append-only `payments.webhook_receipts` (provider, config id when known, payload SHA-256, received_at, `outcome` in `verified | duplicate | unverified | stale_timestamp | unmatched | invalid`, reason class). **Raw bodies are not stored** (they can carry personal data). An unverified delivery writes only a receipt: it never creates a `provider_events` row, never consumes an event id, never changes a payment, mandate or reminder, and raises a metric and alert beyond a threshold. This closes the event-id squatting gap in section 3.3; if the owner prefers keeping unverified rows in `provider_events`, its unique key must instead become partial on `signature_verified` (migration choice recorded at implementation).
5. **Normalise, then enqueue:** a verified delivery is normalised (`NormalisedProviderEvent`, status mapped onto the platform vocabulary, amount parsed to paise) and inserted into `payments.provider_events` (verified), then an outbox event `payment.apply_event` is enqueued in the same transaction. The receiver answers `200` after commit. Unknown event types are stored verified-and-ignored.
6. **Apply (worker, idempotent, one intent at a time under a row lock):** match the event to an intent (or mandate/debit) by provider order/mandate reference **and** lender; unmatched events become exceptions (`settlement_no_intent`-style classes) and change nothing. Outcome is recorded in new append-only `payments.event_processing` (event id, `applied | already_applied | ignored_stale | deferred | exception`, reason). Rules:
   - A captured payment is recorded in `payments.provider_payments`, then the intent is moved to `succeeded` **only** by the existing database guard: one verified event, same intent, lender and provider, status in `private.payment_success_statuses()` (`captured`, `paid`, `succeeded`), and amount equal to the intent's. Adapters map the provider's vocabulary onto these statuses; anything else (`authorized`, `pending`, `failed`, `refunded`) can never authorise success.
   - **Out of order:** events carry a monotonic rank per object (created < authorised < captured < refunded/disputed). A lower rank after a higher is `ignored_stale`; a refund or dispute that arrives before its capture is `deferred` and retried for a bounded time, then becomes an exception. A `failed` event after `succeeded` never reverts the intent (terminal); it opens a reconciliation exception.
   - **Concurrent duplicates:** serialised by the intent row lock plus the unique keys; the loser gets `already_applied`.
   - Mandate events and debit events go through the same pipeline into `payments.mandate_events` / `payments.mandate_debits`.
7. **Unverified or lost callbacks are hints:** a `pending` intent or `registration_pending` mandate older than a threshold triggers a **status lookup** through the port; a lookup result is evidence of the same standing as a verified event only if the adapter authenticated the call to the provider (recorded with `signature_verified` semantics = "provider-authenticated response"), never a borrower or lender claim.
8. Receiver limits: per-IP and per-token rate limits (behind the load balancer, which needs the trusted-proxy setting from audit R-02), maximum body size, no CORS, no cookies, no logging of bodies or headers beyond ids.

## 10. Reconciliation

Purpose: show, for each lender and day, that provider-confirmed payments, provider settlements, the lender's bank credits and the lender's LMS receipts agree, and that **no money settled anywhere but the lender's account**.

- **Inputs (all read-only, all lender-scoped):** (1) Udofin's verified provider events/payments and mandate debits; (2) provider settlement reports through `PaymentProviderPort.fetchSettlements(date)` or an imported file (batch id, UTR, period, gross/fee/tax/net, items by provider payment ref); (3) lender LMS receipts (section 6); (4) optionally the lender's bank statement, uploaded by the lender (CSV/MT940 parsed to value date, amount and UTR only; narrations are not stored because they may contain names). Which of (2) to (4) exist per lender is a capability flag; missing inputs mean "not reconcilable", never "reconciled" (D-69).
- **Matching is exact, never fuzzy:** provider payment ref ↔ settlement item; settlement UTR ↔ bank credit; lender receipt ref or bank reference ↔ provider payment ref (needs the lender to carry Udofin's intent id or the provider reference in its receipt; a dependency to agree per lender, D-69). Amounts must be equal to the paisa. Tolerances are zero by default; a timing tolerance (default settlement lag window per provider) is configuration.
- **Tables:** `payments.settlement_batches`, `payments.settlement_items`, `payments.bank_credits`, `payments.reconciliation_runs` (period, lender, input hashes, counts, status), `payments.reconciliation_matches`, `payments.reconciliation_exceptions` with append-only `…_exception_events` (acknowledged, assigned, resolved with a note and the person, reopened). Exceptions are never deleted; resolution is an annotation, and anything needing money to move is the lender's or provider's action.
- **Exception classes:** `provider_success_no_settlement`, `settlement_no_intent`, `amount_mismatch`, `duplicate_payment`, `lender_receipt_missing` (payment captured but not posted after the configured hours), `lender_receipt_no_intent`, `refund_unmatched`, `settlement_account_mismatch` (**critical**: a settlement item or merchant profile whose destination is not the lender's verified account), `merchant_mismatch`, `late_settlement`, `stale_sync`, `mandate_debit_unresolved`, `webhook_gap` (intent pending beyond threshold with no event after a status lookup). Each has a severity, an owner (lender vs platform) and an age; ageing drives alerts.
- **Settlement states are derived, not set:** `provider_captured` (verified event), `provider_settled` (settlement report item), `bank_confirmed` (matched bank credit), `lender_posted` (matched LMS receipt). They are computed in a view from the tables above. **No API or user action sets any of them**; "settled" cannot be marked on a request alone.
- **Schedule and evidence:** a daily job at a fixed IST time (after provider cut-off, configurable per provider) runs per lender, idempotent per `(lender, date)`; a deterministic report is stored with its input hashes so a rerun on unchanged inputs gives byte-identical output. A standing report line "settled to non-lender accounts: 0 items, ₹0" is the evidence for the roadmap gate "Udofin holds no borrower funds".
- **Lag monitoring:** metrics and alerts in section 19 (settlement lag, unposted-receipt age, pending-intent age, sync staleness, exception ageing).

## 11. Reminders and communications (service messages only)

- **Policy (per lender, lender-approved, two-person):** offsets relative to each due date (default proposal T-3, T-1, due day; overdue D+1, D+3, D+7, then weekly until closure or lender hold; D-72), channels (in-app/push, SMS), borrower language, maximum messages per account per day and week, and quiet hours. **Service messages only** (D-14): no offers, cross-sell, referral or marketing content of any kind; the template editor refuses promotional vocabulary classes by a lender-approved allow-list of variables and a platform "no marketing" check, and the marketing-consent data model stays unused.
- **Templates:** versioned, lender-approved, per language, hash stored; variable set is closed: lender display name, due date, amount, link reference, penal-charge text, help contact. **No borrower name, phone, account number, PAN or Aadhaar in any message body, URL, log or event** (CLAUDE.md rule 3). Push payloads carry only an opaque id and a fixed title (PSH-01). SMS templates carry a DLT template id registered by whoever is the principal entity (D-72, D-02); until a provider and registration exist the SMS channel answers `not_configured` and is never reported as sent. Provider acceptance is not delivery; "delivered" is recorded only when a delivery report arrives.
- **Penal-charge text:** the lender supplies it as structured fields (amount or percentage, basis = overdue amount only, reason) plus the sentence it wants shown, approved with the template (D-73). Every **overdue** reminder includes it, because RBC 30(7) requires the applicable penal charges whenever a reminder concerns non-compliance. If a lender has no approved penal text, overdue reminders to that lender are **not sent** and an exception opens for the lender (a missing disclosure is worse than a missing reminder). Pre-due reminders do not need it under 30(7) (interpretation) but may include a pointer. Whenever the lender reports a penal charge levied, a levy notice with its reason can be sent (RBC 30(7), second limb); Udofin renders what the lender reports and never computes or adds a charge. Penal charges are shown as a separate flat/percentage charge on the overdue amount, never as a higher rate and never compounded (RBC 30(1)).
- **Windows (configuration, conservative default):** all borrower reminders 08:00 to 19:00 IST by default, matching the recovery-hours rule (RBC para 100) because the text does not say whether messages count and an overdue reminder is recovery-adjacent (our interpretation, counsel to confirm); lender policy may only narrow. TRAI promotional-hours rules are not relied on because these are service messages (3.2, unverified). A message outside the window is deferred to the next window, never dropped silently.
- **Idempotency and suppression:** the plan is generated per `(loan, installment seq, offset, channel)` with a unique key, so an at-least-once outbox cannot double-send. Suppress when: the lender reports the instalment paid; a verified payment for it succeeded in the last 48 hours (avoid dunning someone who has paid; configurable); the loan is closed; a **communication hold** exists (complaint pending, hardship, dispute or lender instruction; the app also pauses on a borrower's request and links to Phase 09 grievance). Holds never block the borrower from paying.
- **Language:** borrower preference (en/hi); an unapproved Hindi template falls back to the approved English with the fallback recorded.
- **Delivery evidence:** `servicing.reminder_deliveries` (attempt, channel, DLT template id, content hash, penal-text hash, provider acceptance, delivered/failed time and class). The lender can read the log for its borrowers.

## 12. Statement of account, foreclosure quote, NOC and closure

### 12.1 Statement of account

The lender issues it (CF 9(3) describes digitally signed account statements on the lender's letterhead; periodic statement frequency for fixed-rate loans is **not stated** in the text read). Preferred path: `LmsPort.fetchStatement` returns the lender-issued signed PDF; Udofin stores it as a lender-owned, hash-verified document and serves it through the authenticated retrieval path with `Cache-Control: no-store` and an access log entry (same discipline as Phase 07; **blocked in staging and production until the production document store exists**). Where the lender cannot issue through an API, a request appears in the lender portal queue and the lender uploads the signed file (upload validated by the shared upload-safety helper). Until a lender-issued statement exists, the app shows **"Account activity"** built from lender-reported receipts and snapshots, clearly labelled "not a statement of account". Udofin never presents its own rendering as the statement.

### 12.2 Foreclosure quote (and cooling-off exit)

Udofin asks the lender's LMS (`requestForeclosureQuote`) for the amount payable on a date; the answer is stored append-only in `servicing.foreclosure_quotes` (lender quote ref, amount, components as reported including any pre-payment charge, `as_of`, `valid_until`, hash). Rules: Udofin never computes or adjusts a quote; a quote is shown with its validity and "as quoted by <lender>"; an intent for `foreclosure` must equal an unexpired quote (7.2); a payment that arrives after expiry is an exception for the lender to decide. **Advisory flag (interpretation, not a block):** if a quote includes a pre-payment charge on a loan the KFS data marks as floating-rate, individual and non-business, a flag goes to the lender for review (RBC 33 to 34). Cooling-off exit (CF para 11; amount per the lender's configuration from Phase 07) uses the same quote type with the borrower's exit option stated in plain language.

### 12.3 Closure, NOC and security release

When a verified lender report moves a loan to `closed` or `foreclosed`: (1) reminders stop and mandates are cancelled and the cancellation verified; (2) a `servicing.closure_records` row opens with the NOC due date computed from the **platform SLA default** (D-74; labelled as a platform setting, not a regulatory deadline) and, for secured products, the **30-day** security-release period of RBC paras 35 to 36 (confirmed in text for the cases it covers); (3) the lender issues the **no-dues / NOC / closure letter** (API fetch or portal upload) and it appears in the app; (4) for each collateral type a `servicing.security_release_tasks` row is created: **Udofin holds no collateral and has no depository or RTA access**; the lender (lien holder or pledgee) releases it, and the lender portal task shows the due date. The task moves to `confirmed_by_lender` only on the lender's confirmation. Delay reasons are lender-supplied text shown to the borrower; the ₹5,000-a-day exposure (RBC paras 39 to 41) is the lender's and is noted in the lender portal for lateness (and as an indemnity risk if Udofin's process caused the delay).
**LAMF:** `loan_against_securities` is `feasibility_review` and not enabled, so no LAMF loan can exist; the closure prompt is built generically for collateral type `mutual_fund_lien` (plus demat pledge) and exercised in tests with a test-only fixture product flagged with that collateral type. The borrower sees "<Lender> will release the lien on your units; status: <lender-reported>", never a claim that Udofin released anything. This satisfies the roadmap criterion "closing a LAMF loan triggers the lien-release prompt" without enabling the category.

## 13. Optional module 08b: collections (recovery agent), gated by D-11

**Owner decision (13 Oct 2026):** yes, Udofin acts as recovery agent, and other agencies may be used. ADR 0023 and `docs/architecture/collections-and-dlg.md` already fix the design; this section adds only what changed since and what blocks go-live. **Everything below is built behind `collections` per lender, default off.**

- **Gates before it can be switched on for a lender** (each recorded as evidence, checked by the database): (a) a signed recovery-agent mandate from the lender naming Udofin and any sub-agency (with the lender's consent for sub-agencies, outsourcing directions); (b) the agent register complete for every collector (police verification date and re-verification due date, signed undertaking date, training record, ID-card reference; **IIBF certificate reference field present but its necessity is unverified** until the 6 Aug 2026 amendment text is read); (c) Udofin's written collection code and the lender's recovery policy referenced; (d) notice and authorisation-letter templates approved by the lender, containing both parties' contact details (RBC paras 95 to 96); (e) the lender's recovery-grievance route printed in the disbursal pack (RBC para 92; Phase 07 pack item); (f) **counsel's review of collections conduct** and the amendment text read (D-76); (g) the call-recording and telephony decision if any voice contact is wanted (D-78).
- **Hard database limits** (a lender policy can only narrow): contact only between 08:00 and 19:00 IST (09:00 to 18:00 for any loan flagged microfinance), applied to every outbound channel (our conservative reading), checked **both when a contact is scheduled and when it is sent**; `collections.record_contact` takes the time from `now()` (only the system may supply a scheduled time) so the roadmap test "a contact attempt at 19:01 IST is blocked" is a database test; a contact is refused until the assignment's notice is **delivered** (delivery evidence, not provider acceptance), and again after a change of agent; maximum attempts per account per day and week from lender policy (the text sets no number; the chosen numbers are recorded); contact only the borrower (no referees, family or employers), no masked identity, no threats or false statements (scripts are versioned and lender-approved); every attempt is an append-only log row with script version and outcome; hardship and complaint holds pause contact.
- **Money:** collections creates payment links through section 7 in the lender's name only; there is no field anywhere for a borrower-facing collection fee (CF 10(4)); cash receipts stay disabled. Settlement and restructuring requests are forwarded to the lender, which alone decides.
- **Conflict-of-interest controls (ADR 0023):** one login cannot hold `collections.work` and `dlg.manage` (existing database rule); collections staff cannot read DLG tables or per-loan invocation status; DLG status never appears in borrower communications; no incentive field tied to amounts recovered or penal charges collected (RBC 30(4) Note (ii) suggests penal charges are not a revenue tool; D-77); a lender kill switch per case and per portfolio; quarterly conduct report per lender.
- **Not built until the owner decides:** call recording and retention (D-78), commission rules (D-77), voice telephony and 1600-series numbering (3.2).

## 18. Security and privacy

- Tenant isolation twice (API permission checks and forced RLS with composite tenant keys); every new table has cross-tenant and cross-borrower negative tests. The webhook receiver and workers use their own logins; the public API never holds the system login (existing boundary test extended to the new modules).

Account numbers are never stored (masked reference plus keyed fingerprint only); provider raw bodies and bank-statement narrations are not stored; payment links are bearer secrets. - Secrets are referenced by name (`secret_ref`) and live in the secrets manager; none in rows, repo, `NEXT_PUBLIC_*`/`EXPO_PUBLIC_*` or responses. Mobile holds no privileged key.
- Input validation on the server for every field; amounts as decimal strings parsed to `bigint`; no `Number()` on money (audit R-07); no floats in the payments or servicing code (a lint/grep test).
- Financial writes are idempotent (keys), atomic (one transaction per state change with its outbox row), append-only (history), corrected only by compensating rows, and never trust a provider success response without a verified event plus reconciliation (CLAUDE.md rule 4).
- Data stays in India (Supabase Mumbai; AWS ap-south-1 in production). Provider calls leave India only if the provider processes data abroad, which each provider contract must rule out or limit (open item for D-65).
- AI is not used in this phase.

## 20. How mocks are labelled and blocked in production

- Ports `PaymentProviderPort`, `MandateProviderPort` and `LmsPort` each declare a `ProviderDescriptor` (`mode: 'mock' | 'sandbox' | 'production'`, capability `payment_collection`, `mandate`, `lms`). Mocks (`mock_payment_provider`, `mock_mandate_provider`, `mock_lms`) call `assertProviderAllowed`, additionally refuse `staging` (as the mock lender connector does), never touch the network, and expose deterministic scenarios: success, failure, delayed, duplicate event, out-of-order, partial amount, wrong merchant, refund, chargeback, unknown outcome, bad signature, old timestamp, mandate rejected/returned/revoked.
- `provider_mode` is stored on configurations, intents, events, mandates, settlement batches and LMS connections. A database guard (same pattern as `udofin.allow_mock_connectors`: `udofin.allow_mock_providers`) refuses `mock` rows unless the session explicitly allows it (tests), and no API can set it. Production start-up validation (`config.ts`) refuses any mock/sandbox provider and a sandbox credential; the activation gate for `payments`, `mandates` and `servicing_sync` requires `provider_mode = 'production'` evidence, so **a mock or sandbox record can never satisfy a production gate** (the "planned database check" in `docs/integrations/register.md` §3.2 is delivered here).
- Mock webhook scheme is named `mock-standard-webhooks-v1` and exists only in test code paths; non-production apps and portals show a persistent SANDBOX/TEST banner; non-production statements/NOCs carry a watermark; synthetic data only in staging.

## 28. Risks

The largest risks are not technical: no lender LMS, provider, sponsor bank, DLT or telephony arrangement exists, so everything runs on mocks and "works" only in tests; the payments and mandate rules of 2026 are known only from summaries; an unverified webhook or a redirect return could be mistaken for a payment unless the UI and database keep the three-stage truth; a lender that does not carry Udofin's reference in its receipts makes reconciliation partial; staleness can make a correct app show an old figure unless the banner and payment gating are kept; Udofin acting as both guarantor and collector invites a conflict-of-interest challenge from lenders (segregation, no incentive fields, kill switches); the OPC's capacity to give DLG and to act as recovery agent is the owner's position, not counsel's; the 6 Aug 2026 recovery text is unread; reminders are only "service messages" by our interpretation and a regulator or telecom operator may classify some as promotional; and every new schema must be added to the grant and structure tests or a later regression will pass CI unnoticed.

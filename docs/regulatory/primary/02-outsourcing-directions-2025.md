## 1. Applicability

### 1.1 Which lenders

- Para 3(1): applies to NBFC-D, NBFC-ICC, NBFC-Factor, NBFC-MFI, NBFC-IFC, IDF-NBFC, HFC, SPDs, CICs, NBFC-P2P and NBFC-AA, "subject to layer-wise applicability".
- Para 3(2): "The provisions prescribed in these directions **except Chapter IV and IT-specific provisions contained in Chapter II** ... shall apply to NBFCs included in Base layer. These directions including Chapter IV and IT-specific provisions contained in Chapter II are applicable for NBFCs in Middle layer and above layers."
- Para 3(3): Chapter IV and the IT-specific Chapter II paragraphs apply to a Mortgage Guarantee Company in Middle layer and above.
- Para 3(4): not applicable to NOFHC.
- **Banks are not covered by this instrument.** If a partner lender is a bank, a different RBI instrument applies and has not been read here (open question Q9).

Consequence for Udofin: for a **Base-layer lender**, only Chapter III (plus non-IT Chapter II) reaches Udofin; Chapter IV (including the cloud paragraph 97, the 6-hour incident rule in para 61 and the India-storage clause in para 74(vi)) does not apply to that lender by force of the Directions. For a **Middle-layer or above lender**, both Chapter III (for the financial-services part) and Chapter IV (for the IT part) are in play. A single contract for all lenders should assume the stricter Chapter IV set.

### 1.3 What counts as outsourcing (quoted definitions)

- **Para 6(1) "Outsourcing":** "use of a third party (either an affiliated entity within a corporate group or an entity that is external to the corporate group) by an NBFC to perform activities on a continuing basis that would normally be undertaken by the NBFC itself, now or in the future. 'Continuing basis' shall include agreements for a limited period."
- **Para 14 "Material Outsourcing" (Chapter III):** "arrangements, which if disrupted, have the potential to significantly impact the business operations, reputation or profitability or customer service." Materiality factors (i)-(vi): importance of the activity, impact on earnings/solvency/liquidity/funding/capital/risk profile, reputation, cost as a proportion of operating costs, aggregate exposure to that service provider, and "significance of activities outsourced in the context of customer service and protection".
- **Para 58(2) "Material Outsourcing of IT Services" (Chapter IV):** those which "(i) if disrupted or compromised shall have the potential to significantly impact the NBFC's business operations; or (ii) may have material impact on the NBFC's customers in the event of any unauthorised access, loss, or theft of customer information."
- **Para 58(1):** "'IT services' means IT services / IT enabled services / IT activities."
- **Para 58(3) "Service Provider"** (Chapter IV): "provider of IT services including entities related to the NBFC, or those which belong to the same group or conglomerate to which the NBFC belongs." The proviso lists (indicative, not exhaustive) entities that are **not** Service Providers for Chapter IV:
  - (i) "vendors providing business services using IT (e.g., BCs)";
  - (ii) payment system operators authorised under the PSS Act;
  - (iii) "partnership based FinTech firms such as those providing co-branded applications, products, and services (would be considered under outsourcing of financial services in Chapter III)";
  - (iv) "FinTech firms providing services for data retrieval, and data validation and verification such as, financial statement analysis, GST returns analysis, fetching of vehicle information, digital document execution, data entry and call centre services";
  - (v) telecom providers of leased lines; (vi) independent security or audit consultants.
- **Para 58(4) "Sub-contractor":** "those providing material / significant IT services to the service provider and is specific to the material / significant IT services arrangement that the NBFC has entered into with the service provider."
- **Para 5(1)** Chapter IV covers outsourcing of: (i) IT infrastructure management, maintenance and support; (ii) network and security solutions; (iii) "application development, maintenance and testing by Application Service Providers (ASPs)"; (iv) "services and operations related to data centres"; (v) "cloud computing services"; (vi) managed security services; (vii) IT infrastructure and technology services of the payment system ecosystem.
- **Para 5(2) exclusions** include: (ii) external audit services such as VA/PT, IS audit and security review; (iii) SMS gateways incl. bulk SMS; (iv) hardware procurement; (v) licensed software "on a licence or subscription basis" and vendor enhancements; (vi) OEM maintenance; (ix) "off-the-shelf products ... wherein only a license is procured with no or minimal customisation"; (xi) BC services, payroll processing, statement printing.

## 3. Implications for collections and recovery agents

What this instrument says that bears on a provider acting as recovery agent (Udofin directly, or through its own sub-contracted collection agents):

1. **Recovery agents are named once, in para 17**: the NBFC "shall ... be responsible for the actions of its service provider including Direct Sales Agents (DSAs) / Direct Marketing Agents (DMAs) and recovery agents and the confidentiality of information pertaining to the customers that is available with the service provider." Actions of Udofin and of any agent Udofin uses are attributed to the lender.
2. **Recovery is not on the para 15 prohibited list** (core management functions, internal audit, strategic and compliance functions, KYC compliance for deposit accounts, loan sanction, investment portfolio). Nothing in this text bars outsourcing recovery. It also is not one of the para 4(1) examples, so its Chapter III coverage rests on the definition in para 6(1) and the naming in para 17 (Q4).
3. **The agent's nature must be written down**: para 33 requires the agreement to "bring out the nature of legal relationship between the parties, i.e., whether agent-principal or otherwise." For recovery this is the clause that fixes whether Udofin collects as the lender's agent.
4. **Sub-collection agents are sub-contractors**: para 34(iv) requires the lender's "prior approval or consent" for any sub-contractor; para 30(vi) asks about the "quality of due diligence exercised by the service provider of its ... sub-contractors". Para 74(xiii) (contractual liability for sub-contractors) applies if Chapter IV applies.
5. **Customer-data limits**: need-to-know access (paras 24, 69(i)); no comingling across lenders (26, 70); confidentiality survives termination (34(xi)); liability for leakage (34(v), 28). The collections team and any agent must therefore see only the borrowers of the lender they act for.
6. **Conduct and reputation risk** (para 22(ii)): "its customer interactions are inconsistent with the overall standards of the NBFC". Para 18(v): "same high standard of care ... as would be employed by the NBFC". Para 56 (microfinance): the loan agreement and FPC must declare the NBFC "accountable for inappropriate behaviour of the employees of the service provider". The text sets no collection-conduct rules (calling hours, harassment, etc.); those sit in other RBI instruments not covered here.
7. **Grievances about recovery**: remain with the NBFC (paras 56, 57(2)); the GRO machinery "will also deal with the issue relating to services provided by the outsourced agency". Udofin must route recovery complaints to the lender's machinery, and the 30-day timelines in para 57(3)-(4) apply to the lender.
8. **Disclosure**: para 55 requires lender literature to state that service providers "may be used" in sales and marketing; the text speaks of "sales, and marketing" only, so recovery-agent disclosure is not expressly required here (Q5).
9. **Termination publicity** (44, 89): on termination the lender must tell customers so they "do not continue to deal with the service provider". A terminated recovery agent must stop contacting borrowers.
10. **Call-centre exclusion is narrow** (para 58(3)(iv)): "call centre services" by FinTech firms are not Chapter IV "IT service providers". That does not exempt a collections call centre from Chapter III.
11. **Reconciliation** (paras 12(2)(ii), 39): if the agent collects or reconciles funds, the lender must ensure timely reconciliation and the Audit Committee reviews ageing of unreconciled entries. This clashes with the no-pass-through rule in ADR 0008; keep all borrower payments direct to the lender.
12. **CTR/STR** (19): suspicious activity detected during recovery is reported by the lender; the agent escalates to it.
13. **Conflict with DLG**: the text does not address a provider that both guarantees losses and collects. Para 22(i) (provider "conducts business on its own behalf, inconsistent with the overall strategic goals") and 22(ii) are the nearest hooks; a provider with its own money at stake in a defaulted loan has an incentive that the lender must assess (Q6).

## 4. Implications for hosting (Indian data location, cloud, a cheaper India-based provider)

**Does the text require Indian hosting?** Only indirectly:

- Para 74(vi): agreement must include "storage of data only in India (as applicable) as per extant regulatory requirements" (Chapter IV, Middle layer and above). The rule itself is left to "extant regulatory requirements".
- Para 53(viii): for offshore outsourcing of financial services, "all original records continue to be maintained in India."
- Paras 53(vi)-(vii) and 94: an offshore regulator or court must not reach the Indian operations' data merely because it is processed there.
- Para 97(v)(c): "data sovereignty" is a cloud-policy compliance item.
- **Nothing in this text states the "process abroad and return within 24 hours" rule** (searched; not present). That rule must be sourced from the digital lending directions.

**Conditions a hosting provider must meet according to the text** (Chapter IV, applied through the lender to Udofin; each is a diligence question for any Indian provider):

1. Subject to a jurisdiction that "uphold[s] enforceability of agreements and the rights available thereunder ... including those relating to aspects such as data storage, data protection, and confidentiality" (97(vi)); not impeding RBI supervision (18(ii)); in principle in a jurisdiction that upholds confidentiality (52, 93).
2. Data stored only in India, as applicable (74(vi)); list of data captured, processed and stored (74(vii)); all locations, including backups and replicas, known (97(iii)).
3. Contractual access for the lender, its auditors, RBI and competent authorities to data and to "relevant business premises ... subject to appropriate security protocols" (81); RBI inspection of the provider "and any of its sub-contractors" (74(xii)); lender audit rights, copies of audit reports (34(vii)); prior consent to sub-contractors (34(iv)); contractual liability for sub-contractors (74(xiii)).
4. Encryption keys and HSMs "under the control of the NBFC" in a "secure container-based data management" design (97(vii)(b)). A provider that cannot support lender-controlled keys per tenant is a problem (Q3).
5. Protection "against co-mingling of data, in case of multi-tenancy environments" (97(vii)(c)); isolation so that only lender-authorised personnel access the lender's data (70).
6. Resilience and recovery from failure of any component (97(vii)(d)); business continuity for CSP failure (97(xiv)); DR drills evidenced (97(xv)); BCP/DR tested periodically (40, 83).
7. IAM: role-based, segregation of duties and role conflict matrix, need-to-know and least privilege, MFA for cloud access (97(viii)); security controls at least equal to on-premises, secure network configuration, monitoring and change authorisation (97(ix)).
8. Information security policy framework; ability to keep pace with threats; testing of controls proportionate to materiality; assessment of its own sub-contractors (97(x)(a)-(d)).
9. Log and event integration into the lender's SOC where applicable, and retention of relevant logs for incident handling (97(xi)); log retention period not set in this text.
10. Patching and updates by both parties (97(xii)); threat-and-vulnerability management with threat intelligence (97(xiii)).
11. Exit: agreed turnaround times for returning data and "service collaterals", data completeness and portability, secure purge, liabilities and penalties in the SLA, exit plan matched to the technology stack, prompt take-over of records and configuration data, "independent assurance before signing off" (97(xvi)); plus 87 (no erasure during transition) and 88 (cooperate with the new provider).
12. Audit and certification coverage of the ten areas in 97(xvii); "globally recognised third-party certifications" may be relied on (79) but do not remove the lender's duty of assurance.
13. Incident reporting: ability to detect and notify fast enough for the six-hour rule in 61, with defined material adverse events (74(iii)).
14. Financial and operational soundness (30(iv), 38, 80); back-to-back arrangements with OEMs (74(xviii)); legal obligation to comply with RBI directions about the outsourced service (74(xiv)).
15. Not owned or controlled by a lender's director, KMP or relatives (18(vi), 59).

**On "cheaper India-based provider instead of AWS".** The text has no preference for any named provider, hyperscaler or not. It sets conditions, not brands; there is no empanelment or MeitY-listing requirement in this instrument (nothing on MeitY was found in the text). The conditions above are the test. A smaller Indian provider is likelier to be fine on the jurisdiction/enforceability and India-storage points, and likelier to struggle on 97(vii)(b) (lender-controlled keys/HSMs), 97(xv) (DR drills), 97(xi) (log integration), 97(xvii) (certified audits), 74(xii) (RBI inspection of the sub-contractor) and 97(xvi) (exit). Each one must be shown in writing; none may be assumed from the provider being Indian.

## 7. Open questions for counsel

1. **Chapter III or IV (or both) for the Udofin platform.** Para 58(3)(iii) puts "partnership based FinTech firms ... co-branded applications" in Chapter III, while para 5(1)(iii) puts "application development, maintenance and testing by ASPs" in Chapter IV. Does a lender-branded borrower app run by Udofin fall in one, the other or both? Until answered, contract to the stricter Chapter IV list.
2. **Does the lender "use cloud" when only Udofin does?** Para 97 is written for "an NBFC that uses cloud services". Is the hosting provider a "sub-contractor" under para 58(4) bound through paras 74(i), 74(xii), 74(xiii), and does every para 97 sub-item need to be contractually present in our hosting contract?
3. **Para 97(vii)(b): "encryption keys and Hardware Security Modules are under the control of the NBFC".** Does this require per-lender keys held or controlled by the lender in a shared multi-tenant platform, or is Udofin-managed per-tenant KMS with lender revocation enough? Which Indian providers can support it?
4. **Is recovery within Chapter III?** Para 4(1) lists examples that do not include recovery; para 17 names recovery agents. Confirm that recovery by a platform provider is an in-scope outsourcing, and which other RBI instruments (fair practices, recovery conduct) govern the agent.
5. **Disclosure of recovery agent use.** Para 55 mentions "sales, and marketing". Must the lender also disclose recovery-agent use in its literature and loan agreement (outside this text)?
6. **DLG plus recovery plus platform.** Does a provider that guarantees 5% of losses and also collects raise a conflict the lender cannot accept (paras 22(i)-(ii), 22(iv) financial capacity)? Is the DLG itself permitted and how must it be funded and evidenced? (Not decided by this text.)
7. **Does the "storage of data only in India (as applicable)" in para 74(vi) cover logs, IP addresses, backups, telemetry and support tickets?** And how do we reconcile it with the reported 24-hour rule, whose source is not in this text?
8. **Group status.** Are Udofin, Finbyx, LawDocsComply and Equator "group entities" of any lender (para 6(2) definition by cross-reference)? If not, are the OUT-12 disclosure steps still needed, and on what basis?
9. **Bank partners.** If any partner is a bank, which instrument replaces this one and does it impose stricter terms (this instrument covers NBFCs only)?
10. **Six-hour clock.** Para 61 runs from "detection by the service provider". How must we define "detection" in the contract, and what notice period to the lender is defensible (we propose a much shorter one)?
11. **Pooled audits (para 77) and certifications (para 79).** Can we satisfy several lenders with one pooled audit and one certificate set, and what do lenders in practice accept?
12. **Layer of each partner lender.** Confirm each lender's scale-based layer (Base vs Middle and above) before deciding whether Chapter IV is mandatory for that contract, and record it in the lender onboarding checklist.
13. **Repeal and saving (paras 100-101).** Confirm that references in our matrix to the 2017 Master Direction and the April 2023 IT outsourcing MD are updated to the 2025 Directions, and that nothing in the repealed instruments that we rely on has been dropped.

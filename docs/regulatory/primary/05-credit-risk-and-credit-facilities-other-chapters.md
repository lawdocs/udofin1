meaning as have been assigned to them under the Banking Regulation Act, 1949, or the Reserve Bank of

Points the text does not settle:

- **"DLG provider" is not a defined term.** It appears in paras 19, 20, 22, 26(2), 27 and 28. The
  eligibility rule is in para 19: "An NBFC may enter into DLG arrangements only with an LSP / other
  lender engaged as an LSP. Further, the LSP providing DLG shall be incorporated as a company under
  the Companies Act, 2013." The "another entity" in the DLG definition is therefore, for NBFCs, an
  LSP or a lender engaged as an LSP.
- **"Recovery agent" is not defined in the CF Directions.** The term is used in para 6(5), para 9(6)
  and para 10(5). The LSP definition lists "recovery of specific loan or loan portfolio" among the
  functions an LSP can perform. The recovery-agent conduct rules are in the Responsible Business
  Conduct Directions, 2025, which are cross-referred (para 6(5)) but are not in the PDFs read.
- **The PA proviso says "Chapter IV".** In this PDF Chapter IV is "Lending against Gold and Silver
  Collateral" and Chapter III is "Digital Lending". The cross-reference looks like a drafting
  error, but this file does not assume so. Counsel question Q9.

"loan against property", "secured loan". Their treatment depends on the specific paragraphs below.
The other 4(1) definitions (actual DCCO, appointed date, credit event, date of financial closure,

CRM Directions definitions that matter (para 4(1); paraphrase unless quoted):

- 4(1)(ivb): "'Personal Loans' shall have the same meaning as defined under Banking Statistics
  (Harmonised Definitions). However, for these Directions, personal loans shall exclude loans for
  investments in financial assets."
- 4(1)(va), 4(1)(vb): "Related Party" and "Related Person" (inserted effective 1 April 2026). A
  related person includes a promoter, director or KMP of the NBFC, a holder of more than 5 per cent
  of paid-up equity or voting rights, a person who can nominate a director through an agreement,
  a person in control, and the relatives of such a person.
- 4(1)(viia): "'Specified employees' mean all employees of a NBFC who are positioned upto two levels
  below the Board and any employee designated as such as per the NBFC's policy."

CF Directions applicability (para 3, printed pages 5-6), for comparison:

- 3(1): all layers unless specified otherwise, for NBFC-D, NBFC-ICC, NBFC-Factor, NBFC-MFI, NBFC-IFC, IDF-NBFC, HFC.

The text does not say which paragraphs are "prudential" and which are "conduct". The LTV rule in para 106 is probably prudential; digital lending chapter is probably conduct. This is an interpretation. Q5.
- 3(2)(ii): paras 105, 106 and 107 apply to a CIC; 3(2)(iii): para 21(2) applies to NBFC-P2P.
- 3(3): not applicable to MGC, NBFC-AA, SPD, NOFHC, "NBFC not availing public funds and not having any customer interface".

### 3.3 Restrictions the product catalogue must respect (from the CRM Directions)

1. No loan against the lender's own shares (para 14) or own debentures (para 15).
2. Related-party lending is permitted only under the lender's credit policy, with limits, Board or Committee sanction above the materiality threshold, and recusal (paras 13A-13J). Udofin must not route a lender's related-party or specified-employee applicants around those controls.
3. LEI is required for non-individual borrowers at Rs.5 crore aggregate exposure or more (paras 19-20). It does not touch the current individual-borrower catalogue.
4. Security-interest filing with CERSAI is the lender's act and is "advised" on an ongoing basis (para 28). No number of days is stated.
5. Credit appraisal rules (paras 6-8) bind Notified NBFCs only and are written for corporate lending.

### 4.2 Gold and silver collateral and other collateral rules that reach beyond gold

Chapter IV (printed pages 29-33). Para 31 is deleted with effect from 1 April 2026 (footnote 2).
Relevant extracts:

- **Para 35(1) and (2), a rule for any loan an NBFC grants, printed under "Restrictions and Ceilings":** "The NBFC shall not grant any advance or loan: (1) For purchase of gold in any form including primary gold, ornaments, jewellery, or coins, or for purchase of financial assets backed by gold, e.g., units of Exchange-traded funds (ETFs) or units of Mutual Funds; and (2) against primary gold or silver or financial assets backed by primary gold or silver."
  Consequence for the LAMF catalogue (interpretation): gold or silver ETF and gold or silver fund-of-fund units are probably not eligible collateral, and a loan to buy them is barred. The eligible-scheme list should be reviewed against para 35. Q1.
- Para 36: no loan where ownership of the collateral is doubtful; the borrower must give "a suitable document or declaration ... to the effect that the borrower is the rightful owner of the eligible collateral". (Text is in the gold chapter and speaks of "eligible collateral" defined for that chapter.)
- Para 43 LTV table for consumption loans against gold or silver ornaments or coins: up to and including Rs.2.5 lakh, 85 per cent; above Rs.2.5 lakh up to and including Rs.5 lakh, 80 per cent; above Rs.5 lakh, 75 per cent. Para 44: "The prescribed LTV ratio shall be maintained on an ongoing basis throughout the tenor of the loan."
- Paras 46-47: disbursal to the borrower's bank account; transfers to third-party accounts only for the listed exceptions; "Loan servicing, repayment, etc. is executed by the borrower directly in the NBFC's account without any pass-through account or pool account of any third party." The same rule for all digital lending is at para 10(1)-(3).

Scope for an LSP (one line each):

- **Gold and silver (Chapter IV):** outside Udofin's product scope today. It needs physical custody, assaying and vault handling by the lender, so it is not a remote digital product. Only paras 35, 46 and 47 echo rules already visible elsewhere.
- **Microfinance (Chapter V):** does touch scope. Para 51 defines a microfinance loan as a collateral-free loan to a household with annual household income up to Rs.3,00,000, and says "all collateral-free loans, irrespective of end use and mode of application / processing / disbursal (either through physical or digital channels), provided to low-income households ... shall be considered as microfinance loans". An unsecured personal loan to a borrower in such a household would therefore carry para 52-62 duties for the lender: board-approved household income assessment (52), mandatory submission of household income to CICs (54), a limit of "maximum 50 per cent of the monthly household income" on loan repayment obligations including the new loan (55-56), repayment flexibility (59), and pricing policy and no usurious rates (60-61). The text does not say which NBFC types the chapter binds beyond the general applicability in para 3. Q5.
- **Project finance (Chapter VI), partial credit enhancement (Chapter VII), IPO ceiling (para 104), finance for housing projects (para 108):** outside the retail catalogue. No LSP role.

### 4.3 Housing and real estate paragraphs relevant to the home loan and LAP rows

- Para 109: "While appraising loan proposals involving real estate, the NBFC shall ensure that the borrowers have obtained prior permission from Government / local Government / other statutory authorities for the project, wherever required. ... the proposals may be sanctioned in normal course, the disbursements shall be made only after the borrower has obtained requisite clearances from the Government/ their statutory authorities." Home-loan flows need a lender-confirmed clearance milestone before disbursement.
- Para 110: "Further, loans for third dwelling unit onwards to an individual will be treated as CRE exposure." The lender classifies; Udofin should capture the number of existing dwelling units only if the lender's policy requires it.
- Para 4(1)(xiv) (quoted in section 2.2): a loan against mortgage for a purpose other than buying, constructing or renovating a dwelling unit is a non-housing loan. LAP for general purposes is therefore not "Housing Finance" under this text.

None of the pages read sets an LTV cap, valuation method or margin for vehicle loans, home loans or loans against property. Not addressed in this text. For mutual-fund collateral the same holds. LTV numbers are in the text only for listed shares (para 106) and gold or silver (para 43). Any LTV for the other secured products comes from other instruments or from lender policy.

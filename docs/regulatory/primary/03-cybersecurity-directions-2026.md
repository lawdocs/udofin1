## 1. What the text is, in one paragraph

It is a single, stand-alone set of Directions for NBFCs (including HFCs registered under the NHB Act) covering IT governance, IT policy, information security, cybersecurity, IT operations, information systems audit, business continuity and disaster recovery, and, for Chapter IV only, IT services outsourcing. It is addressed only to regulated entities. **It does not mention lending service providers, digital lending apps, DLAs, or Udofin-type entities anywhere.** Whatever it requires of an LSP reaches the LSP only through the lender's contract, the lender's due diligence and the lender's audit rights. It has no data-localisation rule, no data-retention period, no log-retention period and no CERT-In timeline (see sections 3 and 6).

### 2.3 Which chapter a typical Udofin partner lender falls under, and why it matters (Udofin analysis)

- Udofin serves "small and mid" NBFCs. In practice that spans three cases: (a) Base Layer below Rs 500 crore (Chapter III); (b) Base Layer at Rs 500 crore and above (Chapter IV); (c) Middle Layer (Chapter V). We cannot assume which; the lender's chapter is a due-diligence fact to record in each lender's onboarding file (asset size on latest audited balance sheet; SBR layer; whether a CIC, HFC or deposit-taker).
- **Chapter III lender (below Rs 500 crore).** Text imposes only: a Board-approved IT/IS policy with basic standards (para 8), BCP policy with annual Board reporting (para 8(8)), backup with periodic testing (para 8(9)), and the Chapter IV rules on Digital Signature Certificates, mobile financial services and social media (paras 31, 33, 34 via para 8(5)). It will probably ask Udofin for less formal evidence, but the lender is still bound by the Outsourcing Directions 2025 and the Digital Lending rules (not in this file), and will still need access, logs and a BCP from its LSP. Para 33 (mobile services: confidentiality, integrity, authenticity and end-to-end encryption) is directly relevant to the borrower app.
- **Chapter IV lender (Rs 500 crore and above, Base Layer).** Adds paras 60-64 (IT outsourcing contract content, Board/ITSC accountability, BCP assurance from the provider) and para 17 (ITSC governance of outsourced operations). This is the first chapter that gives the lender a written list of what an IT outsourcing contract must contain and that expects "audit trails and logs for administrative activities" to be retained by the provider and made accessible (para 62(2)(i)). It sets six-hour DAKSH reporting (para 28) and an annual IS audit that covers BCP/DR (paras 47-58). There is no VA/PT cadence in Chapter IV, only "a vulnerability management process" (para 23).
- **Chapter V lender (Middle Layer and above).** The most demanding. It sets numeric cadences the lender will push down: VA at least once in every six months and PT at least once in 12 months for critical information systems and customer-facing DMZ systems (para 121), DR drills at least half-yearly (para 129), RTO/RPO (paras 134-136, 147), vendor and partner resilience testing (paras 138, 146), annual security review (para 98), audit-trail monitoring (para 111), MFA for privileged users (para 115), a CISO-run SOC (para 82(4)). **Chapter V has no equivalent of Chapter IV paras 60-64; it points outsourcing elsewhere** (the heading note before para 117 says para 117 applies to IT/cyber third-party arrangements "which are not within the applicability of" the NBFC Managing Risks in Outsourcing Directions, 2025). So for a Chapter V lender, the contract terms for Udofin come from the 2025 Outsourcing Directions (register row C01) plus para 117 here; for a Chapter IV lender they come from paras 60-64 here.
- **Why the lender's chapter determines what it demands of Udofin.** The Directions bind the lender, not Udofin. Each lender must evidence its own compliance, including for the systems and services Udofin operates for it (the lender's information assets sit in Udofin's platform when Udofin acts as technology service provider). The lender will therefore copy the paragraphs of its chapter into its vendor questionnaire, contract schedule and audit plan. A Chapter V lender will ask for VA/PT reports at the stated cadence, DR drill evidence and RTO/RPO; a Chapter III lender will mostly ask for a security policy, access control, backups and BCP. Udofin should build once to the Chapter V standard (section 5) and show the lender the paragraph map for its own chapter.

**Terms the text uses but does not define** (important for contract drafting; counsel to decide how they will be read):

- "critical systems" / "critical information systems" / "critical applications" / "critical activities" (paras 21(4), 95(3), 105, 109, 115, 116(2), 119, 121, 123, 129, 135, 138, 147). There is **no definition**, no test and no list. Para 95(3) only requires the NBFC to identify them. Udofin analysis: the lender will decide, and a platform that holds the loan journey and borrower consents is likely to be classed critical; assume yes.
- "third party", "service provider", "outsourcing service provider", "vendor", "partner". Not defined. Para 62 uses "outsourcing service provider"; para 95(2) refers to "third-party personnel"; para 117 refers to "vendor"; para 138 to "vendors and partners". The text cross-refers to the Outsourcing Directions 2025 for what counts as outsourcing (heading note before para 117).
- "lending service provider", "digital lending", "customer data" (para 117(4) says "customer data" without definition), "incident severity" (para 139(1) requires classification but gives no scale).

## 5. Minimum security baseline Udofin should adopt to satisfy a lender's due diligence

Derived strictly from the text. Where the text states no value (retention periods, fix deadlines, RTO/RPO numbers, patch timelines, notice times), Udofin must choose a value and agree it by contract; those are flagged "no value in text." Paragraph references are to this instrument. Items marked (V) are textually Chapter V requirements and (IV) Chapter IV; adopting the stricter set once avoids per-lender variants.

### 5.1 Governance

1. Board-approved (or equivalent top-management-approved) information security policy, cybersecurity policy and cyber crisis management plan, reviewed at least annually (paras 6, 20-22, 68-69, 83-84).
2. A named senior owner of IT and a named security lead who does not report to the head of engineering and has no delivery targets (para 81; the CISO rules are a lender-side requirement but the lender will look for the equivalent), with the CISO reviewing cyber risk before top management at least quarterly (para 82(7)).
3. Documented roles, with segregation of duties between development and operations, system administration, database administration and transaction processing (paras 21(2), 95(2)).
4. Annual IT and cyber risk assessment with a risk register that identifies critical information systems (paras 32, 94-96).
5. Asset inventory and security classification of assets (paras 21(1), 91).
6. Acceptable-use and security policy acknowledged by staff and sub-processors (para 97); ongoing security training with completion records and effectiveness checks (paras 35-36).
7. Background screening of personnel with privileged access (para 21(4)).
8. Vendor risk process: sub-processor register, concentration and single-point-of-failure review, conflict-of-interest and supply-chain assessment (para 117); contract terms for outsourced IT services per para 62.
9. Contract content Udofin should expect to sign and be able to meet: continuous monitoring; protection of data; lender access to books, records and information; retention and request-based access to administrative audit trails and logs; audit by the lender's internal or external auditors with copies of audit reports; RBI access within a reasonable time, in paper and electronic form (para 62).

### 5.2 Technical controls

1. Role-based, least-privilege access with documented approval for changes to roles and key business parameters; no dependence on one person (paras 21(3), 113).
2. MFA for all privileged users of critical systems and for critical activities, and MFA for remote access to critical systems (paras 115, 116(2)).
3. Maker-checker (two-person) approval on money-affecting and parameter-changing actions (para 21(6)).
4. Privileged activity fully logged and periodically reviewed (para 114).
5. Audit trails on every system that can access or affect critical or sensitive information, detailed enough for audit, forensics, dispute resolution and non-repudiation; unauthorised activity recorded; trails monitored regularly to detect unauthorised activity or attack (paras 21(8), 109-111). Retention period: no value in text.
6. Strong cryptography with non-deprecated algorithms, key lengths, cipher suites and protocols for transmission, processing and authentication (para 118); end-to-end encryption for mobile services (para 33); PKI where practicable (para 21(9)).
7. Controls against the threat list in para 27 (DoS and DDoS, ransomware, destructive malware, business email fraud, phishing, spear phishing, whaling, vishing, drive-by downloads, browser gateway fraud, ghost administrator exploits, identity fraud, memory update fraud, password-related fraud); measures against zero-day, remote access and targeted attacks (para 26); phishing and spoofing defences (para 98).
8. Vulnerability management process (para 23); VA at least once in every six months and PT at least once in 12 months for critical systems and customer-facing DMZ systems, and VA/PT before go-live, after go-live and after changes (para 121); by trained, independent testers (para 122); on production or a production-like environment (para 124); time-bound fixes with CVSS or similar scoring (paras 125-126) (no fix deadlines in text).
9. Documented change and patch management with impact assessment, approvals and rollback (para 112); no unsupported hardware or software, end-of-support tracking and refresh plan (paras 92-93). Patch timelines: no value in text.
10. Secure development: defects identified and remediated through design, development and testing (para 39); product approval and quality assurance before any new application goes live (para 88); for software supplied to a lender, written confirmation of freedom from known vulnerabilities, malware and covert channels, renewed on material changes (para 106); vendor maintenance commitments and source-code escrow or access (paras 104-105).
11. Automated, authenticated, audit-trailed data transfer with no manual modification of data in transit between systems (paras 119-120).
12. Public-facing infrastructure able to handle IPv6 traffic (para 18(4)).
13. Physical and environmental controls, and DC/DR geographic separation, evidenced through the cloud provider (paras 21(5), 101-103).

### 5.3 Operations

1. Capacity assessment at least annually (para 108); availability across all service delivery channels (para 107).
2. IT service management and service level management with segregation of duties (paras 89-90).
3. Data migration policy with audit trail and business sign-offs (para 99); data dictionary (para 100).
4. Cooperation with the lender's information systems audit: audit-mode access for auditors, inspectors and regulators; copies of audit reports; remediation owned and tracked (paras 57, 62(2)).
5. System-generated data and reports the lender needs for supervisory returns and read-only RBI access paths where Udofin holds the data (paras 45-46).
6. Annual review of security infrastructure and policies (para 98).

### 5.4 Resilience

1. Board-level (or top management) approved BCP with business impact analysis, recovery strategy and worst-case scenario testing at least annually and after significant change, with gap analysis fed back (para 59).
2. DR drills at least half-yearly for critical systems, switching to the alternate site and running a full working day, with major issues fixed and re-tested before the next cycle (paras 129-131).
3. Defined RTO and RPO per critical system, near-zero RPO and minimal RTO targeted, documented reconciliation for non-zero RPO (paras 134-136, 147); no numeric values in text, so agree them with each lender.
4. Backups taken, periodically restored to prove usability, integrity preserved, protected from unauthorised access (paras 8(9), 133).
5. Identical configuration and patch levels at DC and DR (para 137); DC and DR geographically well separated (para 102).
6. Willingness to take part in collaborative, co-ordinated resilience testing with the lender and to test Udofin's own vendors (paras 59(5), 138).
7. Key cybersecurity controls restored on recovery, not only service (para 128).

### 5.5 Incident

1. Written incident response and recovery procedures with named roles, classification and assessment, communication plan, containment and recovery (paras 139, 142-143).
2. An incident-notice clock to each lender short enough to let the lender report "within six hours of detection" on DAKSH (paras 28, 141); the text sets no provider deadline, so the contractual deadline is Udofin's choice and the lender's ask. Notice to the lender should not wait for root cause.
3. Support for lender-led CERT-In notification (para 141) and, where the lender is an HFC, NHB reporting (Note to para 141).
4. Forensic readiness: preserve evidence and analyse severity, impact and root cause, with corrective and preventive actions (para 140).
5. Lessons learned and joint drills with stakeholders, including service providers (paras 145-146).
6. Incident, impact and non-recurrence reporting in lender MIS format (para 44(9)).

### 6.2 Corrected

1. **"The 31 July 2026 instrument might be a supervisory consolidation" (register row 6 of the open-questions table: "Department of Supervision consolidation (64 directions ...) ... Not necessarily inconsistent, but no note confirms the full list"; also register C04 and C05 "may be the '31 July 2026' instrument").** Correction from the primary text: this document is **not** a 64-direction consolidation. It is one stand-alone set of Directions with 158 paragraphs, six chapters and a single subject (NBFC cybersecurity, technology, resilience, assurance), issued under s.45-L and 45-M RBI Act and the Factoring and NHB Act provisions. It carries its own reference (RBI/DoS/2026-27/461) and file number (DoS.CO.CSITEG. 55 /31.01.015/2026-27). The repeal provision (para 155) repeals only "the existing directions, instructions, and guidelines relating to Information Technology Framework and IT Governance as applicable to Non-Banking Financial Companies ... as communicated vide **circular no. DoS.CO.PPG.66/11.01.005/2026-27 dated July 31, 2026**." That separate circular is the instrument that lists what is repealed; it is not in our file. The text says nothing about "64" directions or "628 circulars", so those numbers remain secondary-source claims (RDL) and unverified. It is plausible that this Direction was issued as one of a same-day batch from the Department of Supervision, but the batch itself is outside this text. Recommended wording for the register: "Confirmed from primary text: stand-alone NBFC cyber/technology Directions RBI/DoS/2026-27/461; repeal list is in circular DoS.CO.PPG.66/11.01.005/2026-27 of 31 Jul 2026 (not yet obtained); '64 directions' remains unverified."
2. **C05 / register open-question 6: "May be the '31 July 2026' instrument the owner had in mind" (NBFC Compliance Function Directions, 2026).** The cybersecurity Directions here are a different instrument from the Compliance Function Directions. Nothing in this text refers to a compliance-function direction. Treat them as separate rows.
3. **C03 "VA every 6 months, PT every 12 months ... NBFC Middle Layer and above" and OUT-10 basis "RBI MD on IT Governance (2023), flow-down".** The numbers are confirmed, but the **authority has changed**: para 155 repeals the earlier IT Framework and IT Governance directions for NBFCs (the particular 2023 Master Direction is not named in this text; it is identified only through circular DoS.CO.PPG.66/11.01.005/2026-27). The citation for OUT-10 should become "RBI/DoS/2026-27/461, para 121 (Chapter V)". Also, **VA/PT cadence is not stated for Chapter III or IV lenders** (para 23 only requires a vulnerability management process). OUT-10's "Applies to: RE (Udofin by contract)" is right, but the cadence is a Chapter V lender demand; a Chapter IV lender may still ask for it by contract, and we should plan to meet it for everyone.
4. **C01 and OUT-05 phrase "Chapter IV (IT outsourcing) applies to NBFC Middle Layer and above".** In this instrument, outsourcing content is in Chapter IV G (paras 60-64) and applies to **Base Layer at Rs 500 crore and above only**; Chapter V (Middle Layer and above) has only the para 117 third-party paragraph and defers to the 2025 Outsourcing Directions. The two chapter numberings must be kept distinct in our docs.
5. **OUT-06 "security controls, audit trails and logs, access for the lender and RBI, and incident notice fast enough for the lender's 6-hour DAKSH reporting ... (as reported)".** Confirmed in substance; the "as reported" label can be dropped for the reference and title, but "likely" should stay for the **flow-down to Udofin**, because the text never mentions LSPs and gives no provider notice period. The "research suggests 2 hours" lender-notice figure is not in the text and must stay labelled as our recommendation.

### 6.4 New items the existing docs do not yet capture

- Chapter III has no incident reporting or cyber paragraph beyond "robust information security and cybersecurity controls"; a small lender's demands will be contractual, not regulatory.
- Para 106 (Chapter V): certificate from the application vendor that software is "free of known vulnerabilities, malware, and any covert channels", renewed on material changes, and para 105 source-code or escrow. If Udofin supplies the borrower app or portals to a Chapter V lender, expect this clause. Not in controls-matrix.
- Para 18(4) (Chapter IV): public-facing infrastructure to handle IPv6. Not in checklist.
- Para 131 (Chapter V): DR test must run production from the alternate site for at least a full working day. Not in checklist B-9.
- Para 137: identical configuration and patches at DC and DR.
- Para 138: collaborative resilience testing with vendors and partners at the lender's RTO.
- Para 146: crisis communication drills with service providers.
- Para 62(2)(i): "requisite audit trails and logs for administrative activities shall be retained by the service provider" with access to the NBFC on approved requests. This is the strongest text hook for the admin-log retention the register describes but gives no period.
- Para 111 and 114: log monitoring and privileged review are explicit duties of Chapter V lenders; plan alerting and review reports.
- Para 155 repeals the earlier IT Framework and IT Governance directions; the register's C02 and C03 should be re-checked against circular DoS.CO.PPG.66/11.01.005/2026-27.

## 7. Open questions for counsel

1. **Layer thresholds.** The Middle Layer, Upper Layer and Top Layer definitions are in the 2025 SBR Directions (not in our source folder). Confirm the Middle Layer asset threshold and any category-based inclusion (for example deposit-taking NBFCs, HFCs, Government-owned NBFCs, IFCs) so each lender can be placed in Chapter III, IV or V.
2. **Circular DoS.CO.PPG.66/11.01.005/2026-27 (31 Jul 2026).** Obtain it. What exactly does it repeal, including the earlier IT Governance, Risk, Controls and Assurance Practices Master Direction (register C03), the IT outsourcing Master Direction (C02) and any cyber circulars? Does the "64 directions" count appear there?
3. **Does the text apply to Udofin at all?** It binds NBFCs only. Does RBI treat an LSP as an outsourcing service provider for purposes of paras 62 and 117, and is a digital-lending LSP "within the applicability of" the Outsourcing Directions 2025 (the carve-out in the heading note to para 117 turns on this)? Which paragraphs govern a Chapter V lender's LSP contract: the Outsourcing Directions 2025 only, or para 117 as well?
4. **"Critical information systems".** Undefined. Will RBI or lenders treat the borrower app back end, loan origination APIs and consent store as critical? Is there any RBI clarification (para 158 allows RBI clarifications; they are "final and binding")?
5. **Six-hour clock.** "of detection": whose detection starts the lender's clock when the incident happens at Udofin? Can a contract require Udofin to notify within a stated number of hours without being treated as outsourcing the lender's reporting duty? What notice period is defensible (research suggests 2 hours)?
6. **CERT-In.** Para 141 says "pro-actively notify" CERT-In without a time. How does that interact with the CERT-In Directions of 28 Apr 2022 six-hour requirement, and who notifies when both lender and Udofin are covered entities (service provider versus body corporate)? Is the register's "likely" for CERT-In 6 hours and 180 days confirmed against the CERT-In primary text?
7. **Data location and log retention.** Since this text is silent, confirm the controlling instruments for India-only storage, the 24-hour offshore allowance and log retention (digital lending directions, CERT-In directions, Outsourcing Directions) and the paragraph numbers.
8. **Chapter V has no mobile paragraph.** Paragraphs 31, 33 and 34 (DSC, mobile financial services, social media) appear in Chapter IV and are imported into Chapter III by para 8(5), but Chapter V has no equivalent. Does a Chapter V lender remain subject to para 33 (end-to-end encryption for mobile services), or is that dropped? Counsel should confirm the intended reading.
9. **Chapter IV carries the contractual-clause list; Chapter V does not.** For a Chapter V lender, should Udofin accept the Chapter IV para 62 clause set as the standard contract in any case?
10. **CIC carve-out.** CICs fall in Chapter III (para 3(2)) but are excluded from Chapter V (para 3(4)); a separate CIC instrument (reportedly /470) exists. Is a CIC ever a partner lender for Udofin? If so, which instrument governs and where is the text?
11. **Software certification (para 106).** If Udofin supplies the lender-branded app or portals, is Udofin the "application developer or vendor" who must give the vulnerability-, malware- and covert-channel-free certificate? What standard of "known vulnerabilities" is acceptable, and what counts as a "material change" for a continuously deployed service (para 106 lets parties agree)?
12. **Source code escrow (para 105)** for a multi-tenant platform serving several lenders: can escrow be satisfied by a platform-level arrangement, and who bears its cost?
13. **Independence of testers (para 122)** and CERT-In empanelment: is empanelment needed, or is any independent, trained tester acceptable? Does the answer change by lender chapter?
14. **HFC lenders.** Confirm that an HFC partner reports to NHB and that NHB's cyber expectations match this text (Note to para 141).
15. **Transition.** Para 2 makes the Directions effective immediately and para 156 saves actions under the repealed instruments. Is there any grace period or compliance date for lenders to bring vendor contracts in line (the register mentions a 10 Apr 2026 date for existing IT outsourcing contracts under the 2025 Outsourcing Directions; confirm it is unaffected)?
16. **Register status labels.** Once counsel confirms this file, which register and controls-matrix rows (C03, C04, C05, OUT-06, OUT-10) may be relabelled "confirmed against primary text", and which must stay "likely"?

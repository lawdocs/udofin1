- **Reference:** RBI/DOR/2025-26/362, DOR.MCS.REC.No.281/01-01-039/2025-26, 28 November 2025 (signed Veena Srivastava, CGM). Issued under sections 45JA, 45L and 45M of the RBI Act, 1934.
- **Source file:** `docs/regulatory/sources/rbi-nbfc-responsible-business-conduct-directions-2025-updated-2026-04-01.pdf` (36 pages)
- **Method:** Extracted from the primary text with `pdftotext -layout` and read in full (pages 1-36: Chapters I-V, Annex I, Annex II). Page images were inspected for pages 34 and 35 (Annex I, the KFS template, which the text layer flattens) and matched the text layer. The APR illustration and repayment schedule (pages 12-13) were read from the text layer only; their figures were recomputed independently (section 3.8). Page 1 has a Hindi header that the text layer garbles; it carries no operative content. No paragraph number below was unreadable. Para 37 contains a printed defect ("issued on or December 1, 2023", presumably "on or after"); quoted as printed.
- **Status label for everything quoted here:** confirmed (primary text), **as of the 1 April 2026 consolidation**. Statements about what Udofin "must provide" are our reading (interpretation) and need counsel review (section 6).
- **Version caveat (important):** this file is the text "updated as on April 1, 2026". The controls matrix refers to later instruments (the RBC Second Amendment of 15 Jun 2026, and the recovery-agent amendment of 6 Aug 2026, called the "Third Amendment" in REC-03). **They are not in this file.** Nothing here confirms or contradicts them. A current consolidated text must be obtained before launch (Q1).

Framing: these Directions bind the **NBFC (the lender)**. Udofin is reached through the contract and through the lender's duty to be "responsible for the actions" of its agents. The text uses the word "LSP" only once, in the KFS template (Annex I, Part 2, item 6(ii)). It does not use the words "DLG", "default loss guarantee", "call recording", "IIBF", "certification" (of agents), or "Lending Service Provider". It names **"Recovery Agents"** in five places (paras 7 A.1(8), 94-99, 100) and **defines** them in para 93 as "agencies engaged by the NBFC for recovery of dues from its borrowers and the employees of these agencies". On that definition Udofin, if engaged by a lender to collect, **is itself a Recovery Agent**, and so are its employees and any sub-agency.

## 1. Applicability

### 1.1 Which lenders and which loans

- Para 3: applies to NBFC-D, NBFC-ICC, NBFC-Factor, NBFC-MFI, NBFC-IFC, IDF-NBFC and HFC "for all layers, unless specified otherwise".
- Para 4: for **NBFC-P2P**, only "Paragraph 98 to Paragraph 100" apply (recovery-agent responsibilities, harassment ban). A P2P platform is therefore bound by the harassment ban and the 8:00-19:00 rule but not by the KFS, penal-charge or FPC chapters.
- Para 5: **not applicable** to Mortgage Guarantee Companies, Core Investment Companies, NBFC-AA, Standalone Primary Dealers, NOFHC, and "NBFCs not having any customer interface".
- Para 9: the Fair Practices Code guidelines bind "NBFCs having customer interface".
- **Banks are not covered** (same gap as the outsourcing note, Q9 there). Bank partners fall under other instruments not read here.
- Para 29 (KFS): "all retail and MSME term loan products extended by all NBFCs"; "Credit card receivables are exempted".
- Para 30 (penal charges): "all credit facilities offered by the NBFC" except credit cards, ECBs, trade credits, structured obligations and foreign-currency loans.
- Section D (paras 31-32, floating-rate reset): EMI-based **personal loans**; para 32 extends to "all equated instalment-based loans of different periodicities, irrespective of whether they are linked to an external benchmark or an internal benchmark".
- Section F (paras 35-41, document release): "Personal Loans" as defined by para 6(11) (cross-reference to RBI XBRL harmonisation, not read).
- Sections G (gold and silver, paras 42-72) and H (microfinance, paras 76-97): product-specific, see 1.2.
- No layer split: unlike the outsourcing Directions, there is no Base / Middle / Upper carve-out except for pre-payment charges on business loans (para 34(2)).

## 3. Design consequences for Udofin

### 3.1 Calling hours and channels

1. Enforce **08:00-19:00** as a platform limit on every outbound recovery call (para 100), and **09:00-18:00** for any loan flagged microfinance (para 91(2)). The lender's `collections.policy` can only narrow the window, never widen it.
2. The text says "calling". It does not say whether SMS, WhatsApp, email or app notifications fall under the same hours. Conservative design: apply the window to every outbound recovery channel, except system-generated receipts and legally required notices (interpretation; Q4). The timezone is not stated; use Indian Standard Time.
3. "Persistently calling" has no number. Define a maximum attempts-per-day and per-week per account in the lender policy and refuse beyond it, and record the number chosen (Q4).
4. Contact is limited to the borrower. Paras 91(3) and 100 bar harassing or intruding on "family members, referees and friends"; para 18 bars interference. The platform does not store or call third-party numbers for pressure. Whether a neutral call to a referee to locate the borrower is ever acceptable is not stated and should be treated as banned (Q4).
5. No masked, anonymous or withheld caller identity (para 100). Calls use identifiable numbers on the notice.
6. Content rules: no threats, no abuse, no false or misleading statements about the amount or consequences (paras 91(6), 100), no public naming (91(4), 100), no social-media messaging.

### 3.2 Agent notice, identification, authorisation

7. Before the first contact: notice with agency and lender contact details and an authorisation letter (paras 95-96); the agent's ID card number shown; a change of agent triggers a new notice. `collections.assignments` needs: authorisation letter reference, ID-card reference, police-verification date, undertaking date, re-verification due date.
8. Notice delivery by email or SMS comes from the digital lending text (9(6)); the RBC text speaks of "provid[ing] the details of recovery agents to the borrower while initiating the process of recovery" and of the agent carrying documents. Both apply; our existing gate (no contact before delivery is recorded) satisfies the stricter of the two.

### 3.3 Recovery-agent engagement, training and verification

9. Police verification and re-verification per person (para 94), signed undertaking (99), training on the topics in para 98, and Udofin's own written code (99) must exist **before** the first lender goes live. Sub-agents (consent needed from the lender under outsourcing para 34(iv)) must evidence the same for their staff.
10. The text names **no certification**, no minimum training hours and no re-verification period (the lender decides, para 94). Do not cite this text for an IIBF requirement.

### 3.4 Grievance

11. Recovery-related complaints go to the lender's "dedicated mechanism" (para 92), whose details are given at disbursal. Udofin's complaint intake forwards them and pauses contact on request (already in `collections.complaints_links`). The nodal officer and generic-mailbox rule (Annex I Part 2 item 3) feed the KFS.

### 3.5 KFS, penal charges, fees

12. A charge cannot exist unless it is in the KFS or has explicit consent (29(5)). Collections must have **no** fee type of its own (consistent with digital lending 10(4)).
13. Reminders carry the applicable penal charge, and each levy produces a notice with its reason (30(7)). Add this to collections templates and to the `collections.notices` content hash.
14. The servicing read-model must model penal charges as a separate charge on the overdue amount only (30(1), Note (ii)) and keep interest on unpaid EMI at the contracted rate. No penal-on-penal.

### 3.6 Loan agreement documents and change of terms

15. Deliver the executed agreement and every quoted enclosure at sanction or disbursement (14, 27), in the borrower's language (10, 14). Change of terms: notice first, prospective effect only (15); the agreement template needs the prospective-only condition.

### 3.7 Conflict of interest (DLG plus recovery)

16. The text is silent on a provider that guarantees losses and also collects. The conduct paragraphs make the lender liable for its agent's behaviour; the segregation of duties in collections-and-dlg.md section 4.3 remains the right control. Para 30(4) Note (ii) (penal charges not "a revenue enhancement tool") argues against any commission measured on penal charges collected.

### 3.8 Rounding and APR (test vectors)

17. Para 103 requires the lender's transactions to be "rounded off to the nearest rupee". Udofin stores Money as integer paise, which is compatible, but every figure shown to the borrower (EMI, interest, totals, penal charges) must follow the lender's whole-rupee rounding: 50 paise and above rounds up. The KFS illustration itself shows the effect: the EMI of ₹969.73 is shown as ₹970 and the instalment total ₹23,280 differs from the ₹23,274 in the KFS by rounding (footnote, page 13).
18. APR illustration (page 12-13), usable as a Phase 07 test vector: ₹20,000, 24 monthly EPIs, 15% fixed, fees ₹400 (₹240 to the NBFC, ₹160 to a third party), net disbursed ₹19,600, total interest ₹3,274, total payable ₹23,274, APR **17.07%**. Our own recomputation (not in the text): monthly rate on 969.73 x 24 payments from a net outflow of 19,600 gives an IRR of 1.4225% per month, i.e. 17.07% when multiplied by twelve. So the text's "APR" is the **nominal annualised IRR** (monthly IRR x 12), not an effective annual rate (18.47%). With the rounded ₹970 instalment the same method gives 17.10%, so the APR must be computed from the unrounded instalment. The first instalment is taken one month after disbursal (the illustration says "30 days"). Counsel and the lender must confirm the method before the engine is built (Q9).

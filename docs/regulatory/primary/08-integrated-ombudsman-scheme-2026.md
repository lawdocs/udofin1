# Primary-source extract: Reserve Bank – Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026 (RB-IOS 2026)

- **Instrument:** Reserve Bank – Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026, made under s.35A of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949, s.45L of the RBI Act, 1934, s.18 of the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007 and s.11 of the Credit Information Companies (Regulation) Act, 2005.
- **Commencement:** 1 July 2026 (clause 1(2)). The RB-IOS 2021 stands repealed from 1 July 2026 (clause 20(1)). Today (13 Oct 2026) the 2026 Scheme is in force.
- **Reference:** the extracted PDF prints **no RBI circular number, issue date or signatory** (the text starts with the Scheme title). Not available from this file; do not cite a reference number from it.
- **Source file:** `docs/regulatory/sources/rbi-integrated-ombudsman-scheme-2026.pdf` (24 pages: clauses 1-20 pp 1-17; Annex pp 18-24).
- **Method:** Read in full through the PDF text layer (all 24 pages, including the Annex complaint form and authorisation). Page images were not separately inspected; there are no tables in the operative text, so no garbling risk was seen. Clause numbers are as printed. **Unreadable:** none. Footnote markers 1 to 4 (CRPC e-mail and address) extracted cleanly.
- **Status label for everything quoted here:** confirmed (primary text). Statements about what Udofin "must" or "may" do are our reading (interpretation) and need counsel review (section 9).

Important framing: the Scheme binds **Regulated Entities (REs)**, here the NBFC lender. It never mentions an LSP, an outsourced service provider, a digital lending app, a "recovery agent" (except as a category in the Annex form), an Internal Ombudsman or the Digital Lending Directions. It contains **no clause that addresses complaints handled by a service provider on the RE's behalf**. Everything about Udofin below is inferred from the definitions and from the lender's duties.

## 1. Which complaints and which lenders are in scope

### 1.1 Lenders covered (clause 1(3)(b))

The Scheme "shall apply to the services provided by" (b) "all Non-Banking Financial Companies (excluding Housing Finance Companies, Core Investment Company (CIC), Infrastructure Debt Fund-Non-Banking Financial Company (IDF-NBFC), Non-Banking Financial Company - Infrastructure Finance Company (NBFC-IFC), Non-Operative Financial Holding Company (NOFHC), Primary Dealers (PDs), Mortgage Guarantee Companies (MGC)) which (i) are authorised to accept deposits; or (ii) have customer interface, with an assets size of ₹100 crore and above as on the date of the audited balance sheet of the previous financial year."

- Clause 3(1)(j): "NBFC" excludes one "in resolution or winding up/liquidation, or under Directions of Reserve Bank of India".
- Clause 1(3)(a), (c), (d): banks (with a ₹50 crore deposit test for primary co-operative banks), non-bank PPI issuers and credit information companies are also covered; not relevant to our lenders except where a partner is a bank (clause 1(3)(a): all commercial banks and RRBs).
- Clause 10(2)(i) excludes "a grievance pertaining to customers of Regulated Entity not included under the Scheme", so a borrower of an out-of-scope NBFC (HFC, or non-deposit-taking NBFC with assets below ₹100 crore, or no customer interface) **has no RB-IOS route**.
- Clause 2: RBI may suspend the Scheme, generally or for a named RE.

**Consequence:** per lender, we need a flag "RB-IOS applies yes/no" (deposit-taking, or customer interface with assets of ₹100 crore or more at the last audited balance sheet; HFC always no). This resolves matrix open item 4 (HFC excluded; ₹100 crore threshold) from the primary text.

### 1.2 Definitions that decide scope (clause 3(1))

- (f) "Complaint": "a representation in writing, alleging a deficiency in service on the part of a Regulated Entity, submitted by its customer or his/her authorised representative, and seeking relief under the provisions of the Scheme".
- (h) "Customer": "a person who uses, or is an applicant for, a service provided by a Regulated Entity". An applicant who never receives a loan can complain.
- (i) "Deficiency in service": "a shortcoming or an inadequacy in any service, which the Regulated Entity is required to provide statutorily or otherwise, which may or may not result in financial loss or damage to the customer".
- (c) "Authorised Representative": "a person, other than an advocate, duly appointed and authorised in writing".
- (n) "Regulated Entity": a bank, NBFC, non-bank PPI issuer or CIC, "or any other entity as may be specified by the Reserve Bank from time to time".

### 1.3 Grounds (clause 9) and what may be complained about

Clause 9: "Any customer aggrieved by an act or omission of a Regulated Entity resulting in deficiency in service may file a complaint under the Scheme personally or through an authorised representative". Clause 8(1): the Ombudsman "shall consider the complaints of customers of Regulated Entities relating to deficiency in service"; clause 8(2): in doing so takes into account "principles of banking law and practice" and RBI "directions, instructions, guidelines or regulations". **No list of grounds is in the operative text.** The optional Annex form (Part B, item (II)(g)) lists categories, among them "Loans and advances (including complaints against recovery Agents/ Direct Sales Agents, harassment, non-observance of Fair Practices Code. Etc.)", "Mis-selling", "Internet/Mobile/Electronic Banking", and "Issue related to KYC updation (including freezing of accounts)". The Annex says the response to that question "is optional for the Complainant". Category list is not a limit on grounds.

Reading: an act or omission of Udofin on the lender's behalf (app failure, wrong KFS display, collections conduct by Udofin staff or agents, KYC-updation reminders mishandled) is an act "of" the lender for RB-IOS purposes because the lender is the RE and remains responsible (see outsourcing para 17). The Scheme does not say so itself (Q2).

### 1.4 Exclusions (clause 10(2))

Excluded: (a) "matters related to commercial judgment or decision of a Regulated Entity" (so credit approval or rejection, pricing, limit-setting are the lender's commercial judgment); (b) "a dispute between a vendor and a Regulated Entity" (so a dispute between Udofin and a lender is out); (c) grievances against management or executives; (d) action in compliance with orders of a judicial, quasi-judicial, statutory or law-enforcing authority; (e) "a service not within the regulatory purview of the Reserve Bank"; (f) dispute between REs; (g) employer-employee disputes; (h) a grievance for which s.18 of the CIC (Regulation) Act gives a remedy; (i) customers of an RE not included under the Scheme.

Clause 10(3): complaints failing clause 10(1) or within 10(2) "shall be rejected at the outset as non-maintainable without further examination". Clause 16(2)(a), (e) also lets the Ombudsman reject where there is "no deficiency in service" or the matter "requires consideration of elaborate documentary and oral evidence".

Note: clause 10(2)(a) is the textual basis for our rule that credit decisions are the lender's "commercial judgment" (matrix GRV-04's "Udofin cannot finally reject matters the lender owns" is a Udofin design point; the Scheme does not say it).

## 2. Maintainability: the 30-day wait, deadlines and conditions (clause 10(1))

A complaint is maintainable "only if the following conditions are satisfied":

| Clause | Condition (quoted) |
| ------ | ------------------ |
| 10(1)(a) | "the complaint is addressed to the RBI Ombudsman directly. However, it does not include a communication in which the Reserve Bank is merely endorsed/marked in copy". |
| 10(1)(b) | lodged "by the Complainant personally or through an authorised representative other than an advocate unless the advocate is the aggrieved person". |
| 10(1)(c) | the Complainant "provides complete information as specified in clause 11". |
| 10(1)(d) | "not abusive or frivolous or vexatious in nature". |
| **10(1)(e)** | "the Complainant had first made a complaint in writing or through any other mode to the Regulated Entity concerned, where proof of having made a complaint can be produced by the Complainant, before making a complaint under the Scheme". |
| **10(1)(f)** | "the Complainant has not received any reply within 30 days or within the time specified by the Reserve Bank, National Payments Corporation of India, or under Card Network guidelines, if any, whichever is higher after the Regulated Entity received the complaint; or the Complainant is not satisfied with the reply / resolution provided by the Regulated Entity". |
| **10(1)(g)** | "the complaint is made to the RBI Ombudsman within 90 days from the date on which the timeline specified in sub-clause (1)(f) expires or the date of the last communication from the concerned Regulated Entity, whichever is later". |
| 10(1)(h)-(i) | not the same grievance already pending before, or settled or dealt with on merits by, the Office of the RBI Ombudsman. |
| 10(1)(j)-(k) | not the same grievance "already pending before any Court, Tribunal or Arbitrator or any other judicial or quasi-judicial forum", nor "already settled or dealt with on merits" by one. Explanation 1: criminal proceedings and police investigation do not count as the "same grievance". |
| 10(1)(l) | the complaint to the RE "was made before the expiry of the period of limitation prescribed under the Limitation Act, 1963, for such claims". |

**The wait, read plainly:** the borrower must complain to the lender first, with provable evidence (e). The borrower may go to the Ombudsman only when (f) either no reply arrives within 30 days (or any longer RBI/NPCI/card-network period), **or** the borrower is "not satisfied" with the reply earlier. So a lender's early reply that does not satisfy the borrower opens the door before day 30; the 30-day period is the point after which silence opens it. The Annex (Part A, items 1-2) repeats this in plainer words: "A Complainant must necessarily file his/her complaint FIRST with the Regulated Entity", and "no action will be taken" on a complaint filed directly. Time to file: 90 days from the later of the (f) deadline or the lender's last communication.

Our reading: **where a complaint was first received by Udofin (app or support), clause 10(1)(f) runs "after the Regulated Entity received the complaint"**. The Scheme does not say that a complaint to an LSP counts as receipt by the RE. The matrix row GRV-02 says "a complaint received by Udofin starts the lender's clock"; that is a safe design rule (route to the lender at once and time-stamp receipt), but it is not stated in this text (Q1). The safest posture is to treat Udofin's receipt as the lender's receipt for clock purposes and forward within hours.

Clause 10(1) mixes "and" and "or" at the end of (g), (i), (j), (k); counsel should read the list as printed (Q6). Clause 16(1)(c): a complaint is rejected "at any stage" if a case on the same cause of action is filed before a court, tribunal, arbitrator or other forum while the complaint is under examination.

These artefacts are lender-owned data; Udofin may hold them as the lender's processor with the minimum fields.

## 4. Lender (RE) response, award, compensation and appeal

### 4.1 What the Ombudsman can do and how much

- Clause 8(3): "There is no limit on the amount in a dispute"; for "consequential loss suffered by the Complainant" compensation "up to ₹30 lakh"; "In addition ... up to ₹3 lakh for the loss of the Complainant's time, expenses incurred, harassment/mental anguish suffered, etc." (The Annex repeats both figures.)
- Clause 8(4)-(5): the Ombudsman may examine and close all complaints; the Deputy Ombudsman may close those under clause 10 and those resolved under clause 14(8)(a)-(c).
- Clause 8(6): the Ombudsman may make "any Regulated Entity, other than the one against whom the complaint has been lodged, a party to the complaint if ... such Regulated Entity has, by an act, negligence, or omission, failed to comply with any directions, instructions, guidelines, or regulations issued by the Reserve Bank." A second lender, or a lender whose LSP erred, can be joined.
- Clause 8(7): RBI publishes an annual report. Clause 13(3): the Office keeps information confidential, except as required by law, with consent, for natural justice, or to RBI or a court.
- Clause 14(1): proceedings "shall be summary in nature and shall not be bound by any rules of evidence".

### 4.2 Timelines for the lender

| Step | Clause | Time |
| ---- | ------ | ---- |
| Reply to the borrower before the Scheme is available | 10(1)(f) | 30 days, or any longer period set by RBI, NPCI or card-network guidelines (whichever is higher) |
| Written response to the Ombudsman, with copies of documents relied on | **14(2)** | **"within 15 days"** of receipt of the complaint (the complaint copy is forwarded under 13(1)); the Ombudsman may grant more time "at the request of the Regulated Entity in writing" |
| If no response | 14(3) | Ombudsman "may proceed ex-parte, based on the documents / material available on record, and issue an Award" |
| Failure to furnish requisitioned information or certified copies | 13(2) proviso | "deemed that the Regulated Entity has no information to furnish or no submissions to make" |
| Comply with the advisory | 14(4), (6) | Within the time the Ombudsman stipulates; if complied with and the complainant accepts, closed under 14(8)(b) |
| Complainant's letter of acceptance of an Award | 15(4) | 30 days from receipt of the Award, else it lapses; no acceptance needed if the complainant has appealed under 17(3) |
| RE complies with Award, or appeals | 15(5) | "within 30 days from the date of receipt of the letter of acceptance from the Complainant" |
| RE appeal to the Appellate Authority | 17(2) | within 30 days from receipt of the Complainant's letter of acceptance; "only with the previous sanction of the Executive Director/Official of equal rank" (17(2)(a)); further period not exceeding 30 days if sufficient cause (17(2)(b)) |
| Complainant appeal | 17(3) | within 30 days of receiving the Award, plus up to 30 days for sufficient cause |

### 4.3 Awards and appeals

- Clause 15(1): the Ombudsman passes an Award on (a) non-furnishing of documents or information (the clause 14(3) ex parte case) or (b) the matter not resolved under 14(8), after "a reasonable opportunity of being heard (oral or in writing)" to both parties.
- Clause 15(2): the Award may direct "specific performance of its obligations" and compensation per clause 8(3). Clause 15(3): copy sent to both.
- **Clause 17(1): "There shall not be any right of appeal to a Regulated Entity for an Award issued under clause 15(1)(a)"** (the ex parte or non-response case). So a missed 15-day reply can produce an Award the lender cannot appeal.
- Clause 17(2): the RE may appeal an Award under 15(1)(b). Clause 17(5): the Appellate Authority (Executive Director in charge of the Consumer Education and Protection Department, clause 3(1)(a)) may dismiss, set aside, remand, modify or pass another order after a hearing. Clause 17(6): its order has the same effect as the Award or rejection.
- Clause 14(6)-(8): the Ombudsman may issue an advisory "at any stage"; clause 14(7): conciliation meeting "in the presence of the RBI Ombudsman, and the parties in person or online"; clause 14(8)(a)-(e): closure grounds.
- Clause 16: rejection grounds at any stage (non-maintainability, not a valid complaint, parallel court case, no deficiency, compensation sought beyond the Ombudsman's power, lack of diligence, without sufficient cause, needs elaborate evidence, no financial loss or inconvenience).

For a digital-only lender, how "places where the business is transacted" extends to the lender's app (and ours) is an interpretation (Q3).

## 6. Outsourced and LSP-handled complaints, and the interplay with internal grievance

**What the text says:** nothing specific. The Scheme has no clause about outsourced services, service providers, or complaints against an agent. The nearest provisions:

- The RE is the only respondent; "Customer" and "Deficiency in service" are defined by reference to services "provided by" the RE (3(1)(h), (i)).
- Clause 8(6): other REs can be joined for their own non-compliance with RBI directions.
- Clause 10(2)(b): a dispute between a vendor and an RE is excluded (that is a dispute between the two, not a customer complaint).
- Clause 10(2)(e): "a service not within the regulatory purview of the Reserve Bank" is excluded, so a service that Udofin sells on its own account (for example a group product that is not the lender's service) may fall outside RB-IOS. The Scheme does not draw this line.
- Annex Part B (II)(g): the loans category covers "recovery Agents/ Direct Sales Agents, harassment, non-observance of Fair Practices Code".

**Interplay with internal grievance (our reading):** the sequence the text fixes is (1) complaint to the RE in any mode with proof; (2) RE reply; (3) after 30 days of no reply (or sooner if the borrower is dissatisfied) and within 90 days of the later of the 30-day expiry or the RE's last communication, complaint to the Ombudsman at CMS. The RE's **internal** grievance machinery (nodal officers, the lender's grievance officer, any Internal Ombudsman) is not defined here; this Scheme only requires that the borrower has complained first. A lender's internal escalation levels must therefore finish inside the 30 days if the lender wants to keep the first chance at resolving.

**What Udofin should do (our reading):** treat Udofin's grievance desk as a front door for the lender's complaint process: log, time-stamp, forward the complaint to the lender's nodal officer without delay, issue the lender's reference number, and never close or finally reject a complaint on the lender's behalf; keep the lender's reply and any CMS reference the borrower provides; supply documents inside the lender's 15-day window (4.2). Udofin's conduct is judged as the lender's.

## 7. Transitional and administrative clauses

- Clause 1(3)(b)(ii) and 10(2)(i): scope and exclusion by entity category.
- Clause 19: RBI may remove difficulties by provisions not inconsistent with the Acts or the Scheme.
- Clause 20(2): complaints received **before 1 July 2026**, appeals from them, and execution of Awards under the 2021 Scheme remain governed by the 2021 Scheme and RBI instructions issued under it. A lender-facing document that still cites RB-IOS 2021 for new complaints is out of date.
- Clauses 4, 5, 7: appointment and tenure of Ombudsman (generally three years), offices, staffing (RBI bears cost).
- Annex Part B authorisation form: written authorisation of a representative, which may be submitted later with the complaint number.

## 10. Obligations that matter to Udofin, ranked (see also the KYC note)

1. **Route every borrower complaint to the lender at once with a time-stamped, lender-numbered record**; the borrower must have complained to the RE first (10(1)(e)) and attaches the complaint, its number and the reply (Annex).
2. **Never finally reject, close or answer a complaint for the lender**; the lender replies inside 30 days (10(1)(f)), and decides.
3. **Support the lender's 30-day reply window and 90-day borrower window**: show the lender's reply date and the borrower's filing deadline (the later of the 30-day expiry or the lender's last communication plus 90 days, 10(1)(f)-(g)).
4. **Track the lender's 15-day written-response deadline to the Ombudsman** (14(2)) and be able to deliver documents inside it. A missed deadline can produce an ex parte Award with no right of appeal for the lender (14(3), 17(1)); the lender's 30-day compliance or appeal clock follows acceptance (15(5), 17(2)).
5. **Mirror the lender's clause 18 disclosures**: Principal Nodal Officer (rank not below General Manager) name and contact details, CRPC address and the CMS link (https://cms.rbi.org.in), salient features and a copy of the Scheme, in English, Hindi and the regional language where the lender requires it (18(2)-(6)).
6. **Keep a per-lender "RB-IOS applies" flag** (clause 1(3)(b)): HFCs and small non-deposit-taking NBFCs have no Ombudsman route; the grievance page must not promise one.
7. **Do not obstruct or displace the Ombudsman route**: no arbitration or ADR step that makes the same grievance "pending before ... Arbitrator" and blocks the borrower (10(1)(j)-(k)), no language that discourages complaints, and no cost or fee for the borrower.
8. **Expect Udofin's conduct to be treated as the lender's** (outsourcing para 17; Annex categories on DSA/recovery agent harassment, mis-selling, KYC updation); keep audit logs for collections contact, KFS display and KYC-updation reminders that the lender can produce to the Ombudsman.
9. **Compensation exposure up to ₹30 lakh (consequential loss) plus ₹3 lakh (time, expenses, harassment)** per complaint (8(3)) falls on the lender, and the lender will pass it down by contract; check indemnity and insurance sizing before accepting it.
10. **Never use a lender's complaint data outside that lender's scope**: minimal fields (reference number, dates, category, outcome), no names, phone numbers, PAN or Aadhaar in logs, URLs, events or push payloads (CLAUDE.md rule 3); the Annex form itself carries personal data under the borrower's consent to RBI, not to Udofin.

## 11. Points the text does not address (so no statement in our docs should cite this instrument for them)

- Any rule for an LSP, DLA, service provider or outsourced complaint handling.
- Internal Ombudsman, nodal-officer duties inside the lender's own grievance machinery, or grievance-officer rules under the Digital Lending Directions.
- The contents of the lender's final reply.
- The effect of an arbitration clause, mediation or Lok Adalat on maintainability beyond clauses 10(1)(j)-(k) and 16(1)(c).
- Fee or cost rules (the Scheme says only that it aims to be "cost-effective"; clause 7 says RBI bears the cost of the offices).
- DPDP Act handling of the complaint data held by Udofin.
